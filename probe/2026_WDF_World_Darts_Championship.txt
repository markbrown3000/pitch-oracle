The '''2026 WDF World Darts Championship''' is an upcoming [[darts]] tournament that will be held from 27 November to 6 December 2026 at the [[Lakeside Leisure Complex|Lakeside Country Club]] in [[Frimley Green]], Surrey, England. It is the fifth [[WDF World Darts Championship|World Darts Championship]] to be organised by the [[World Darts Federation]] since it succeeded the now-defunct [[British Darts Organisation]].<ref>{{cite web |date=15 December 2021 |title=WDF World Darts Championship postponed: 2022 Lakeside tournament pushed back to April |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/12496541/wdf-world-darts-championship-postponed-2022-lakeside-tournament-pushed-back-to-april |website=Sky Sports |access-date=10 August 2026}}</ref> The open tournament, women's tournament, youth tournament, and girls' tournament will share a total prize fund of £223,500, with the open champion receiving the biggest winner's prize of £50,000.

In the senior competitions, [[Jimmy van Schie]] is the reigning open champion after beating [[Mitchell Lawrie]] 6–3 in the [[2025 WDF World Darts Championship#Open|2025 open final]]; however, Van Schie is unable to defend his title after winning a [[PDC Tour Card]] in January 2026.<ref name="Lawrie25">{{cite web |date=7 December 2025 |title=Mitchell Lawrie, 15, described as 'future of darts' after 6-3 loss to Jimmy van Schie in WDF World Darts Championship final |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/13480680/mitchell-lawrie-15-described-as-future-of-darts-after-6-3-loss-to-jimmy-van-schie-in-wdf-world-darts-championship-final |website=Sky Sports |access-date=8 November 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=11 January 2026 |title=Manby & De Zwaan secure automatic Tour Cards as 2026 Q Schools conclude |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/january/11/manby-de-zwaan-secure-automatic-tour-cards-2026-q-schools-conclude |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=8 November 2026}}</ref> [[Deta Hedman]] is the defending women's champion, having defeated Lerena Rietbergen 4–1 in the 2025 women's final.<ref>{{cite web |last=Adams |first=Lewis |date=10 December 2025 |title=Darts champion 'blubbered like a child' after win |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/clyzx9l3k2jo |website=BBC |access-date=8 November 2026}}</ref> In the youth competitions, Lawrie is the defending world youth champion, having beaten Florian Preis 4–2 in the 2025 final while [[Zehra Gemi]] is the defending girls' world champion, having won the 2025 final 3–0 against Rebecca Allen.<ref name="Lawrie25"/><ref>{{cite web |date=7 December 2025 |title=Glory for Gemi in WDF Girls' World Championship |url=https://dartswdf.com/news/glory-for-gemi-in-wdf-girls-world-championship |website=World Darts Federation |access-date=8 November 2026}}</ref>

== Prize money ==
A total of £223,500 will be on offer across the four events. The prize fund for the open and women's event remained the same as 2024 and 2025. The winner of the open event receives the highest prize of £50,000.<ref name="2026WDFPrizeMoney">{{cite web |date=11 October 2026 |title=£223,500 prize fund on offer at 2026 WDF World Championships |url=https://dartswdf.com/news/223-500-prize-fund-on-offer-at-2026-wdf-world-championships |website=World Darts Federation |access-date=11 October 2026}}</ref>
