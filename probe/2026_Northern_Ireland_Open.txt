The '''2026 Northern Ireland Open''' (officially the '''2026 BetVictor Northern Ireland Open''') was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 18 to 25 October 2026 at the [[Waterfront Hall]] in [[Belfast]], Northern Ireland. Qualifying took place from 13 to 16 September at the [[Leicester Arena]] in [[Leicester]], England. The 11th consecutive edition of the tournament since it was first staged in [[2016 Northern Ireland Open|2016]], it was the seventh [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2026–27 snooker season]], following the [[2026 Shenzhen Open]] and preceding the [[2026 International Championship]]. It was the second of four tournaments in the season's [[Home Nations Series]], following the [[2026 English Open (snooker)|2026 English Open]] and preceding the [[2026 Scottish Open (snooker)|2026 Scottish Open]] and the [[2027 Welsh Open (snooker)|2027 Welsh Open]].

[[Jack Lisowski]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Judd Trump]] 9–8 in the [[2025 Northern Ireland Open|2025 final]], but he was beaten by [[Anthony McGill]] in the last 32.

[[Kyren Wilson]] won the tournament beating [[Jak Jones]] 9–2 in the final.

The [[Northern Ireland Open (snooker)|Northern Ireland Open]] was first staged in [[2016 Northern Ireland Open|2016]] at the [[Titanic Exhibition Centre]] in [[Belfast]], Northern Ireland.<ref>{{cite web |last=Petrie |first=Richard |date=17 October 2024 |title=Northern Ireland Open: All you need to know about the 2024 tournament in Belfast |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c20mjpppn3go |access-date=13 October 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-date=4 November 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251104175758/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c20mjpppn3go |url-status=live}}</ref> The inaugural winner was [[Mark King (snooker player)|Mark King]], who defeated [[Barry Hawkins]] 9–8 in the final to win his maiden ranking title.<ref name="BBC_Mark_King">{{cite news |date=20 November 2016 |title=Northern Ireland Open: Mark King ends 25-year wait for ranking title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/38045803 |access-date=11 September 2025 |work=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-date=21 November 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161121074103/http://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/38045803 |url-status=live}}</ref> The tournament winner received the [[Alex Higgins]] Trophy, named in honour of the two-time World Champion, who died in 2010.<ref>{{cite news |date=27 September 2016 |title=Alex Higgins: NI trophy named after two-time world champion |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/37487430 |access-date=11 September 2025 |work=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260422074902/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/37487430 |archive-date=22 April 2026 |url-status=live}}</ref> At the inaugural edition of the tournament, the trophy was presented by Higgins's daughter Lauren.<ref name="BBC_Mark_King" />

=== Format ===
The tournament used a tiered format first implemented for the [[Home Nations Series]] in the [[2024–25 snooker season]]. In round one, players seeded 65–96 faced those [[seeding (sports)|seeded]] 97 and below, including selected amateurs. In round two, the winners from round one faced players seeded 33–64. The winners from round two faced the top 32 seeds.<ref>{{cite web |date=5 April 2024 |title=Tiered format for Home Nations and German Masters in 2024/25 |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/april/05/tiered-format-for-home-nations-and-german-masters-in-2024-25/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405185554/https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/april/05/tiered-format-for-home-nations-and-german-masters-in-2024-25/ |archive-date=5 April 2024 |access-date=5 April 2024 |work=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref> All matches were played as the best of 7 {{cuegloss|frame|frames}} until the quarter{{nbh}}finals, which were the best of 11. The semi{{nbh}}finals were the best of 11, and the final was a best{{nbh}}of{{nbh}}17-frame match played over two {{cuegloss|session|sessions}}.<ref name="s.o_M">{{cite web |title=BetVictor Northern Ireland Open |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2548 |work=snooker.org |date=25 October 2026 |access-date=25 October 2026 |archive-date=25 October 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20261025202746/https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2548 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Home nations quarter–finals change to best of 11 |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/june/26/home-nations-quarter-finals-change-to-best-of-11/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=26 June 2026 |access-date=21 October 2026}}</ref>

=== Quarter-finals ===
[[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] recovered from 2–4 behind to beat [[David Gilbert (snooker player)|David Gilbert]] 6–4, making three [[century break]]s of 104, 115 and 101 in the last four frames.<ref>{{cite web |title=Vintage Williams beats Gilbert with ton trio |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/october/23/Vintage-Williams-Beats-Gilbert-With-Ton-Trio/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=23 October 2026 |access-date=24 October 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=Wilson and Williams through to NI Open semi-finals |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cqy0r7wj5e9ro |work=[[BBC Sport]] |first=Richard |last=Petrie |date=23 October 2026 |access-date=24 October 2026}}</ref>

=== Semi-finals ===
[[Kyren Wilson]] recovered from 3–5 behind to defeat [[Sam Craigie]] 6–5.<ref>{{cite web |title=Wilson beats Craigie in Belfast drama |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/october/24/Wilson-Beats-Craigie-In-Belfast-Drama/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=24 October 2026 |access-date=24 October 2026}}</ref>

[[Jak Jones]] recovered from 1–4 behind to defeat Williams 6–5.<ref>{{cite web |title=Jones stuns Williams to reach final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/october/24/Jones-Stuns-Williams-To-Reach-Final/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=24 October 2026 |access-date=25 October 2026}}</ref>

=== Final ===
Wilson faced Jones in a repeat of the [[2024 World Snooker Championship|2024 World Championship final]] when Wilson won 18–14.<ref>{{cite news |title=Wilson to face Jones in NI Open final |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cxyvrgrmv75zo |work=[[BBC Sport]] |first=Jana |last=McCabe |date=24 October 2026 |access-date=25 October 2026}}</ref> At the end of the afternoon session Wilson led Jones 6–2.<ref>{{cite web |title=Wilson takes control of Waterfront final with century blitz |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/october/25/Wilson-Takes-Control-Of-Waterfront-Final-With-Century-Blitz/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=25 October 2026 |access-date=25 October 2026}}</ref> Wilson went on to win the match, and the tournament 9–2.<ref>{{cite web |title=Wilson reigns supreme in Belfast |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/october/25/Wilson-Reigns-Supreme-In-Belfast/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=25 October 2026 |access-date=25 October 2026}}</ref><ref name="BBC_finalResult">{{cite news |title=Wilson beats Jones to secure NI Open title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cqwyrgg0nylwo |work=[[BBC Sport]] |first=Jana |last=McCabe |date=25 October 2026 |access-date=26 October 2026}}</ref>
