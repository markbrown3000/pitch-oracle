The '''2026 China Open''' (officially the '''2026 Satuo China Open''') was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 8 to 16 August 2026 at the [[Taiyuan Riverside Sports Centre Gymnasium|Riverside Sports Centre]] in [[Taiyuan]], China. Qualifying took place from 10 to 14 June at the [[Leicester Arena]] in [[Leicester]], England. The first edition of the revived [[China Open (snooker)|China Open]] after it was cancelled because of the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], it was the second [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2026–27 snooker season]], following the [[2026 Championship League (ranking)|2026 Championship League]] and preceding the [[2026 Wuhan Open (snooker)|2026 Wuhan Open]]. The winner received £250,000 from a total prize fund of £1,205,000.

[[Neil Robertson]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Jack Lisowski]] 11–4 in the [[2019 China Open (snooker)|2019 final]], but he was beaten 1–6 by [[Mark Selby]] in the quarter-finals.

Selby won the tournament, defeating [[Noppon Saengkham]] 10–6 in the final.

== Overview ==
The first non-ranking China Masters was held in 1985. [[Steve Davis]] won the [[1985 China Masters|inaugural edition]], defeating [[Dennis Taylor]] 2–1 in the final.<ref>{{cite news |title=Steve's king of the Orient |url=https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/viewer/bl/0000445/19850910/342/0015 |url-access=subscription |work=Aberdeen Evening Express |date=10 September 1985 |page=15}}</ref> The first major international tournament played in the Asian country was the [[China Open (snooker)|China International]], held as a non-ranking event in [[1997 China International|1997]] and as a [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] in [[1999 China International|March 1999]]. It was renamed the China Open for the [[1999 China Open (snooker)|December 1999 edition]], which [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] won by beating [[Stephen Lee (snooker player)|Stephen Lee]] 9–2 in the final.<ref>{{cite web |last=Turner |first=Chris |title=China International, China Open, Shanghai Masters, Jiangsu/Wuxi Classic |url=http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/China.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120216155144/http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/China.html |archive-date=16 February 2012 |work=cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk |publisher=Chris Turner's Snooker Archive |access-date=30 April 2026 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="WST_China_Open_Returns">{{cite web |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/april/19/china-open-returns-to-world-snooker-tour-in-taiyuan-in-2026/ |title=China Open returns to World Snooker Tour in Tayuan in 2026 |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=19 April 2026 |access-date=30 April 2026 |language=en}}</ref> It was staged 18 times as a ranking event until it was discontinued due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. As of the [[2019 China Open (snooker)|2019 edition]], the last one to be played before its discontinuation, [[Mark Selby]] and [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] were the most successful players, each having won the tournament three times.<ref name="WST_China_Open_Returns" />

The 2026 edition of the tournament was held from 8 to 16 August at the [[Taiyuan Riverside Sports Centre Gymnasium|Riverside Sports Centre]] in [[Taiyuan]], China.<ref>{{cite web |date=10 July 2026 |title=2026 China Open officially launched in Taiyuan |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/july/10/2026-china-open-officially-launched-in-taiyuan/ |access-date=10 July 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=22 April 2026 |title=Official announcement! Based in Taiyuan, the 2026 World Snooker Tour China Open makes a grand return in August |url=https://weibo.com/ttarticle/p/show?id=2309405290594691055646 |work=[[Weibo]] |language=zh |access-date=2 May 2026}}</ref><ref name="WST_China_Open_Returns" /> Qualifying took place from 10 to 14 June at the [[Leicester Arena]] in [[Leicester]], England.<ref name="s.o_Quali">{{cite web |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2759 |title=China Open Qualifiers 2026 |website=snooker.org |access-date=14 May 2026 |language=en}}</ref> It was the second ranking event of the [[2026–27 snooker season]], following the [[2026 Championship League (ranking)|2026 Championship League]] and preceding the [[2026 Wuhan Open (snooker)|2026 Wuhan Open]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?season=2026&template=2&tour=all |title=Calendar 2026/2027 |website=snooker.org |access-date=29 April 2026|language=en}}</ref> [[Neil Robertson]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Jack Lisowski]] 11–4 in the 2019 final,<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.skysports.com/more-sports/snooker/news/12243/11687645/neil-robertson-warms-up-for-world-snooker-championship-with-china-open-success |title=Neil Robertson warms up for World Snooker Championship with China Open success |work=[[Sky Sports]] |date=8 April 2019 |access-date=30 April 2026 |language=en}}</ref> but he was beaten 1–6 by [[Mark Selby]] in the quarter-finals.<ref name="WST_day07" />

The opening ceremony was held in [[Taiyuan]] on 7 August. At the ceremony the chairman of the [[World Professional Billiards and Snooker Association]], [[Jason Ferguson (snooker player)|Jason Ferguson]] said: "Today marks a significant moment for our sport as we celebrate the return of one of snooker's most prestigious and historic tournaments. After a six-year absence, the China Open once again takes its rightful place on the World Snooker Tour calendar, reaffirming its status as the flagship snooker event in China and one of the highlights of our international season."<ref>{{cite web |title=Taiyuan welcomes snooker greats |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/07/taiyuan-welcomes-snooker-greats/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=7 August 2026 |access-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> China Open promoter Jay Wang noted the tournament's successful return.<ref>{{cite web |title=Taiyuan proves perfect host city for China Open return |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/16/Taiyuan-Proves-Perfect-Host-City-For-China-Open-Return-/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=16 August 2026 |access-date=16 August 2026}}</ref>

There were 120 [[century break]]s made during the event, with 80 made during the qualification stage,<ref name="si_cent_Q" /> and 40 at the main stage,<ref name="si_cent_M" /> including a [[maximum break]] made by [[Michael Holt (snooker player)|Michael Holt]] during qualifying.<ref name="Max 242" />

=== Format ===
The revived edition followed a structure similar to that of the [[UK Championship]] and the [[World Snooker Championship|World Championship]].<ref name="WST_China_formats">{{cite web |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/june/03/china-formats-this-season-explained/ |title=China formats this season explained |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=3 June 2026 |access-date=15 June 2026}}</ref> 144 players were entered into the tournament, including Chinese wildcards as well as top-up amateurs from the Q School Order of Merit. The top 16 ranked players were [[Seeding (sport)|seeded]] through to the final rounds in [[Taiyuan]], while all other players needed to play between 2 and 4 rounds of qualifying in [[Leicester]] in order to qualify for the main stage, depending on their seeding.<ref name="WST_China_formats" />

All matches up to and including the semi-finals were the best of 11 {{cuegloss|frame|frames}}, while the final was a best-of-19 frames match played over two {{cuegloss|session|sessions}}.<ref name="s.o_Main" />

With 607 unanswered points, this breaks the record for a professional match, beating the previous record of 556, set by [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] at the [[2014 Masters (snooker)|2014 Masters]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Magnificent Murphy breaks unanswered points record with 607 |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/09/Magnificent-Murphy-Breaks-Unanswered-Points-Record-With-607/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=9 August 2026 |access-date=9 August 2026}}</ref> O'Sullivan beat [[Jackson Page]] 6–2.<ref name="WST_day02" /><ref>{{cite news |title=Trump loses, Murphy breaks record at China Open |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cly81lwjvd4o |work=[[BBC Sport]] |first=Alex |last=Brotherton |date=9 August 2026 |access-date=9 August 2026}}</ref>

==== Last 16 ====
The last 16 matches were played on 12 and 13 August.<ref name="s.o_Main" />

Gilbert beat Wu 6–2, and Zhou defeated Liu 6–4 making four [[century break]]s of 119, 108, 134, and 140.<ref name="WST_day05">{{cite web |title=China Open day five |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/12/china-open-day-five/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=12 August 2026 |access-date=12 August 2026}}</ref> Saengkham beat O'Sullivan 6–4, and Murphy defeated Xiao 6–2.<ref name="WST_day05" />

Bingham whitewashed Vafaei, and defending champion Robertson beat Si 6–2.<ref name="WST_day06">{{cite web |title=China Open day six |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/13/china-open-day-six/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=13 August 2026 |access-date=13 August 2026}}</ref> Selby beat Wilson 6–1, and Zhang defeated Wakelin 6–4.<ref name="WST_day06" />

==== Quarter-finals ====
The quarter-finals were played on 14 August.<ref name="s.o_Main" />

Saengkham beat Murphy 6–4, and Zhou defeated Gilbert 6–5.<ref>{{cite web |title=Giant-killer Saengkham stuns Murphy |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/14/Giant-Killer-Saengkham-Stuns-Murphy/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=14 August 2026 |access-date=14 August 2026}}</ref> Selby beat defending champion Robertson 6–1, and Bingham defeated Zhang 6–3.<ref name="WST_day07">{{cite web |title='Faultless' Selby thrashes Robertson to reach semis |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/14/-Faultless--Selby-Thrashes-Robertson-To-reach-Semis/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=14 August 2026 |access-date=14 August 2026}}</ref>

==== Semi-finals ====
[[File:Mark Selby at Snooker German Masters (DerHexer) 2015-02-08 38.jpg|left|thumb|upright=0.85|[[Mark Selby]] (''pictured in 2015'') beat [[Stuart Bingham]] 6–2 in the second semi-final.]]
[[File:Noppon Saengkham PHC 2015-2.jpg|thumb|upright=0.75|[[Noppon Saengkham]] (''pictured in 2015'') beat [[Zhou Yuelong]] 6–5 in the first semi-final.]]
The semi-finals were played on 15 August.<ref name="s.o_Main" />

Saengkham beat Zhou 6–5, making a 134 {{cuegloss|total clearance}} in the {{cuegloss|deciding frame}} to reach his second ranking final, having lost to [[Gary Wilson (snooker player)|Gary Wilson]] in the final of the [[2023 Scottish Open (snooker)|2023 Scottish Open]].<ref name="WST_day08_1" /> After the match Saengkham said: "Last night I couldn't sleep, I went to sleep at 6am and woke up at 10am. I was thinking about everything, at 5–5 I still believed I could do it, I just wanted one chance. From 5–3 I didn't make many mistakes. I told myself that if I had one chance I had to go for it. You need a big heart if you want to win big."<ref name="WST_day08_1">{{cite web |title=The Noppon effect! Saengkham edges Zhou to reach final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/15/The-Noppon-Effect--Saengkham-Edges-Zhou-To-Reach-Final/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=15 August 2026 |access-date=15 August 2026}}</ref>

Selby beat Bingham 6–2 to reach his 39th ranking final.<ref name="WST_day08_2" /> After the match Selby said: "I played OK tonight, not as good as yesterday but I didn't do a lot wrong, Stuart {{bracket|Bingham}} looked good up until 2–2, then after that the bits of run didn't go his way. I seem to be punishing my opponent's mistakes and you need to do that to compete for tournaments. I have hit the ball well and felt good on the practice table all week, and trusted myself out there. Noppon {{bracket|Saengkham}} is in the final for a reason, he's playing very well and he's beaten Judd {{bracket|Trump}}, Ronnie {{bracket|O'Sullivan}}, Shaun Murphy and Zhou Yuelong. It will be tough. I played him the [[World Snooker Championship|World Championship]] in [[2020 World Snooker Championship|2020]] and beat him 13–12, he played unbelievably well over three sessions so I am expecting something similar to that. I'll give it my all as I do in every match. It's great to be back in a final here in China."<ref name="WST_day08_2">{{cite web |title=Selby on target for fourth China Open crown |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/15/Selby-On-Target-For-Fourth-China-Open-Crown/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=15 August 2026 |access-date=15 August 2026}}</ref>

==== Final ====
The final was played on 16 August as the best of 19 {{cuegloss|frame|frames}}, played over two {{cuegloss|session|sessions}}.<ref name="s.o_Main" />

At the end of the afternoon session the score was tied at 4–4.<ref>{{cite web |title=Selby and Saengkham tied at 4–4 |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/16/Selby-And-Saengkham-Tied-At-4-4/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=16 August 2026 |access-date=16 August 2026}}</ref> Selby went on to win the match, and the tournament 10–6, for his 26th ranking win and his fourth [[China Open (snooker)|China Open]] title.<ref name="WST_day09">{{cite web |title=Selby too strong for Saengkham in China final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/august/16/Selby-Too-Strong-For-Saengkham-In-China-Final/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=16 August 2026 |access-date=16 August 2026}}</ref> After the match Selby said: "I'm very happy to be standing here as champion, I felt I played some really good stuff all week, Noppon {{bracket|Saengkham}} played well in the first session and I was fortunate to pinch the last two frames and finish 4–4, I was over the moon with that scoreline because Noppon probably felt he deserved to be ahead." Saengkham said: "Mark {{bracket|Selby}} was very strong today and controlled the game very well, his safety game was so good. When I was 4–2 up he was under pressure but in the evening the game changed and it looked like Mark was controlling everything. I am very happy with the way I played during the tournament and to be here until the end. I will keep trying hard and hopefully one day I will be the champion."<ref name="WST_day09" /><ref>{{cite news |title=Selby defeats Saengkham to win fourth China Open |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cpd77pl86wqo |work=[[BBC Sport]] |first=Steve |last=Sutcliffe |date=16 August 2026 |access-date=16 August 2026}}</ref><ref name="WST_final_match">{{cite web |title=Match: Mark Selby v Noppon Saengkham |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/eb26d0bd-3b44-499c-a0bf-4ba2902991e2 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=16 August 2026 |access-date=18 August 2026 |archive-date=18 August 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260818075311/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/eb26d0bd-3b44-499c-a0bf-4ba2902991e2 |url-status=live}}</ref>
