The '''2026 Shenzhen Open''' was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 28 September to 4 October 2026 at the [[Bao'an Stadium|Bao'an Sports Centre]] in [[Shenzhen]], China. Formerly staged in [[Xi'an]] as the Xi'an Grand Prix, the tournament was relocated and rebranded as the [[Shenzhen Open (snooker)|Shenzhen Open]] for the 2026 edition. Qualifying took place from 19 to 22 July at the [[Leicester Arena]] in [[Leicester]], England. The third consecutive edition of the tournament since its inaugural staging in [[2024 Xi'an Grand Prix|2024]], it was the sixth [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2026–27 snooker season]], following the [[2026 English Open (snooker)|2026 English Open]] and preceding the [[2026 Northern Ireland Open]]. The winner received £177,000 from a total prize fund of £850,000.

[[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Shaun Murphy]] 10–3 in the [[2025 Xi'an Grand Prix|2025 final]], but he was beaten by [[Fan Zhengyi]] in the last 64.

[[Wu Yize]] won the tournament, beating [[Yuan Sijun]] 10–5 in the final.

==Overview==
The [[Shenzhen Open (snooker)|Shenzhen Open]] was a professional ranking snooker tournament held in China. The first two editions, in [[2024 Xi'an Grand Prix|2024]] and [[2025 Xi'an Grand Prix|2025]], were staged in [[Xi'an]] as the Xi'an Grand Prix. [[Kyren Wilson]] won the inaugural edition, defeating [[Judd Trump]] 10–8 in the final.<ref>{{cite web |title=Xi'an Grand Prix |url=https://www.wst.tv/xiangrandprix/ |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |access-date=22 July 2024 |archive-date=20 July 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240720171233/https://www.wst.tv/xiangrandprix/ |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=25 August 2024 |title=Wilson beats Trump to win Xi'an Grand Prix final |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cd6y7dnxwj9o |access-date=18 April 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260418115246/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cd6y7dnxwj9o |archive-date=18 April 2026 |url-status=live}}</ref> The 2026 edition—for which the tournament was relocated to [[Shenzhen]] and rebranded as the Shenzhen Open—took place from 28 September to 4 October.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/june/30/shenzhen-open-added-to-calendar-as-xi-an-event-relocates/ |title=Shenzhen Open added to calendar as Xi'an event relocates |date=30 June 2026 |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |access-date=30 June 2026 |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Shenzhen Open officially launched |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/september/04/Shenzhen-Open-Officially-Launched/ |date=4 September 2026 |access-date=5 September 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref><ref name="s.o_M">{{cite web |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2761 |title=Shenzhen Open 2026 |website=snooker.org |access-date=26 May 2026 |language=en}}</ref> Qualifying took place from 19 to 22 July at the [[Leicester Arena]] in [[Leicester]], England.<ref name="WST_matches_Q">{{cite web |title=Shenzhen Open 2026 qualifiers matches |url=https://www.wst.tv/matches/5787c207-89fe-4d52-8003-011041193771? All {{cuegloss|match|matches}} were the best of nine {{cuegloss|frame|frames}} up to the semi-finals, which were the best of eleven frames. The final was a best of 19 frame match, played over two {{cuegloss|session|sessions}}.<ref name="s.o_M" />

There were 112 [[century break]]s made during the event, with 56 mage during qualifying<ref name="si_cent_Q" /> and 56 at the main venue.<ref name="si_cent_M" /> The highest {{cuegloss|break}} was a [[Maximum break|147]] made by [[Chang Bingyu]] during qualifying.<ref name="Max 244" />

==== Semi-finals ====
The semi-finals were played on 3 October as the best of 11 frames.<ref name="s.o_M" />

[[File:Yuan Sijun 2025.jpg|left|upright=0.85|thumb|alt=picture of Yuan Sijun|[[Yuan Sijun]] (''pictured in 2025'') beat [[Jimmy Robertson (snooker player)|Jimmy Robertson]] 6–5.]]

In the afternoon {{cuegloss|session}}, Yuan beat Robertson 6–5 to reach his first [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking]] final. After the match Yuan said: "The pressure was huge throughout the whole match, right from the moment I came out to play. I could feel that I was playing completely differently compared with the previous few matches. Although I kept telling myself not to think about it, sometimes your brain just instinctively and subconsciously goes back to those thoughts. There were so many different feelings mixed together. There is happiness, but also the sense of how difficult it has been and all the struggles over the years. I suppose, in a way, this is a little bit of an answer to myself after all these years. So many emotions came together, and in the end, I probably just couldn't quite control them."<ref>{{cite web |title=Yuan reaches maiden ranking final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/october/03/yuan-reaches-maiden-ranking-final/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=3 October 2026 |access-date=3 October 2026}}</ref><ref name="TS_semiFinals">{{cite web |title=World champ Wu Yize to face Yuan Sijun in all-Chinese Shenzhen Open snooker final |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/world-champ-wu-yize-to-face-yuan-sijun-in-all-chinese-shenzhen-open-snooker-final-9262723 |website=totallysnookered.com |first=Michael |last=Day |date=3 October 2026 |access-date=3 October 2026}}</ref>

[[File:Wu Yize 2025.jpg|upright=0.85|thumb|alt=picture of Wu Yize|[[Wu Yize]] (''pictured in 2025'') beat [[Judd Trump]] 6–1.]]

In the evening session, Wu beat Trump 6–1. After the match Wu said: "This was the best {{Cuegloss|safety}} performance of my professional career. It would have been nice if I could have been a little more fluent in scoring. This match was also very important for my career. To be able to perform at this level in terms of safety play is very satisfying. Playing Judd {{bracket|Trump}} is always very straightforward in one sense. Even though I won six frames in a row, he put me under a huge amount of pressure in every frame. I needed to perform well in every aspect because it's very difficult to get easy chances from him."<ref>{{cite web |title=Wu overpowers Trump to make final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/october/03/wu-overpowers-trump-to-make-final/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=3 October 2026 |access-date=3 October 2026}}</ref><ref name="TS_semiFinals" /><ref>{{cite news |title=Wu thrashes Trump to set up Yuan final in Shenzhen |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cm1exp2nj1y9o |work=[[BBC Sport]] |date=3 October 2026 |access-date=3 October 2026}}</ref>

==== Final ====
The final was played on 4 October as the best of 19 frames, played over two sessions. Yuan faced Wu.<ref name="s.o_M" />

At the end of the afternoon session, Wu led Yuan 5–4.<ref>{{cite web |title=Narrow lead for Wu in Shenzhen final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/october/03/Narrow-Lead-For-Wu-In-Shenzhen-Final/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=4 October 2026 |access-date=4 October 2026}}</ref> Wu went on to win the match, and the tournament, 10–5.<ref name="WST_Yuan_Wu">{{cite web |title=Match: Yuan Sijun v Wu Yize |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/b62796f2-4c12-45b1-9298-d49d86d38a41 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=4 October 2026 |access-date=4 October 2026 |archive-date=4 October 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20261004162020/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/b62796f2-4c12-45b1-9298-d49d86d38a41 |url-status=live}}</ref> After the match, Wu said: "This time I was able to be a little more composed than I was in winning my previous two titles, I've grown both physically and mentally compared with before. I just hope I can continue to get better from here and keep pushing myself to break through. Maybe the pressure at the [[2026 World Snooker Championship|World Championship]] had become so great that I forgot how to enjoy it, I was completely immersed in the competition. Perhaps the happiness was still there, but I had already buried it somewhere deep inside. I wanted to prove myself again. There have definitely been a lot of voices around me recently, and I felt that I needed to keep improving, win another title and keep pushing myself towards my peak. So this is another breakthrough for me. Today Yuan {{bracket|Sijun}} put me under a lot of pressure in the first session. The turning point was probably the eighth frame, I had already prepared myself for being 5–3 behind. In the second session, he was probably under a little bit of pressure as well." Yuan said: "Overall, I think it was a pretty good performance. I played quite well in the first half of the tournament, but over the final two days I didn’t perform at the level I know I'm capable of, so hopefully I can do better next time. I just didn't play very well tonight and I wasn't able to put him {{bracket|Wu}} under enough pressure. I really wasn't nervous at all today. I just felt like my whole body was completely out of sorts. I felt really uncomfortable, and no matter how I tried to aim or strike the ball, it just didn't feel right. So the whole day was actually quite frustrating for me."<ref name="WST_FinalResult" /><ref>{{cite news |title=Wu wins first title since Crucible at Shenzhen Open |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cq4g1l0gxl44o |work=[[BBC Sport]] |first=Timothy |last=Abraham |date=4 October 2026 |access-date=5 October 2026}}</ref>

As a result of the win, Wu moved from fourth to second in the [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|world rankings]], while Yuan moved from 33rd to 26th.<ref>{{cite web |title=Johnstone's Paint world rankings update: Wu up to two |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/october/03/Johnstone-s-Paint-World-Rankings-Update--Wu-Up-To-Two/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=4 October 2026 |access-date=6 October 2026}}</ref>
