The '''2025 Snooker Shoot Out''' (officially the '''2025 9Club Snooker Shoot Out''') was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 10 to 13 December 2025 at the [[Tower Circus]] in [[Blackpool]], England, where the tournament was previously held from 2011 to 2015.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |title=Shoot Out |url=https://www.wst.tv//betvictorshootout |access-date=2025-07-15 |website=World Snooker Tour |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |title=Tournaments 2025–26 |url=https://www.wst.tv/tournaments |access-date=2025-07-15 |website=World Snooker Tour |language=en}}</ref> The 16th consecutive edition of the tournament since it was revived in [[2011 Snooker Shoot-Out|2011]], it was the 10th [[Snooker world rankings|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season|2025{{nbnd}}26 season]], following the [[2025 UK Championship]] and preceding the [[2025 Scottish Open (snooker)|2025 Scottish Open]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Calendar 2025/2026 - snooker.org |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?template=2 |access-date=2025-07-23 |website=www.snooker.org |language=en}}</ref> It was broadcast by [[TNT Sports]] in the United Kingdom and Ireland, by [[Eurosport]] in mainland Europe, by local channels in China and elsewhere in Asia, and by [[WST Play]] in all other territories. The winner received £50,000 from a total prize fund of £171,000.

The tournament was played under a variation of the standard [[rules of snooker]], with each match contested over a single {{cuegloss|frame}}, lasting a maximum of 10 minutes. [[Tom Ford (snooker player)|Tom Ford]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Liam Graham (snooker player)|Liam Graham]] 31{{nbnd}}28 in the [[2024 Snooker Shoot Out|2024 final]], but he lost 21–55 to [[Stuart Bingham]] in the fourth round. Former professional player [[Alfie Burden]], competing in the tournament as an amateur substitute, won the first ranking title of his 30-year career by defeating Bingham 63–8 in the final. He won the title the day before his 49th birthday, making him the oldest maiden ranking event winner, surpassing [[Doug Mountjoy]], who had won the [[1988 UK Championship]] at age 46. Burden was the second player to win a ranking title while competing as an amateur, following [[Zhao Xintong]] at the [[2025 World Snooker Championship]].

The tournament produced four [[Century break|century breaks]], the highest of which was a 141 by [[Wu Yize]] in the first round. Wu's century was the third-highest break in the history of the event.

==Overview==
The tournament was the seventeenth edition of the [[Snooker Shoot Out]], first held as a non{{nbh}}ranking event in [[1990 Shoot-Out|1990]] before being revived in [[2011 Snooker Shoot-Out|2011]] and since then having been held annually. It was promoted to a ranking event starting with the [[2017 Snooker Shoot Out|2017]] edition. [[Tom Ford (snooker player)|Tom Ford]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Liam Graham (snooker player)|Liam Graham]] 31{{nbnd}}28 in the [[2024 Snooker Shoot Out|2024 final]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-12-07 |title=Snooker Shoot Out: Tom Ford wins emotional first ranking title in Leicester by beating Liam Graham |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cly77dz0mdlo |access-date=2025-07-23 |website=BBC Sport |language=en-GB}}</ref>

===Format===

The tournament was played using a variation of the traditional [[Rules of snooker|snooker rules]]. The draw was randomised before each round. All matches were played over a single {{cuegloss|frame}}, each of which lasted up to 10 minutes. The event featured a variable [[shot clock]]; players were allowed 15 seconds per shot during the first five minutes and 10 seconds per shot during the final five minutes. The player with the most points after the time runs out or after all balls have been pocketed (or after a foul on the final {{cuegloss|black ball|black}}) won the match. All {{cuegloss|foul|foul shots}} resulted in the opponent receiving a {{cuegloss|ball in hand}}. Unlike traditional snooker, a foul was called if a ball did not either hit a {{cuegloss|cushion}} or enter a pocket on every shot. Rather than a [[coin toss]], a {{cuegloss|lag}} was used to determine which player {{cuegloss|break off|broke off}}. In the event of a draw, the frame would be decided by a "{{cuegloss|blue ball}} shootout". With the cue ball placed inside {{Cuegloss|"D", the |the "D"}} and the blue ball on its spot, the players took alternate shots until one player had potted the blue more times than their opponent from an equal number of attempts.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Rules of the Game {{!}} WPBSA {{!}} Snooker and English Billiards |url=https://wpbsa.com/rules/ |access-date=2025-07-15 |website=WPBSA |language=en-GB}}</ref>

== Summary ==

=== Round one (last 128) ===
The first-round matches were played on 10 and 11 December.<ref name="TNT_format_etc" /><ref name="s.o_main">{{cite web |title=9Club Shoot Out 2025 |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2310 |access-date=13 December 2025 |website=snooker.org}}</ref> The defending champion [[Tom Ford (snooker player)|Tom Ford]] defeated [[Martin O'Donnell (snooker player)|Martin O'Donnell]] by nine points. "Getting the first win on the board definitely settles your nerves," said Ford afterwards. "I think quickly and I usually know what I'm doing before the white stops rolling so that's why this event suits me." [[Michael Holt (snooker player)|Michael Holt]], winner in [[2020 Snooker Shoot Out|2020]] and runner-up in [[2019 Snooker Shoot Out|2019]], trailed [[Ashley Hugill]] by seven points with 45 seconds remaining. He {{Cuegloss|snooker|snookered}} Hugill, who then committed a time foul, allowing Holt to pot a {{Cuegloss|red ball|red}} and a {{Cuegloss|pink ball|pink}} to win. The victory was controversial, as bleeps did not sound to warn Hugill that the shot clock was about to expire.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jones |first=Matt |date=11 December 2025 |title=Snooker Shoot Out chaos: Jimmy White, Alan McManus react as Ashley Hugill beaten after 'unfair' bleeper error - 'Story will not end there' |url=https://www.tntsports.co.uk/snooker/chaos-jimmy-white-alan-mcmanus-ashley-hugill-unfair-bleeper-error-michael-holt_sto23249330/story.shtml |access-date=11 December 2025 |website=[[TNT Sports]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> [[Alfie Burden]], who secured a place in the event that morning when Alex Clenshaw withdrew, faced [[David Lilley (snooker player)|David Lilley]] after a four-hour drive from London. Burden came from 46 points behind to win the match with a 49 break. [[Luca Brecel]] won his first professional match since September as he defeated [[Long Zehuang]]. Brecel said afterwards: "I felt very calm, in control of the game and potted some good balls. In this event you just have to pot balls and don't do stupid things."<ref>{{cite web |date=10 December 2025 |title=Snooker Shoot Out day one afternoon |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/10/9club-shoot-out-day-one-afternoon/ |access-date=11 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

[[Shaun Murphy]], the only player in the event's history to make a [[maximum break]], potted five reds and five {{Cuegloss|black ball|blacks}} before missing the sixth red. He later made another break of 51 to secure victory over Patrick Whelan. "Not everything has to be hard work and super-serious all the time," Murphy said. "It's great to come here and have interaction with the crowd, like a big exhibition. I love it!" [[Ishpreet Singh Chadha]] trailed [[Dean Young (snooker player)|Dean Young]] by 11 points with 50 seconds remaining. Young missed a red, and Chadha potted two reds and two blacks to secure victory. [[David Gilbert (snooker player)|David Gilbert]] made a 106 break as he beat [[Matthew Stevens]], and [[Sanderson Lam]] made a 105 to defeat Jonas Luz.<ref>{{cite web |date=10 December 2025 |title=Snooker Shoot Out day one evening |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/10/9club-shoot-out-day-one-evening/ |access-date=11 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>
[[File:Ng On Yee PHC 2017-3.jpg|thumb|[[Ng On-yee]] ''(pictured in 2017)'' defeated the world number nine [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]]. She was the first woman to beat a top-10 player at a ranking event.]]
[[Ng On-yee]] made a 25 break to move 16 points ahead of the world number nine [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]], winner of the [[2023 Snooker Shoot Out (2023–24 season)|December 2023]] event. In the last seconds, Allen potted two reds and two pinks but missed a {{Cuegloss|plant}}; Ng won the match, becoming the first woman to beat a top-10 player at a ranking event. She said afterwards: "I hope it helps people realise this is not just a male sport. Women can play snooker too and hopefully there will be more to join our sport." [[Ellise Scott]], aged 14, attempted to become the youngest female player to win a match at a ranking event. She led the reigning World Women's Champion [[Bai Yulu]] by two points with less than a minute remaining, but Bai secured victory after Scott missed a shot with the {{Cuegloss|rest}}. [[Wu Yize]] made a 141 {{Cuegloss|total clearance}} against [[Craig Steadman]], the third-highest break in the tournament's history. The reigning World Champion [[Zhao Xintong]] led [[Ross Muir]] by eight points with two minutes remaining, but Muir won the match with a break of 15 after Zhao missed a red. The previous year's runner-up [[Liam Graham (snooker player)|Liam Graham]] defeated [[Cheung Ka Wai]], saying afterwards: "This format really suits me, I play fast and I enjoy the buzz and the crowd." [[Michał Szubarczyk]], aged 14 and playing in his debut season on the tour, defeated [[Jamie Jones (snooker player)|Jamie Jones]].<ref>{{cite web |date=11 December 2025 |title=Snooker Shoot Out day two afternoon |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/11/shoot-out-day-two-afternoon/ |access-date=11 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

[[Sahil Nayyar]], playing in his first season on the professional tour, defeated [[Antoni Kowalski]]. Having trailed by one point with two minutes remaining, Nayyar potted a red and a blue to move ahead, securing victory after Kowalski missed a red. It was his second win on the professional tour. "It was nerve-wracking but I enjoyed it," Nayyar said afterwards. "When I was preparing I just tried to see the ball and hit it fast, go for it and whatever happens will happen." Amateur player Zachary Richardson trailed the world number 15 [[Si Jiahui]] 8–26 but won the match with a 59 break, becoming one of ten amateur players to reach the last 64. "I am really pleased by how I took the opportunity," he said. "It was a really good experience." [[Stephen Maguire]] lost to [[Farakh Ajaib]], and former winner [[Anthony McGill]] lost to [[Liam Davies (snooker player)|Liam Davies]]. [[Stuart Bingham]] made a 76 break to defeat Jack O'Brien.<ref>{{cite web |date=11 December 2025 |title=9Club Shoot Out day two evening |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/11/9club-shoot-out-day-two-evening/ |access-date=12 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

=== Round two (last 64) ===
The second-round matches were played on 12 December.<ref name="TNT_format_etc" /><ref name="s.o_main" /> [[Stan Moody]] trailed [[Lan Yuhao]] by 42 points with two minutes and four reds remaining but cleared to the pink to win the match. "I'm still shaking, I thought I was out," said Moody afterwards. "It was amazing at the end when the crowd were chanting my name. I love getting them involved and having some fun." From 52 points behind against [[Ricky Walden]], [[Liam Highfield]] made breaks of 38 and 15, moving one point in front. With seconds remaining, Walden missed a cut on the {{Cuegloss|yellow ball|yellow}} to a {{Cuegloss|baulk pocket}}, and Highfield secured victory. [[Oliver Lines]] came from 36 points behind to beat [[Chang Bingyu]], while the defending champion Ford defeated [[Jimmy Robertson (snooker player)|Jimmy Robertson]], and Gilbert beat Wu. Belgian players [[Ben Mertens]] and [[Julien Leclercq (snooker player)|Julien Leclercq]] advanced with victories over Graham and Szubarczyk respectively.<ref>{{cite web |date=12 December 2025 |title=9Club Shoot Out day three afternoon |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/12/shoot-out-day-three-afternoon/ |access-date=12 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

Ng took a 51–0 lead over [[Aaron Hill (snooker player)|Aaron Hill]], who responded with a break of 45. Ng secured victory after Hill missed the last red to a baulk pocket with ten seconds remaining. She reached the last 32 of a ranking event for the second time that season, having previously reached that stage at the [[2025 Championship League (ranking)|2025 Championship League]]. "I'm very happy to win a couple of matches and it helps me just to play against the likes of [Allen] and [Hill]," she said afterwards. "In practice I have tried to be decisive, getting down on the shot and committing to it." Brecel made a 52 break to beat Murphy. Muir trailed [[Liu Hongyu (snooker player)|Liu Hongyu]] 34–60 with a minute remaining but made a clearance to win the match, potting the black with just five seconds left.

[[Mitchell Mann]] trailed [[Stuart Carrington]] by two points with 30 seconds left but won the match after Carrington {{Cuegloss|foul|fouled}} the blue while playing a {{Cuegloss|safety}} shot. [[Jackson Page]] recovered from 28 points behind to defeat Holt. [[Bulcsú Révész]] secured his first appearance in the last 32 of a ranking event as he beat Davies. Kaylan Patel, a 17-year-old amateur, defeated fellow amateur Kayden Brierley and paid tribute afterwards to practice partner [[Ben Woollaston]], who he said had helped him improve as a player. [[Amir Sarkhosh]] defeated Nayyar with an 85 break, and Burden also made an 85 break as he beat Chadha. [[Elliot Slessor]] made a break of 57 as he advanced with a win over amateur Paul Deaville.<ref>{{cite web |date=12 December 2025 |title=9Club Shoot Out day three evening |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/12/Shoot-Out-Day-Three-Evening/ |access-date=13 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

=== Round three (last 32) ===
The third-round matches were played on 13 December.<ref name="TNT_format_etc" /><ref name="s.o_main" /> Five amateur players, Ethan Llewellyn, Patel, Halim Hussain, Richardson, and Burden reached the last 16. Llewellyn, playing the day before his 18th birthday, defeated [[Ryan Day (snooker player)|Ryan Day]]. "It's unbelievable, I just came here for the experience but I have played well," he said "I am getting more shaky as the day goes on but hopefully I can hold it together tonight." Patel defeated Révész, while Richardson beat Mann and Hussain defeated [[Oliver Brown (snooker player)|Oliver Brown]]. Burden defeated [[Gary Wilson (snooker player)|Gary Wilson]], eliminating the last top-16 player from the event. Defending champion Ford won his 10th consecutive Shoot Out match, beating Gilbert. Brecel lost to [[Fan Zhengyi]], who made a break of 75. Moody defeated Ng, and Bingham made a 74 break to beat Lam. [[Iulian Boiko]] defeated Lines after Lines missed a red to a corner pocket with two minutes remaining.<ref>{{cite web |date=13 December 2025 |title=9Club Shoot Out day four afternoon |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/13/shoot-out-day-four-afternoon/ |access-date=13 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

=== Round four (last 16) ===
The fourth-round matches were played on 13 December.<ref name="TNT_format_etc" /><ref name="s.o_main" /> In a match between two amateurs, Richardson defeated Patel. Mertens defeated Hussain, reaching the quarter-finals of a ranking event for the second time in his career. After a foul by Llewellyn, Yuan Sijun made a century of 126 to advance. Boiko defeated Slessor after Slessor fouled by failing to hit a cushion with any ball, a rule unique to the Shoot Out format. Facing Burden, Highfield led by five points with a minute remaining but missed a blue, and Burden won the match. Fan failed to score a single point against Page, who advanced with a 68–0 victory. Bingham came from 21 points behind to beat the defending champion Ford 55–21. Moody took a 31-point lead over [[Noppon Saengkham]], but Saengkham recovered to win the match 43–36.<ref name="TNT_Burden">{{Cite web |last=Chan |first=Ka Nam Castor |date=13 December 2025 |title=Shoot Out recap - Late replacement Alfie Burden wins first ranking title with resounding victory over Stuart Bingham in Blackpool |url=https://www.tntsports.co.uk/snooker/9club-shoot-out/2025-2026/liveevent.shtml |access-date=14 December 2025 |website=[[TNT Sports]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

In the match between Yuan and Boiko, only one point was scored in the first five minutes; Boiko eventually won the match  28–18. Saengkham defeated Mertens, who conceded while trailing by 30 points with a minute remaining. Burden defeated fellow amateur Richardson 42–7 after a number of missed pots by Richardson.<ref name="TNT_Burden" />

The semi-finals were played on 13 December.<ref name="TNT_format_etc" /><ref name="s.o_main" /> Boiko took a 26-point lead over Bingham, but Bingham recovered to lead by 13. Bingham won the match 44–35 after Boiko, with 40 seconds to go, missed the pink to a centre pocket. Saengkham took a 14-point lead over Burden, but Burden recovered to make a match-winning break of 73.<ref name="TNT_Burden" /> 

=== Final ===
[[File:Alfie Burden PHC 2016.jpg|upright=0.85|thumb|alt=Photograph of Alfie Burden|[[Alfie Burden]] ''(pictured in 2016)'' defeated [[Stuart Bingham]] in the final to win his maiden ranking title.]]
The final was played on 13 December between Burden and Bingham.<ref name="TNT_format_etc" /><ref name="s.o_main" /> Burden was playing in the final of the event for the first time, while Bingham had been runner-up at the [[2014 Snooker Shoot-Out|2014 edition]], prior to the tournament becoming a ranking event. Bingham had the first scoring opportunity in the final, but missed a red on a break of 6, and Burden moved ahead with a half-century of 56. Bingham then had another scoring opportunity and potted a red, but conceded the match after missing the blue. Burden secured the maiden ranking title of his career, having never previously advanced beyond the quarter-finals of a ranking event. He won the title the day before his 49th birthday, making him the oldest first-time ranking event winner, surpassing [[Doug Mountjoy]], who had won the [[1988 UK Championship]] at age 46.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2022-03-26 |title=Milkins Rules The Rock With First Ranking Crown |url=https://wst.tv/milkins-rules-the-rock-with-first-ranking-crown/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220326223019/https://wst.tv/milkins-rules-the-rock-with-first-ranking-crown/ |archive-date=March 26, 2022 |access-date=2022-03-26 |website=World Snooker |language=en-US}}</ref> He became the second player to win a ranking title while competing as an amateur, following [[Zhao Xintong]] at the [[2025 World Snooker Championship]]. The £50,000 first prize was the largest prize he had ever received in snooker.

"I can't believe I have won it," said Burden afterwards. "From the start I have played well. Everything fell into place for me. I am a ranking event winner now and that is something I am very proud of. After dropping off the tour last season, I found myself in the wilderness. I have had no motivation to play on the [[Q Tour]], I turned up for a few events in terrible shape having not practised. Then a couple of weeks ago I went to the one in Bulgaria with a new cue and got to the semi-finals. Now I am hoping this win will get me back on the tour." Runner-up Bingham praised his opponent, saying "He looked so cool and calm all the way through. He played brilliantly from the start."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jackson |first=Bobbie |date=13 December 2025 |title=Burden beats Bingham in Shoot Out for first ranking title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cwyv8njyv4qo |access-date=14 December 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=13 December 2025 |title=Jubilant Burden wins first ranking title at Shoot Out |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/13/Jubilant-Burden-Wins-First-Ranking-Title-At-Shoot-Out-/ |access-date=14 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>
