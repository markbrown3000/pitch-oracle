The '''2025 International Championship''' was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 2 to 9 November 2025 at the South New City National Fitness Center (SNCNFC) in [[Nanjing]], China. Qualifiers took place from 30 September to 2 October at the [[Ponds Forge|Ponds Forge International Sports Centre]] in [[Sheffield]], England. The 11th edition of the [[International Championship]] since it was first staged in [[2012 International Championship|2012]], it was the eighth [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season]], following the [[2025 Northern Ireland Open]] and preceding the [[2025 UK Championship]]. The tournament was broadcast by local channels in China and elsewhere in Asia, by [[TNT Sports]] in the United Kingdom and Ireland, by [[Eurosport]] in mainland Europe, and by [[WST Play]] in all other territories. The winner received £175,000 from a total prize fund of £825,000.

[[Ding Junhui]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Chris Wakelin]] 10–7 in the [[2024 International Championship|2024 final]], but he lost 2–6 to [[Shaun Murphy]] in the fourth round. The final was contested between 50-year-old [[John Higgins]] and 22-year-old [[Wu Yize]], giving rise to the fourth-largest age difference of any ranking final in snooker history. Following defeats in his two previous ranking finals, Wu defeated Higgins 10–6 to win his maiden ranking title. He advanced to a career high of 13th place in the world rankings, entering the top 16 for the first time. 

The tournament produced a record 160 [[Century break|century breaks]], 37 during the qualifying matches in Sheffield and 123 at the main venue in Nanjing, surpassing the previous record of 155 set at the [[2023 International Championship|2023 event]]. Wu made 14 centuries during the tournament. Two [[Maximum break|maximum breaks]] were compiled at the event: [[Gary Wilson (snooker player)|Gary Wilson]] made the sixth maximum of his career during his 6–3 win over [[Artemijs Žižins]] in the Sheffield qualifiers, and [[Zak Surety]] made the second maximum of his career in his 6–5 win over [[Aaron Hill (snooker player)|Aaron Hill]] in the third round. These were respectively the 11th and 14th maximums of the season and the 228th and 231st official maximums in professional snooker history. 

During his 6–1 win over Umut Dikme in the Sheffield qualifiers, [[Neil Robertson]] became the fourth player to reach the milestone of 1,000 career [[century break]]s, following [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]], Higgins, and [[Judd Trump]]. During her pre-qualifying match in Nanjing, [[Bai Yulu]] compiled a century of 145, the highest break by a female player in professional competition.

==Overview==
The [[International Championship]] is a professional ranking snooker tournament held in China. It first took place in [[2012 International Championship|2012]] and the inaugural champion was [[Judd Trump]], who defeated [[Neil Robertson]] 10–8 in the final.<ref>{{Cite news |date=4 November 2012 |title=Judd Trump beats Robertson in International Championship final |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/20199610 |access-date=4 September 2025 |work=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-date=16 May 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230516180219/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/20199610 |url-status=live }}</ref> Staged from 2012 to 2014 in [[Chengdu]] and from 2015 to 2019 in [[Daqing]], the tournament was not held from 2020 to 2022 due to the impact of the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. ===Format===
Matches were played as the best of 11 {{cuegloss|frame|frames}} until the semi‑finals, which were played as the best of 17 frames, held over two {{cuegloss|session|sessions}}. The final was the best of 19 frames, also held over two sessions.

[[Marco Fu]] withdrew from the tournament before the qualifiers after suffering a fractured elbow. He was replaced in the qualifying draw by amateur player Umut Dikme.<ref name="WST_MAR_wd">{{Cite web |date=28 September 2025 |title=Fu out for two months with fractured elbow |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/september/28/Fu-Out-For-Two-Months-With-Fractured-Elbow/ |access-date=28 September 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260421070856/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/september/28/Fu-Out-For-Two-Months-With-Fractured-Elbow/|archive-date=21 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]], [[Ali Carter]], [[Sam Craigie]], [[Ken Doherty]], and [[David Gilbert (snooker player)|David Gilbert]] also withdrew before the event's main stage. Their respective opponents, [[Anthony McGill]], [[Aaron Hill (snooker player)|Aaron Hill]], [[Jimmy Robertson (snooker player)|Jimmy Robertson]], [[Neil Robertson]], and [[Thepchaiya Un-Nooh]], all received [[Bye (sports)|byes]] to the last 32.<ref name="withdrawal_1">{{cite web |title=Withdrawals from International Championship |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/october/27/williams-and-carter-withdraw-from-international-championship-/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=27 October 2025 |access-date=28 October 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260421071404/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/october/27/williams-and-carter-withdraw-from-international-championship-/|archive-date=21 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Round two (last 64) ===

In the second semi-final, Higgins faced Maguire, the first time they had met in professional competition since 2019. Higgins made breaks including 67, 71, 101, 137, and 97 as he won the first six frames. Maguire, who had won the [[2025 Championship League (ranking)|2025 Championship League]] earlier in the season, took frame seven with a century of 114 and also won frame eight to leave Higgins leading 6–2 after the first session.<ref name="WST_Higgins_Maguire_1">{{cite web |date=8 November 2025 |title=Higgins dominates opening session |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/08/higgins-dominates-opening-session/ |access-date=8 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260421073003/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/08/higgins-dominates-opening-session/|archive-date=21 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> When play resumed, Higgins won three consecutive frames, making breaks including 93 and 84, as he completed a 9–2 victory.<ref>{{cite web |date=8 November 2025 |title=2025 International Championship: John Higgins v Stephen Maguire |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/07dc3f4c-18a8-4e52-a267-8fda24df21ae |access-date=8 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260421072954/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/07dc3f4c-18a8-4e52-a267-8fda24df21ae|archive-date=21 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> It was his fifth consecutive win over Maguire, who had not defeated Higgins since 2014. Commenting on reaching his third ranking final of the calendar year, after winning the [[2025 World Open (snooker)|2025 World Open]] and [[2025 Tour Championship]], Higgins said: "It was pivotal to win the World Open. It gave me that bit of self-belief in my game. It doesn't matter who you are, when you start losing that self-belief the game becomes so difficult. Especially when it comes to getting over the winning line. To make it concrete in my mind and say I was a winner again took the pressure off."<ref name="WST_Higgins_Maguire_2">{{cite web |date=8 November 2025 |title=Higgins demolishes Maguire to make final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/08/higgins-demolishes-maguire-to-make-final/ |access-date=8 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260421072939/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/08/higgins-demolishes-maguire-to-make-final/|archive-date=21 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Nelson |first=Craig |date=8 November 2025 |title=Higgins powers past Maguire to set up Wu final |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c5y4xx708gko |access-date=8 November 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-date=9 December 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251209094830/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c5y4xx708gko |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Final ===
[[File:Wu Yize in 2025.jpg|thumb|[[Wu Yize]] ''(pictured in 2025)'' defeated [[John Higgins]] to win his maiden ranking title and enter the top 16 for the first time.]]
The final was played as the best of 19 frames, held over two sessions, between the world number 6 John Higgins and the world number 22 Wu Yize. Higgins was contesting his 59th ranking final and Wu his 3rd.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Day |first=Michael |date=8 November 2025 |title=Wu Yize and John Higgins to contest International Championship final in Nanjing |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/international-championship/wu-yize-and-john-higgins-to-contest-international-championship-final-in-nanjing-5393961 |access-date=8 November 2025 |website=Totally Snookered |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420141859/https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/international-championship/wu-yize-and-john-higgins-to-contest-international-championship-final-in-nanjing-5393961|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="WST_WuYize" /> The players had previously met in the held-over qualifiers at the [[2019 International Championship|2019 event]] when Wu was a 15-year-old amateur; on that occasion, Higgins recovered from 4–5 behind to win 6–5.<ref>{{Cite web |date=4 August 2019 |title=Higgins and Ding narrowly avoid upsets in International Championship |url=https://www.tntsports.co.uk/snooker/international-championship/2019-2020/snooker-news-trump-eases-past-brown-in-international-championship_sto7397082/story.shtml |access-date=8 November 2025 |website=[[TNT Sports]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> The age difference between 50-year-old Higgins and 22-year-old Wu was the fourth-largest of any ranking final in professional snooker history. The largest age difference had been recorded at the [[2005 UK Championship]] final when 48-year-old [[Steve Davis]] played 18-year-old Ding.<ref name="WST_Higgins_Maguire_2" />

Wu won the opening two frames with breaks of 86 and 62. Higgins took frame three with a century of 101, but Wu made back-to-back centuries of 110 and 113 as he moved 4–1 ahead. Higgins won frame six, but Wu took the seventh with a century of 137 to lead 5–2. Higgins won frame eight on the last {{Cuegloss|pink ball|pink}} and came from behind to win frame nine with a 46 break, reducing Wu's lead to 5–4 at the end of the first session.<ref name="WST_Wu_Higgins">{{cite web |date=9 November 2025 |title=Wu holds narrow lead over Higgins |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/09/wu-holds-narrow-lead-over-higgins/ |access-date=9 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420141901/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/09/wu-holds-narrow-lead-over-higgins/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> When play resumed, Wu won frame 10 with an 87 break. In frame 11, Higgins was leading when he missed a pot on the brown to a {{Cuegloss|baulk pocket}}. Wu made a {{Cuegloss|double}} and went on to produce a {{cuegloss|clearance}} of 43, including potting the last {{Cuegloss|green ball|green}} along the {{Cuegloss|baulk cushion}}, to win the frame on the last black. Wu attempted a maximum break in frame 12 but missed a double on the 11th red, ending the break at 80 but increasing his lead to four frames at 8–4. Higgins won frame 13, but Wu took the next to move one from victory at 9–5. Higgins won frame 15 with a 53 break after Wu missed a red while playing with the {{Cuegloss|rest}}, but Wu potted a long red from Higgins's {{Cuegloss|break|break-off}} in frame 16 and went on to secure a 10–6 victory with a 108 break, his fourth century of the final and fourteenth of the tournament.<ref>{{cite web |date=9 November 2025 |title=2025 International Championship: John Higgins v Wu Yize |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/95c919bd-2a30-4ae8-8492-309122ed0409 |access-date=9 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB |archive-date=10 November 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251110160810/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/95c919bd-2a30-4ae8-8492-309122ed0409 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="WST_WuWinner">{{cite web |date=9 November 2025 |title=Wonderful Wu captures maiden glory |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/09/wonderful-wu-captures-maiden-glory/ |access-date=9 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420141850/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/09/wonderful-wu-captures-maiden-glory/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="TS_Wu_Higgins">{{Cite web |last=Day |first=Michael |date=9 November 2025 |title=Wu Yize wins maiden ranking event title at the International Championship |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/international-championship/wu-yize-wins-maiden-ranking-event-title-at-the-2025-international-championship-in-nanjing-5394414 |access-date=9 November 2025 |website=Totally Snookered April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> The five centuries made in the final took the overall number of centuries at the event to a new record of 160, surpassing the 155 made at the 2023 event.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kane |first=Desmond |date=7 November 2025 |title=Wu Yize on brink of first major title after win over Zhao Xintong, but why has International Championship been hit by a century avalanche? |url=https://www.tntsports.co.uk/snooker/international-championship/2025-2026/wu-yize-brink-first-major-title-win-zhao-xintong-century-record_sto23238934/story.shtml |access-date=9 November 2025 |website=[[TNT Sports]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

Wu won the maiden ranking title of his four-year professional career, following defeats in the finals of the [[2024 English Open (snooker)|2024 English Open]] and the [[2024 Scottish Open (snooker)|2024 Scottish Open]]. He advanced from 22nd to 13th in the world rankings after the event, entering the top 16 for the first time and qualifying automatically for the final stages of the [[2025 UK Championship]], displacing Wakelin.<ref>{{Cite web |date=9 November 2025 |title='Total star' Wu beats Higgins for first title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c07m2zjm83mo |access-date=9 November 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-date=8 December 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251208215856/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c07m2zjm83mo |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="WST_Wu_Higgins" /><ref name="WST_raceToYork" /> Wu became the ninth different player from mainland China to win a ranking title.<ref name="TS_Wu_Higgins" /> "Honestly, it feels unbelievable," he said after the match. "It has been a long wait since reaching the final at the Scottish Open and the English Open last year. At times I honestly felt quite desperate, but deep down I always believed I had the ability to win a title. Every day I kept thinking about it. I had a strong will to lift a trophy. That belief carried me through this week." He paid tribute to the support he had received from his parents during his career. Runner-up Higgins said he had been "nowhere near good enough" during the match and was "disappointed" with his play, but he praised his opponent, saying: "[Wu] was striking the ball beautifully. He was by far the better player. It reminded me so much of playing [[Paul Hunter]]. The way he gets through the ball and gets so much action on it. He is a brilliant player."<ref name="WST_WuWinner" />
