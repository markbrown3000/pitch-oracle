{{2025 SEA Games}}

The '''2025 SEA Games''' ({{langx|th|ซีเกมส์ 2025}},{{efn|For official documents, it should be written as {{lang|th|กีฬาซีเกมส์ ๒๐๒๕}} using [[Thai numerals]].}} {{RTGS|''Kila Sikem 2025''}}, {{IPA|th|kīː.lāː sīː.kēːm sɔ̌ːŋ.pʰān.jîː.sìp̚.hâː|pron}}), officially called the '''33rd SEA Games''' ({{langx|th|กีฬาซีเกมส์ ครั้งที่ 33}},{{efn|{{lang|th|กีฬาซีเกมส์ ครั้งที่ ๓๓}} using Thai numerals}} {{RTGS|''Kila Sikem Khrangthi 33''}}, {{IPA|th|kīː.lāː sīː.kēːm kʰráŋ.tîː sǎːm.sìp̚ sǎːm|pron}}), was an international [[multi-sport event]] sanctioned by the [[Southeast Asian Games Federation]] (SEAGF). The event took place from 9 to 20 December 2025 and was held across the [[Bangkok Metropolitan Region]], as well as in the [[Chonburi province|Chonburi]] province in [[Thailand]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.naewna.com/sport/699989|script-title=th:ไทยได้จังหวัดจัดซีเกมส์ม.ค.นี้ ทรท.รับถ่ายทอดสดทั้งทัวร์นาเมนท์|trans-title=Thailand has been selected as the host province for the SEA Games this January. Thai TV (TRT) will broadcast the entire tournament live.|website=Naewna|date=23 December 2022|access-date=23 December 2022|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221224012809/https://www.naewna.com/sport/699989 |archive-date=24 December 2022 |url-status=live}}</ref>

The joint bid from the three provinces was awarded the right to host the Games on 13 January 2023 after Thailand was confirmed as the host country by the Southeast Asian Games Federation. This marked the first time that host cities were selected through a new bidding and election process for the SEA Games.<ref>{{cite news|title=Everything You Need to Know About the 33rd SEA Games 2025 in Thailand|url=https://www.sportplus.sg/post/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-33rd-sea-games-2025-in-thailand|author=John Yeong Murillo|newspaper=SportPlus|date=11 September 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251001005504/https://www.sportplus.sg/post/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-33rd-sea-games-2025-in-thailand|archive-date=1 October 2025|url-status=live}}</ref>

The 2025 SEA Games also marked the seventh time Thailand hosted the event. The co-host city, [[Bangkok]], had previously hosted the event four times ([[1959 SEAP Games|1959]], [[1967 SEAP Games|1967]], [[1975 SEAP Games|1975]], and [[1985 SEA Games|1985]]), while [[Chiang Mai]] hosted the [[1995 SEA Games|1995 edition]], and [[Nakhon Ratchasima]] hosted the [[2007 SEA Games|2007 edition]].

The original co-host, [[Songkhla province]], was planned to host some events, but flooding caused by [[Cyclone Senyar]] struck shortly before the Games, resulting in all events planned for Songkhla eventually being relocated to venues in Bangkok and Chonburi.

== Development and preparations ==
=== Host selection ===
Hosting duties are rotated among the member countries of the SEA Games Federation. Each member country is assigned a specific year to host the event, but they have the option to withdraw or opt out of hosting for that particular edition.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.weekender.bworldonline.com/2015/09/25/ready-for-sea-games-2019/|title=Ready for SEA Games 2019|author=Michael Angelo S. Murillo|newspaper=[[BusinessWorld|BusinessWorld Online (Weekender)]]|date=25 September 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150925125532/http://www.weekender.bworldonline.com/2015/09/25/ready-for-sea-games-2019/|archive-date=25 September 2015|url-status=dead}}</ref>

====2019 disruption====
On 21 July 2017, the [[Philippine Sports Commission]] (PSC) announced its decision to withdraw support for the country's hosting of the [[2019 SEA Games]]. The government chose to reallocate funds originally intended for the event to support the rehabilitation of [[Marawi]], which had been devastated by the [[Marawi crisis]].<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.abs-cbn.com/sports/07/21/17/ph-withdraws-hosting-of-2019-sea-games |title=PH withdraws hosting of 2019 SEA games |date=21 July 2017 |work=[[ABS-CBN News]]|access-date=21 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170723232839/https://news.abs-cbn.com/sports/07/21/17/ph-withdraws-hosting-of-2019-sea-games |archive-date=23 July 2017 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |url=https://sports.abs-cbn.com/generalsports/news/2017/07/21/phi-withdraws-hosting-2019-southeast-asian-games-28868 |title=PHI withdraws from hosting 2019 Southeast Asian Games. |last=Escarlote |first=Mark |date=21 July 2017 |work=[[ABS-CBN Sports]] |access-date=21 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170723000042/https://sports.abs-cbn.com/generalsports/news/2017/07/21/phi-withdraws-hosting-2019-southeast-asian-games-28868 |archive-date=23 July 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref> Additionally, it was later reported that the [[Philippine Olympic Committee]]'s insistence on handling all aspects of the hosting, including finances, security, and the organisation of the Games, as it had done for the [[2005 SEA Games]], contributed to the PSC's decision to withdraw its support.<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/sports/2017/07/22/fernandez-says-poc-forced-psc-back-out-seag-554390 |title=Fernandez says POC forced PSC to back out of SEAG |date=22 July 2017 |work=[[SunStar Cebu]] |access-date=22 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170725204042/http://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/sports/2017/07/22/fernandez-says-poc-forced-psc-back-out-seag-554390 |archive-date=25 July 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

On 10 August 2017, Charouck Arirachakaran, the vice-president and secretary-general of the [[National Olympic Committee of Thailand]], stated that Thai Prime Minister [[Prayut Chan-o-cha]] had agreed to a replacement plan for the 2019 SEA Games following the Philippines' withdrawal. The potential host provinces for the event were Chiang Mai, Chonburi, and [[Songkhla province|Songkhla]].<ref name="Thailand ready">{{cite news | url=https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/sports/2017/08/08/thailand-willing-to-replace-philippines-as-2019-sea-games-host | title=Thailand willing to replace as SEA Games 2019 host | work=[[Free Malaysia Today]] | date=8 August 2017 | access-date=26 August 2017 | archive-date=25 April 2019 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190425170345/https://www.today.com/category/sports/2017/08/08/thailand-willing-to-replace-philippines-as-2019-sea-games-host/ | url-status=dead }}</ref>

However, six days later, Philippine Olympic Committee President [[Peping Cojuangco]] confirmed that the Philippines would still host the 2019 SEA Games. This decision came after Cojuangco wrote to Philippine President [[Rodrigo Duterte]], appealing for reconsideration of the country's earlier withdrawal.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://sports.mb.com.ph/2017/08/17/ph-to-host-2019-sea-games/|title=PH to host 2019 SEA Games|last=Giongco|first=Nick|date=17 August 2017|newspaper=[[Manila Bulletin]]|language=en|access-date=28 August 2017|archive-date=24 July 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190724190120/https://sports.mb.com.ph/2017/08/17/ph-to-host-2019-sea-games/|url-status=dead}}</ref>

====2025 confirmation====
In December 2021, Thailand was nominated as the host country for the 2025 SEA Games during the SEAGF Council and Executive Board Meetings held in [[Hanoi]], [[Vietnam]].<ref name="host1">{{Cite press release |url=https://ocasia.org/news/2548-dates-confirmed-for-31st-sea-games-in-vietnam.html|title=Dates confirmed for 31st SEA Games in Vietnam |website=[[Olympic Council of Asia]] |date=7 December 2021 |access-date=9 May 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220217060412/https://ocasia.org/news/2548-dates-confirmed-for-31st-sea-games-in-vietnam.html |archive-date=17 February 2022 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref name="host2">{{Cite web |url=https://harakahdaily.net/index.php/2022/01/11/Malaysia-keen-to-host-2027-sea-games/ |title=Malaysia keen to host 2027 SEA Games |website=[[Harakah Daily]] |date=11 July 2022 |access-date=9 May 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220111112718/https://harakahdaily.net/index.php/2022/01/11/Malaysia-keen-to-host-2027-sea-games/|archive-date=11 January 2022|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/worldsport/2261957|script-title=th:มนตรีซีเกมส์ลงมติให้ไทย เป็นเจ้าภาพอีกครั้งในปี 2025 คาดจัดที่ กทม.|trans-title=The SEA Games Council has voted to make Thailand the host once again in 2025, with the event expected to be held in Bangkok.|website=[[Thairath]]|date=10 December 2021 |access-date=20 December 2022 |language=th |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220127050914/https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/worldsport/2261957 |archive-date=27 January 2022 |url-status=live}}</ref> Five months later, the SEAGF Council and Executive Board officially confirmed Thailand as the host for the 2025 SEA Games, with Bangkok initially nominated as the host city. Additionally, [[Malaysia]] and [[Singapore]] were confirmed as the host countries for the [[2027 SEA Games|2027]] and [[2029 SEA Games|2029]] editions of the SEA Games, respectively.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/1123141/sea-games-2025-2027-2029-hosts|title=Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore confirmed as hosts of future SEA Games|website=[[Inside the Games]]|date=14 May 2022 |access-date=20 December 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220514113902/https://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/1123141/sea-games-2025-2027-2029-hosts |archive-date=14 May 2022 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/others/2445100|script-title=th:ไทยจัดปี 2025 "สหพันธ์กีฬาซีเกมส์" เผยคิวเจ้าภาพมหกรรม "ซีเกมส์" 6 ครั้งรวด|trans-title=Thailand will host the 2025 SEA Games. The Southeast Asian Games Federation (SEAGF) has announced the schedule for the SEA Games host countries, marking Thailand as the host for six consecutive editions.|website=[[Thairath]]|date=15 July 2022 |access-date=20 December 2022 |language=th |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220715030452/https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/others/2445100 |archive-date=15 July 2022 |url-status=live}}</ref>

This marked the seventh time that Thailand had hosted the SEA Games. The capital city, Bangkok, hosted the inaugural SEAP Games—the original term for the SEA Games—in [[1959 SEAP Games|1959]] and subsequently in [[1967 SEAP Games|1967]] and [[1975 SEAP Games|1975]], as well as in [[1985 SEA Games|1985]] after the event had become known as the SEA Games. The [[1995 SEA Games|1995]] and [[2007 SEA Games|2007]] editions were held in the Thai provinces of Chiang Mai and [[Nakhon Ratchasima province|Nakhon Ratchasima]], respectively.

On 11 October 2022, Chaipak Siriwat, the vice-president of the National Olympic Committee of Thailand, revealed the framework for the bidding process for the 2025 SEA Games. He emphasised that the host cities or provinces should aim to organise the Games at a budget-friendly cost and that there would be no requirement to build new venues. Unlike the [[2019 SEA Games|2019]] and [[2021 SEA Games|2021 editions]], in which competition venues were spread across 23 and 12 cities, respectively, the number of host cities for the 2025 Games would be limited to three or four.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/others/2523775|script-title=th:"บิ๊กป้อม" ชี้ ซีเกมส์ 2025 ที่ ไทย เป็นเจ้าภาพ จัดแบบประหยัด ไม่สร้างสนามใหม่|trans-title="Big Pom" (Deputy Prime Minister General Prawit Wongsuwon) pointed out that the 2025 SEA Games, which Thailand will host, will be organised in a cost-effective manner, with no new stadiums to be built.|website=[[Thairath]]|date=11 October 2022|access-date=21 December 2022|language=th |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221011043535/https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/others/2523775 |archive-date=11 October 2022 |url-status=live}}</ref>

====Host city contract====
During the first SEAGF Council meeting for the 2025 SEA Games in June 2024, the host city contract was signed by [[Chadchart Sittipunt]], the governor of Bangkok; Thawatchai Srithong, the governor of Chonburi province; and Somnuek Promkhieo, the governor of Songkhla province.<ref>{{Cite press release |url=https://pr-bangkok.com/?p=339493|title=Bangkok, Chonburi, and Songkhla are ready to host. 33rd SEA Games, 9-20 Dec. 2025|website=[[Bangkok Metropolitan Administration]] |date=16 June 2024|access-date=26 December 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241226175009/https://pr-bangkok.com/?p=339493|archive-date=26 December 2024|url-status=live}}</ref> The ceremony followed the model of the [[Olympic Games|Olympic]] and [[Asian Games]] host city agreements, marking the first time a SEA Games host city contract had been signed.<ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.matichon.co.th/sport/news_4627260|script-title=th:ไทยลงนามเจ้าภาพซีเกมส์ 2025 ประกาศยกระดับมาตรฐานเท่าเอเชี่ยนเกมส์|trans-title=Thailand signs as the host of the 2025 SEA Games, announcing the elevation of standards to match those of the Asian Games.|website=[[Matichon]]|date=14 June 2024|access-date=26 December 2024|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241226175740/https://www.matichon.co.th/sport/news_4627260?__cf_chl_rt_tk=Ef0GfygtpZ.kYP8G7BRdjFzERodnh.JwrbXYdGdAqP0-1735235860-1.0.1.1-gh86jRSejuF56Kz9L9uhDmoL5fDEjpMeLX_iavqc0zU|archive-date=26 December 2024|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Venues===
The 2025 SEA Games utilised existing venues across the [[Bangkok Metropolitan Region]] and [[Chonburi province]], including those used for the [[1985 SEA Games]], the [[1998 Asian Games]], and the [[2007 Summer Universiade]], all of which had previously been held in the Bangkok Metropolitan Region.

The Bangkok Metropolitan Region, which includes the city of Bangkok and the neighbouring provinces of [[Nakhon Pathom province|Nakhon Pathom]], [[Nonthaburi province|Nonthaburi]], [[Pathum Thani province|Pathum Thani]], [[Samut Prakan province|Samut Prakan]], and [[Samut Sakhon province|Samut Sakhon]], hosted the majority of the 42 sports. Meanwhile, Chonburi hosted 15 sports on its own. Only three events took place at outlying venues: the men's football preliminaries held in [[Chiang Mai province|Chiang Mai]], skeet shooting in [[Ratchaburi province|Ratchaburi]],<ref name="venues1">{{Cite web|url=https://www.khaosod.co.th/sports/news_9642232|script-title=th:เคาะซีเกมส์ กทม. รับจัด 31 กีฬา-ชลบุรี 15 - บอลชายรอบแรกใช้ สงขลา-เชียงใหม่|trans-title=Bangkok to Host 31 Sports, Chonburi 15 - Men's Football Group Stage in Songkhla & Chiang Mai|website=[[Khaosod]]|date=20 February 2025|access-date=20 February 2025|language=th|archive-date=20 February 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250220074334/https://www.khaosod.co.th/sports/news_9642232?__cf_chl_rt_tk=erkRtSvLoFbgLcctd9ma8L6Al._HW5D9JbcO.PzwfxU-1740037414-1.0.1.1-J3zLeizNkW8cmeaDvT0dvQOfYlC4JokqtasMzb7lCa4|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.siamsport.co.th/other-sports/sports-world/74691/|script-title=th:สังเวียนชิงชัยซีเกมส์ลงตัว กทม.เมืองหลักจัดแข่ง 31 ชนิดกีฬา|trans-title=SEA Games Venues Finalised: Bangkok as Main Host with 31 Sports|website=Siamsport|date=20 February 2025|access-date=20 February 2025|language=th|archive-date=20 February 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250220075340/https://www.siamsport.co.th/other-sports/sports-world/74691/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="venues2">{{Cite web|url=https://www.matichon.co.th/sport/sportscoop/news_5060195|script-title=th:ส่องสนามแข่งซีเกมส์2025 กทม.-ชลบุรี-สงขลาจัดกีฬาไหนบ้าง?|trans-title=Exploring the 2025 SEA Games Venues: Which Sports Will Be Hosted in Bangkok, Chonburi, and Songkhla?|website=[[Matichon]]|date=22 February 2025|access-date=22 February 2025|language=th|archive-date=22 February 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250222051031/https://www.matichon.co.th/web/20250222051031/https://www.matichon.co.th/sport/sportscoop/news_5060195|url-status=live}}</ref> and road bicycle racing in [[Nakhon Nayok province|Nakhon Nayok]] and [[Prachinburi Province|Prachinburi]].

Originally, Songkhla province was intended to host 10 events;<ref name="venues2" /> however, flooding caused by [[Cyclone Senyar]] in late November 2025 led to the relocation of those events to the remaining venues. It was also reported that Thailand would consider how to compensate the participating nations, as some competitors had already booked flights and accommodation in Songkhla.<ref name="movedevents">{{Cite web|url=https://www.bangkokpost.com/sports/3144957/songkhla-sea-games-events-moved-to-bangkok|title=Songkhla SEA Games events moved to Bangkok|website=[[Bangkok Post]]|date=26 November 2025|access-date=14 December 2025|archive-date=22 December 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251222052358/https://www.bangkokpost.com/sports/3144957/songkhla-sea-games-events-moved-to-bangkok|url-status=live}}</ref> The original venues for events planned for Songkhla, as well as for other relocated events, are identified in the footnotes for this section.

===Budget===
The initial estimated budget for the 2025 SEA Games and the [[2025 ASEAN Para Games]] was ฿2.055 billion, with ฿1.683 billion requested from the [[government budget]].'''<ref>{{Cite web |date=28 November 2024 |script-title=th:ไทยเทงบ 2,055 ล้าน จัดกีฬาซีเกมส์-อาเซียนพาราเกมส์ |trans-title=Thailand allocates a budget of 2.055 billion baht to host the SEA Games and ASEAN Para Games. |url=https://www.siamsport.co.th/other-sports/sports-world/68035/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241227140308/https://www.siamsport.co.th/other-sports/sports-world/68035/ |archive-date=27 December 2024 |access-date=27 December 2024 |website=Siamsport |language=th}}</ref>''' The budget for these events was lower than the cost of the [[1998 Asian Games]], which was ฿2.372 billion, but higher than the cost of the [[2007 Summer Universiade]], which amounted to ฿1.843 billion. Meanwhile, the combined cost of the [[2007 SEA Games]] and the [[2008 ASEAN Para Games]] was ฿1.463 billion.

====Charter and Rules amendment====
Over the years, the SEA Games programme has faced criticism for being a "gold-medal mine" for host countries. Hosts have had the freedom to exclude [[Olympic sports|Olympic]] and [[Asian Games sports]] that are disadvantageous to them while including non-Olympic sports or events that increase their medal chances. Section 34 of the amended SEAGF Charter and Rules stipulates that, starting with the 2025 edition, the SEA Games programme will include between 36 and 41 sports. This will consist of 12 compulsory sports under Category I, which are included in both the Olympic and Asian Games, a minimum of 25 Olympic and Asian Games sports under Category II, and a maximum of four additional sports under Category III. The medal events in Categories I and II must align with the Olympic and Asian Games programmes, while Category III sports cannot have more than eight events, which must be selected from a list established a few years in advance.<ref name="Amendment1"/><ref name="Amendment2"/><ref name="Amendment3"/><ref>{{Cite news|url=https://sports.inquirer.net/510667/bambol-assurance-drastic-reduction-of-indigenous-games-in-next-seag-calendar|title=Bambol assurance: Drastic reduction of indigenous games in next SEA Games calendar|newspaper=[[Philippine Daily Inquirer]]|date=17 May 2023|access-date=17 May 2023|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230517102639/https://sports.inquirer.net/510667/bambol-assurance-drastic-reduction-of-indigenous-games-in-next-seag-calendar|archive-date=17 May 2023|url-status=live}}</ref>

During the third SEAGF Council meeting for the 2025 SEA Games in December 2024, the organising committee replaced ten medal events from the finalist list announced in October 2024 with new events across various sports, including dragon boat, rhythmic gymnastics, judo, esports, pencak silat, and woodball. Additionally, one medal event was removed from mountain biking, while six new medal events were introduced in athletics, road cycling, and sailing. As a result, the total number of events increased to 574, still falling short of the previous record of 580 set at the [[2023 SEA Games]] in [[Phnom Penh]], [[Cambodia]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://stadiumastro.com/sukan-lain/sukan-sea-thailand-tuan-rumah-tambah-16-acara-jumlah-sukan-jauh-lebih-tinggi-berbanding-kemboja-2023-279742|title=Sukan SEA Thailand: Tuan rumah tambah 16 acara, jumlah sukan jauh lebih tinggi berbanding Kemboja 2023|website=[[Astro Arena (TV channel)]]|date=28 December 2024|access-date=28 December 2024|language=ms|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241228144300/https://stadiumastro.com/sukan-lain/sukan-sea-thailand-tuan-rumah-tambah-16-acara-jumlah-sukan-jauh-lebih-tinggi-berbanding-kemboja-2023-279742|archive-date=28 December 2024|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://thesun.my/sport/2025-sea-games-event-count-raised-to-585-says-ocm-KA13464439|title=2025 SEA Games: Event count raised to 585, says OCM|website=[[The Sun (Malaysia)]]|date=28 December 2024|access-date=28 December 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241228144410/https://thesun.my/sport/2025-sea-games-event-count-raised-to-585-says-ocm-KA13464439|archive-date=28 December 2024|url-status=live}}</ref> One medal event in sailing was later cancelled, bringing the final number of events to 573.<ref>{{cite web |title=Thailand Concludes the 33rd SEA Games in Grand Style, Reaffirming Its Position as the 14-Time Overall Champion |url=https://www.seagames2025.org/news/details/Thailand-Concludes-the-33rd-S:-935 |website=seagames2025.org |access-date=24 December 2025 |language=en |date=20 December 2025}}</ref>

During the first SEAGF coordination committee meeting for the 2025 SEA Games in February 2025, the organising committee approved the inclusion of [[mixed martial arts]] (MMA) in the Games, following a request from the SEA Games Federation. It was added under a new category described as value creation sports. This decision arose from an agreement among Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore—the hosts of upcoming SEA Games—which mandates that only sports featured in the current edition can be included in future events. Therefore, MMA was introduced to secure its eligibility for future competitions. However, it will not contribute to the official medal tally, as it is designated as a demonstration sport.<ref name="YOG">{{Cite web|url=https://www.matichon.co.th/sport/thai-sport/news_5067206|script-title=th:ซีเกมส์บรรจุ MMA เพิ่มแต่ไม่นับเหรียญ คุณหญิงปัทมาแนะใช้ซีเกมส์เสนอยูธโอลิมปิกเกมส์|trans-title=The SEA Games include MMA but do not count it towards the medal tally; Khun Ying Patama suggests using the SEA Games to propose it for the Youth Olympic Games.|website=[[Matichon]]|date=26 February 2025|access-date=26 February 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250226114720/https://www.matichon.co.th/sport/thai-sport/news_5067206?__cf_chl_rt_tk=661tHZlCFjkn0o8VFAo3HL6qKaRl2RxBQkfOL08CIFw-1740570440-1.0.1.1-mMGLKX2pSwy1zfz897vfJ9N9F36PkGlmm3bRaVV.yUI|archive-date=26 February 2025|url-status=live}}</ref>

====Sports programme====
The fifty-sport programme of the 2025 SEA Games surprised the [[Olympic Council of Asia]] (OCA) by including the most [[List of international sports federations|internationally recognised sports]], with 49 out of the 50 sports also contested in the [[Olympic Games]], [[Asian Games]], and [[Asian Indoor and Martial Arts Games]]. This marked a historic first for the SEA Games.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://asiahockey.org/news/asean-nations-impressed-with-thailand-preparing-for-historic-sea-games/|title=ASEAN Nations Impressed with Thailand, Preparing for Historic SEA Games|website=[[Asian Hockey]]|date=27 February 2025|access-date=4 March 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250304073110/https://asiahockey.org/news/asean-nations-impressed-with-thailand-preparing-for-historic-sea-games/|archive-date=4 March 2025|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="OCA">{{Cite web|url=https://www.siamsport.co.th/other-sports/sports-world/75377/|script-title=th:โอซีเอทึ่งซีเกมส์ที่ไทยบรรจุกีฬาสากลครบ 37 ชนิด|trans-title=OCA amazed as Thailand's SEA Games includes all 37 international sports.|website=Siamsport|date=1 March 2025|access-date=4 March 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250304072943/https://www.siamsport.co.th/other-sports/sports-world/75377/|archive-date=4 March 2025|url-status=live}}</ref>

[[Surfing]] was the only core [[Olympic sports|Summer Olympic sport]] to not be included in these Games. Canoeing and rowing, as well as skateboarding and sport climbing, were combined into single categories, reducing the total count to twenty-eight sports. Additionally, two Winter Olympic sports were featured: ice hockey and ice skating, with figure skating and short-track speed skating categorised together. Furthermore, three optional sports for the [[2028 Summer Olympics]]—baseball/softball, cricket, and squash—were also included, bringing the total number of Olympic sports in these Games to thirty-three.

Moreover, six sports included in the programme of the [[2026 Asian Games]]—esports, jujitsu, kabaddi, karate, sepak takraw, and wushu—were added. In addition, twelve sports that were originally planned for the cancelled [[2021 Asian Indoor and Martial Arts Games]] were also incorporated, including billiards/snooker, bowling, chess, floorball, futsal, indoor hockey, kickboxing, muaythai, netball, pencak silat, pétanque, and teqball. However, futsal and indoor hockey were categorised under their respective parent sports of football and hockey, reducing the count of these additional sports to ten.

Ten sports—aquathlon, baseball5, chinlone, dragon boat, duathlon, hockey5s, jet skiing, practical shooting, water skiing/wakeboarding, and woodball—were not included in the Olympic and Asian Games, nor the cancelled Asian Indoor and Martial Arts Games, but were featured in the upcoming [[World Games]] and [[Youth Olympic Games]], as well as in past Asian Games and SEA Games. Baseball5 and hockey5s are categorised under their parent sports, baseball and hockey, respectively, while aquathlon and duathlon are categorised under triathlon. Dragon boat, jet skiing and water skiing/wakeboarding, chinlone, and practical shooting fall under related sports such as canoeing and rowing, extreme sports, sepak takraw, and shooting, respectively, while woodball is the only independent sport included.

''Numbers in parentheses indicate the number of medal events contested in each discipline.''

===Athlete eligibility rules===
During the Chef de Mission meeting in August 2025, the Organizing Committee confirmed that [[Sex verification in sports|gender verification tests]] would be conducted for athletes. The procedures and methods for testing are determined by each individual sports federation, as the SEAGF operate independently and are not governed by the [[Olympic Council of Asia]] (OCA). All regulations regarding testing are set by the Organizing Committee.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.siamsport.co.th/seagames/87941/|script-title=th:กกท. ยืนยัน! ซีเกมส์ครั้งที่ 33 พร้อมตรวจเพศ "นักกีฬาหญิง LGBTQ+" หากมีความจำเป็น
|trans-title=SAT Confirms: 33rd SEA Games Will Conduct Gender Verification for LGBTQ+ Female Athletes If Necessary.|website=Siamsport|date=15 August 2025|access-date=29 September 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250929121539/https://www.siamsport.co.th/seagames/87941/|archive-date=29 September 2025|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/others/2877753|script-title=th:"ซีเกมส์ 2025" คุมเข้ม! ยันมีตรวจเพศแน่ เผยมาตรการป้องกันนักกีฬาต่างชาติมาแข่งแทน|trans-title=2025 SEA Games to Enforce Strict Measures: Gender Verification Confirmed and Safeguards Against Foreign Athletes Competing on Behalf of Others.|website=[[Thai Rath]]|date=20 August 2025|access-date=29 September 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250929121344/https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/others/2877753|archive-date=29 September 2025|url-status=live}}</ref>

In addition, strict passport verification was implemented for all athletes. This came in response to incidents at the [[2023 SEA Games]], where some [[Cambodian nationality law|Cambodian naturalized]] athletes competed using documents other than their national passports, with certificates issued by their respective nations allowing participation.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://tiebreakertimes.com.ph/tbt/sea-games-adopts-passport-only-requirement-but-with-special-conditions/345517|title=The 33rd Southeast Asian Games in Thailand will strictly enforce a "passport-only" rule for all events.|website=Tiebreaker Times|date=27 August 2025|access-date=29 September 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250929122744/http://web.archive.org/screenshot/https://tiebreakertimes.com.ph/tbt/sea-games-adopts-passport-only-requirement-but-with-special-conditions/345517|archive-date=29 September 2025|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.philstar.com/sports/2025/09/25/2475182/confusion-sea-games-hoops|title=Confusion in SEA Games hoops?|website=[[The Philippine Star]]|date=25 September 2025|access-date=29 September 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250929122424/https://www.philstar.com/sports/2025/09/25/2475182/confusion-sea-games-hoops|archive-date=29 September 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> For the 2025 Games, athlete documentation was to be thoroughly checked upon entry into Thailand, during the competition, and upon departure. Any cases of document falsification or attempts to field athletes representing countries other than their own would result in immediate action, preventing such violations from occurring during this edition of the Games.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.khaosod.co.th/sports/news_9901996|script-title=th:ซีเกมส์ 2025 ตรวจพาสปอร์ตเข้มงวด กันดึงต่างชาติล่าเหรียญแทน|trans-title=2025 SEA Games to Enforce Strict Passport Checks to Prevent Foreign Athletes from Competing for Other Countries.|website=[[Khaosod]]|date=20 August 2025|access-date=29 September 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250929122215/https://www.khaosod.co.th/sports/news_9901996|archive-date=29 September 2025|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.nationthailand.com/news/sport/40054301|title=Thai Hosts Tighten Rules for 2025 SEA Games|website=[[The Nation (Thailand)]]|date=21 September 2025|access-date=29 September 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250929122949/https://www.nationthailand.com/news/sport/40054301|archive-date=29 September 2025|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Motto ===
The official motto for the 2025 SEA Games, ''Ever Forward'' ({{langx|th|ก้าวไปข้างหน้าไม่หยุดยั้ง}}, {{RTGS|''Kaopai Khangna Mai Yutyang''}}, {{IPA|th|kâːwː.pāj kʰâːŋ.nâː mâj jùt.jáŋ|pron}}), replaced the previous winning slogan from a design competition, ''Amity of ASEAN'', designed by Phonchit Sachaiyan.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://thainews.prd.go.th/regionnews/news/view/124742/?bid=1|script-title=th:จังหวัดสงขลา ยืนยันความพร้อมการเป็นเจ้าภาพร่วมจัดการแข่งขันกีฬาซีเกมส์ ครั้งที่ 33 ในปี 2568 ร่วมเปิดสำนักเลขาธิการการจัดการแข่งขันกีฬาซีเกมส์ สร้างการรับรู้ การมีส่วนร่วม|trans-title=The Songkhla province has confirmed its readiness to co-host the 33rd Southeast Asian Games in 2025. The province has also launched the Secretariat Office for the Games' organisation to raise awareness and promote participation. |website=[[National Broadcasting Services of Thailand]]|date=28 February 2024|access-date=26 December 2024|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241226142625/https://thainews.prd.go.th/regionnews/news/view/124742/?bid=1|archive-date=26 December 2024|url-status=live}}</ref> Notably, it bears a resemblance to the motto of the [[Asian Games]], ''Ever Onward'', which was established by [[Guru Dutt Sondhi]], the founder of the games.

=== Mascots ===
[[File:2025 SEA Games mascot.svg|thumb|300px|The San (from left to right: Put, Suk, Angkan, Chan, Arthit), the official mascots of the Games.]]

The official mascots for the 2025 SEA Games, designed by Teeranop Wangsillapakun, the creative director and founder of TNOP Design, were commissioned by the Design Subcommittee of the Thailand Creative Culture Agency (THACCA) under the [[National Soft Power Strategy Committee]].<ref name="Soft Power"/><ref name="TNOP"/>

The mascots consisted of a group of five characters called ''The San'', in red, green, pink, yellow, and blue, representing the diversity of Thai society. Each character was designed using geometric patterns inspired by the structural lines of traditional Thai motifs, blending modernity with Thai identity. Together, they symbolized celebration, creativity, cultural connection, and the promotion of diversity in sports and society.<ref name="New Logo 1">{{Cite web |date=27 January 2025 |script-title=th:"ซีเกมส์ 2025" ปรับโลโก้-มาสคอตใหม่ "อาเซียนพาราเกมส์" เคาะแล้ว 19 ชนิดกีฬา |trans-title="SEA Games 2025" unveils a new logo and mascot, while the "ASEAN Para Games" finalises 19 sports. |url=https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/4333188/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250131075115/https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/4333188/ |archive-date=31 January 2025 |access-date=31 January 2025 |website=[[Daily News (Thailand)|Daily News]] |language=th}}</ref><ref name="New Logo 2">{{Cite web |date=27 January 2025 |script-title=th:ปรับโลโก้-แมสคอต ซีเกมส์ใหม่ เพิ่ม 5 การแข่งขัน ชิงชัยรวม 574 ทอง |trans-title=The logo and mascot for the SEA Games have been updated, with the addition of 5 new events, bringing the total number of gold medals up to 574. |url=https://www.prachachat.net/spinoff/sport/news-1742689 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250131081043/https://www.prachachat.net/spinoff/sport/news-1742689?__cf_chl_rt_tk=gT335Q1dhcxlAID.QzjhyEw6BxnO2luJlkHsKpxW8vw-1738311043-1.0.1.1-16KF06BlJV4bq1eKw.dMwBmUAZsxvk6ePxlqOPgKung |archive-date=31 January 2025 |access-date=31 January 2025 |website=Prachachat |language=th}}</ref><ref name="New Logo 3">{{Cite web |date=6 February 2025 |title=Thailand reveals 33rd SEA Games logo |url=https://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/1151669/thailand-reveals-33rd-sea-games-logo |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250206233204/https://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/1151669/thailand-reveals-33rd-sea-games-logo |archive-date=6 February 2025 |access-date=6 February 2025 |website=Inside the Games}}</ref>

On 7 October 2025, a new version of The San was revealed; the number of mascots was reduced to one, with Chan being retained. It was also recolored to the [[Flag of Thailand|Thai flag]] to represent national pride. The original five San designs were retained for merchandise.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.seagames2025.org/news/details/SAT-Governor-Unveils-New-Vers:-459|title=SAT Governor Unveils New Version of "The San" SEA Games Mascot, Showcasing Thai Identity Through National Colors|date=7 October 2025|archive-date=31 October 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251031022536/https://www.seagames2025.org/news/details/SAT-Governor-Unveils-New-Vers:-459|url-status=live}}</ref>

These mascots replaced an earlier design selected through a competition. The initial mascot, named ''Mawin'', was designed by Tawich Jitthiang and depicted a ''Waree Kunchorn'', a mythical creature from the [[Himavanta]]. This creature has the body of an [[elephant]], along with [[fish]]-like features, including fins along its backbone, leg fins attached to each of its four legs, and a fishtail.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Waree Kunchorn |website=Himmapan |url=https://www.himmapan.com/himmapan_elephant_wareekunchorn.html |accessdate=18 April 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240530054433/https://www.himmapan.com/himmapan_elephant_wareekunchorn.html |archive-date=30 May 2024 |url-status=live}}</ref>

The official theme song of the Games was "1%", performed by [[Violette Wautier]] alongside [[F.Hero]] in the Thai version and TWOPEE in the English version.

== Concerns and controversies ==
{{Criticism section|date=March 2026}}
=== Officiating ===
Numerous allegations of biased officiating and missed calls were reported in multiple sports. In [[Pencak silat at the 2025 SEA Games|pencak silat]], one of Malaysia's coaches assaulted a judge after Nor Farah binti Mazlan lost to Thailand's [[Chongthima Rueanthong]] in the quarterfinals of the women's Class B tanding event due to a tiebreaker rule, as both competitors ended with a 60–60 tie. However, the winner was decided due to the former's number of fouls. According to the Philippine Olympic Committee, the Filipino judge was safe after the assault and had filed a formal protest to the organizing committee.<ref>{{cite news |title=SEA Games 2025: Malaysian silat coach accused of striking Philippine judge after disputing result|url=https://www.straitstimes.com/sport/malaysian-martial-art-coach-accused-of-striking-sea-games-judge |access-date=18 December 2025 |work=The Straits Times |date=17 December 2025|archive-date=22 December 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251222052012/https://www.straitstimes.com/sport/malaysian-martial-art-coach-accused-of-striking-sea-games-judge|url-status=live}}</ref>

The [[Association of Boxing Alliances in the Philippines]] (ABAP) noted multiple "hometown decisions" in favor of Thai boxers and disputed losses of Filipino and Filipina athletes. One notable example was the bout between Indonesia's Hasanah Huswatan and the Philippines' [[Nesthy Petecio]] in the semifinals of the women's 63&nbsp;kg category, where Hasanah won in a 3–2 split decision that surprised both Thai and Filipino officials. ABAP also flagged Thailand's representation in the finals of 16 out of the 17 categories, which they described as "disproportionate."<ref>{{cite news |title=Ricky Vargas raises alarm over Thai advantage in SEA Games boxing|url=https://tiebreakertimes.com.ph/tbt/ricky-vargas-raises-alarm-over-thai-advantage-in-sea-games-boxing/360932 |access-date=18 December 2025 |work=Tiebreaker Times |date=16 December 2025|archive-date=22 December 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251222052933/https://tiebreakertimes.com.ph/tbt/ricky-vargas-raises-alarm-over-thai-advantage-in-sea-games-boxing/360932|url-status=live}}</ref> Thailand went on to win 14 gold medals in the sport. After the boxing event, ABAP revealed that they plan to have an inquiry with [[World Boxing]], the sport's international federation on the irregularities during the tournament, which also affected other countries such as Singapore and Vietnam.<ref>{{cite news |last=Naredo|first=Camille|title=ABAP plans to ask for inquiry over 'irregularities' in SEA Games boxing|url=https://www.abs-cbn.com/sports/othersports/2025/12/20/abap-plans-to-ask-for-inquiry-over-irregularities-in-sea-games-boxing-1505 |access-date=21 December 2025 |work=ABS-CBN News |date=20 December 2025|archive-date=22 December 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251222052948/https://www.abs-cbn.com/sports/othersports/2025/12/20/abap-plans-to-ask-for-inquiry-over-irregularities-in-sea-games-boxing-1505|url-status=live}}</ref>

In the [[Football at the 2025 SEA Games – Women's tournament|women's football]] final between Vietnam and the Philippines, the referee ruled out a goal by [[Nguyễn Thị Bích Thùy]] in the 29th minute for [[Offside (association football)|offside]], despite claims of the attempt being clearly onside based on multiple replays, with some even showing up to three players not counting the goalkeeper standing below Bích Thùy.<ref>{{cite news |title=Philippines women's football team win historic Southeast Asian Games gold medal|url=https://www.espn.ph/football/story/_/id/47335355/philippines-womens-football-team-win-historic-southeast-asian-games-gold-medal |access-date=18 December 2025 |work=ESPN Philippines |date=17 December 2025|archive-date=22 December 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251222053443/https://www.espn.ph/football/story/_/id/47335355/philippines-womens-football-team-win-historic-southeast-asian-games-gold-medal|url-status=live}}</ref> Similarly, the [[Football at the 2025 SEA Games – Men's tournament#Bronze medal match|men's football bronze medal match]] saw [[Faris Danish]] of Malaysia commit a handball inside the penalty area that was not called by the referee, denying the Philippines a potential penalty. Due to both men's and women's tournaments not having a [[video assistant referee]] (VAR) system, both calls were upheld. The Filipinas would go on to defeat Vietnam on penalties and break their streak of four consecutive women's football gold medals, while Malaysia won bronze in the men's tournament.

=== Naming of muaythai competition ===
A debate arose regarding the naming of the combat sport, concerning the use of the term "muay" versus "muaythai".<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/fightsport/muaythai/2866495|script-title=th:“ฝ่ายจัด” ตอบแล้ว ปม “ซีเกมส์ 2025 ที่ไทย” ใช้ชื่อ “มวย” ไม่ใช้ “มวยไทย”
|trans-title=Organizers Respond to Controversy Over Using "Muay" Instead of "Muaythai" at SEA Games 2025 in Thailand.|website=[[Thai Rath]]|date=25 June 2025|access-date=2 November 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251102125906/https://www.thairath.co.th/sport/fightsport/muaythai/2866495|archive-date=2 November 2025|url-status=live}}</ref>  The issue gained public attention around August 2025 when Phai Lik, a member of the [[House of Representatives (Thailand)|House of Respresentatives]] from [[Kamphaeng Phet province|Kamphaeng Phet]] and the president of the Kickboxing Association of Thailand (KAT), publicly urged organizers to confirm the use of the name "muaythai". He argued that the name had been recognized by the [[International Olympic Committee]] (IOC) and the [[International Federation of Muaythai Associations]] (IFMA), aligned with the government's soft power policy, and serves as an affirmation of national identity, particularly after Cambodia used the name "kun khmer" during its host year.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.thairath.co.th/news/politic/2878399|script-title=th:“ไผ่ ลิกค์” ชี้ต้องใช้ “มวยไทย” ในกีฬาซีเกมส์ครั้งที่ 33 อย่าให้กัมพูชาบิดเบือน|trans-title="Phai Lik" Insists: "Muaythai" Must Be Used in 33rd SEA Games, Warns Against Cambodian Distortion.|website=[[Thai Rath]]|date=23 August 2025|access-date=2 November 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250927021342/https://www.thairath.co.th/news/politic/2878399|archive-date=27 September 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> 

Subsequently, on September 10, 2025, Atthakorn Sirilatthayakorn, the [[Ministry of Tourism and Sports (Thailand)|Minister of Tourism and Sports]] (MOTS), addressed the issue in the House of Representatives, confirming that the government was proposing the matter to the Southeast Asian Games Federation (SEAGF) for consideration and to advocate for the official use of the term "muaythai".<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.siamsport.co.th/seagames/91882/|script-title=th:รมต.กีฬา ยันปัญหาเปตองเคลียร์จบ ผลักดันใช้ชื่อ 'มวยไทย' ในซีเกมส์|trans-title=Sports Minister Confirms Petanque Issue Resolved, Pushes for 'Muaythai' in SEA Games.|website=Siamsport|date=10 September 2025|access-date=2 November 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251102125424/https://www.siamsport.co.th/seagames/91882/|archive-date=2 November 2025|url-status=live}}</ref> 

However, in early October 2025, following reports that the [[Sports Authority of Thailand]] (SAT) was preparing to propose the name change, Chaiyapak Siriwat, CEO of the SEAGF, clarified on October 5, 2025, that the sport would retain the name "muay". He stated this name is stipulated in the SEA Games charter and cannot be altered, although he affirmed that the competition's operations and judging would be managed by IFMA and Thai officials.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.matichon.co.th/sport/boxing/news_5397896|script-title=th:บิ๊กแน๊ต ยันใช้ชื่อ ‘มวย’ ในศึกซีเกมส์ 2025 เฟ้นสุดยอดนักชกชิงชัย 16 ทอง|trans-title='Big Nat' Confirms 'Muay' for SEA Games 2025; Top Fighters to Compete for 16 Gold.|website=[[Matichon]]|date=5 October 2025|access-date=2 November 2025|language=th|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251012020204/https://www.matichon.co.th/sport/boxing/news_5397896|archive-date=12 October 2025|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Basketball player eligibility===
[[Philippine Olympic Committee]] president [[Bambol Tolentino|Abraham Tolentino]] was frustrated on the organizers' inability to finalize the eligibility rules on the basketball tournament as of 28 November 2025, derailing the preparations of the Philippine national teams.<ref>{{cite news |title=POC chief fumes at SEAG dilly-dallying on basketball eligibility rules |url=https://www.spin.ph/basketball/fiba/bambol-riled-by-sea-games-delay-on-basketball-eligibility-rules-a795-20251128 |access-date=28 November 2025 |work=Sports Interactive Network Philippines |date=28 November 2025 |archive-date=29 November 2025 |archive-url=https://archive.toda/FyPR1|url-status=live}}</ref>

In August 2025, it was reported that players only needed to have the appropriate passport; however, this was only clarified in November.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Tuazon |first1=Ernest |title=SEA Games adopts 'passport-only' requirement but with special conditions |url=https://tiebreakertimes.com.ph/tbt/sea-games-adopts-passport-only-requirement-but-with-special-conditions/345517 |access-date=25 November 2025 |work=Tiebreaker Times |date=27 August 2025 |archive-date=29 September 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250929122733/https://tiebreakertimes.com.ph/tbt/sea-games-adopts-passport-only-requirement-but-with-special-conditions/345517 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=sbpairs/> The [[Philippine Basketball Association]] submitted rosters assuming such rules were to be in effect over the standard [[FIBA eligibility rules]].<ref name=sbpairs>{{cite news |last1=Terrado |first1=Jonas |title=SBP airs frustration over SEA Games' eligibility rule |url=https://sports.inquirer.net/649808/sbp-airs-frustration-over-sea-games-eligibility-rule |access-date=25 November 2025 |work=Philippine Daily Inquirer |date=25 November 2025 |archive-date=29 November 2025|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251129000321/https://sports.inquirer.net/649808/sbp-airs-frustration-over-sea-games-eligibility-rule|url-status=live}}</ref>

On 24 November 2025, it was confirmed that naturalized players, alongside any other player who obtained the relevant passport after age 16, were not eligible to play in the standard 5x5 tournaments.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Escarlote |first1=Mark |title=Brownlee out for Gilas in SEAG |url=https://tribune.net.ph/2025/11/23/brownlee-out-for-gilas-in-seag |access-date=24 November 2025 |work=Daily Tribune |date=24 November 2025 |archive-date=29 November 2025|archive-url=https://archive.today/hxQ8Y|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Terrado |first1=Jonas |title=No Justin Brownlee, Ange Kouame for Gilas 5×5 SEA Games squad |url=https://sports.inquirer.net/650414/no-justine-brownlee-kouame-for-gilas-sea-games-squad |access-date=28 November 2025 |work=Philippine Daily Inquirer |date=28 November 2025 |language=en|archive-date=29 November 2025|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251129000539/https://sports.inquirer.net/650414/no-justine-brownlee-ange-kouame-for-gilas-sea-games-squad|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=gilaswomen>{{cite news |last1=Morales |first1=Luisa |title=Gilas Women buck personnel issues following late SEA Games decision on eligibility |url=https://www.onesports.ph/gilas/article/38658/gilas-women-buck-personnel-issues-following-late-sea-games-decision-on-eligibility |access-date=28 November 2025 |work=One Sports |date=28 November 2025 |archive-date=29 November 2025|archive-url=https://archive.today/20251129000618/https://www.onesports.ph/gilas/article/38658/gilas-women-buck-personnel-issues-following-late-sea-games-decision-on-eligibility|url-status=live}}</ref> This is stricter than the FIBA eligibility rules, which allow at least one player satisfying either criterion to be on a roster.<ref name=gilaswomen/>

===eSports cheating incident===
{{main|Arena of Valor SEA Games cheating scandal}}

Naphat Warasin, also known by the [[username|handle]] "Tokyogurl", was caught downloading an "unauthorized third-party software" during a team match between her team Thailand against Vietnam in the women's ''[[Arena of Valor]]'' match and was expelled from the tournament. The Thailand team withdrew from the tournament. A lifetime ban was imposed on Naphat by game publisher [[Garena]] while her talent agency terminated her contract. Thailand lost the game against Vietnam and was supposed to play the semifinals with Laos. Laos gained a bye to the gold medal match losing to Vietnam, while [[Timor-Leste]], having lost every game they played, was awarded a bronze medal instantly.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Alba |first1=Katrina |title=SEA Games: What exactly happened in the Thailand AOV cheating issue? |url=https://www.onesports.ph/sea-games/article/39388/sea-games-what-exactly-happened-in-the-thailand-aov-cheating-issue-1 |access-date=19 December 2025 |work=One Sports |date=17 December 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=Thai esports athlete expelled from SEA Games for cheating during Arena of Valor match |url=https://www.channelnewsasia.com/sport/sea-games-thai-cheating-tokyogurl-naphat-warasin-expelled-arena-valor-video-game-esports-5614876 |access-date=19 December 2025 |work=CNA |date=17 December 2025 |language=en}}</ref>

=== National anthem errors ===
During the first match of the [[Football at the 2025 SEA Games – Men's tournament|men's football]] game between Vietnam and Laos, a technical error prevented the national anthems of both teams from being played as scheduled at [[Rajamangala Stadium]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://e.vnexpress.net/news/sports/football/thailand-apologizes-to-vietnam-laos-for-national-anthem-failure-in-sea-games-match-4989664.html|title=Thailand apologizes to Vietnam, Laos for national anthem failure in SEA Games match|date=3 December 2025|website=VNExpress|archive-date=22 December 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251222053937/https://e.vnexpress.net/news/sports/football/thailand-apologizes-to-vietnam-laos-for-national-anthem-failure-in-sea-games-match-4989664.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

The governor of the [[Sports Authority of Thailand]], Dr. Kongsak Yodmanee, acknowledged full responsibility for the mistake and expressed a “deepest apology” for the incident. The committee also ensured such issues would never happen again throughout the tournament.

=== Flag display errors ===
In the Games' official website, the organizing committee displayed the wrong flags for the futsal competition, where they used the Vietnamese flag to represent Thailand, while the Indonesian flag was replaced by Laos.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cnnindonesia.com/olahraga/20251203113731-142-1302244/indonesia-dan-vietnam-jadi-korban-salah-bendera-di-sea-games-2025/amp|title=Indonesia dan Vietnam Jadi Korban Salah Bendera di Sea Games|trans-title=Indonesia and Vietnam became victims of wrong flags and cosplay-like protests at the 2025 SEA Games!|date=2 December 2025|website=CNN Indonesia|language=id|archive-date=22 December 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251222053804/https://www.cnnindonesia.com/olahraga/20251203113731-142-1302244/indonesia-dan-vietnam-jadi-korban-salah-bendera-di-sea-games-2025/|url-status=live}}</ref>

During the opening ceremony, a video clip with giant Singaporean flag [[card stunt]] was used when introducing the [[1997 SEA Games]] held in Indonesia as part of the opening segment about the history of the SEA Games. While the video in question was taken from the actual footage of the opening ceremony,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dE3l9BlUsOM?t=500|title=Gebyar Nusantara Opening Ceremony - SEA Games XIX - 1997|date=25 October 2017|website=YouTube|language=en}}</ref> it was criticized by Indonesian viewers for misrepresenting the country as the host of the 1997 edition.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Lam |first1=Esther |title=SEA Games: Mix-up sees Singapore flag shown instead of Indonesia's at opening ceremony |url=https://www.asiaone.com/asia/sea-games-mix-sees-singapore-flag-shown-instead-indonesias-opening-ceremony |access-date=22 April 2026 |work=AsiaOne |date=11 December 2025 |language=en}}</ref>
