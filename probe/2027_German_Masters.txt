The '''2027 German Masters''' (officially the '''2027 Machineseeker German Masters''')<ref name="snooker.org"/> is an upcoming professional [[snooker]] tournament that will take place from 25 to 31 January 2027 at the [[Tempodrom]] in [[Berlin]], Germany. Qualifying will take place from 16 to 19 November at the [[Robin Park Centre]] in [[Wigan]], England. The 17th consecutive edition of the [[German Masters]] since it was revived in [[2011 German Masters|2011]], it will be the 12th [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2026–27 snooker season]], following the [[2026 Scottish Open (snooker)|2026 Scottish Open]] and preceding the [[2027 Welsh Open (snooker)|2027 Welsh Open]].

[[Judd Trump]] is the defending champion, having defeated [[Shaun Murphy]] 10–4 in the [[2026 German Masters|2026 final]].

==Overview==
The tournament originated as the German Open, a ranking event that was held from 1995 to 1997. The inaugural German Open champion was [[John Higgins]], who defeated [[Ken Doherty]] 9–3 in the [[1995 German Open (snooker)|1995 final]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=German Open (1995)|url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=1674 |access-date=30 December 2025 |website=snooker.org |language=en |archive-date=4 January 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240104234528/https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=1674 |url-status=live }}</ref> After being staged once in [[1998 German Masters|1998]] as the non-ranking German Masters, the tournament dropped off the calendar; it was restored in 2011 as the ranking [[German Masters]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Poole |first=Joseph |date=27 January 2025 |title=German Masters Snooker: The best moments, tournament history, list of winners |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/german-masters/german-masters-snooker-the-best-moments-tournament-history-list-of-winners-4962511 |access-date=30 December 2025 |website=Totally Snookered |language=en |archive-date=15 July 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250715173648/https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/german-masters/german-masters-snooker-the-best-moments-tournament-history-list-of-winners-4962511 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Nunns |first=Hector |date=3 February 2014 |title=How Germany fell in love with snooker |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/26019104 |access-date=30 December 2025 |work=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-date=15 April 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250415183050/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/26019104 |url-status=live }}</ref> The first winner of the tournament after its revival was [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]], who defeated [[Mark Selby]] 9–7 in the [[2011 German Masters|2011 final]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=6 February 2011 |title=Mark Williams edges past Selby to win German Masters |url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport2/hi/other_sports/snooker/9389058.stm |access-date=2 January 2026 |work=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-date=7 February 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110207051506/http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport2/hi/other_sports/snooker/9389058.stm |url-status=live }}</ref> In 2021, the tournament trophy was named the Brandon Parker Trophy in memory of the late manager, promoter, and [[World Snooker Tour]] director who first brought the tournament to Berlin's [[Tempodrom]] in 2011. Parker had died from cancer in 2020.<ref>{{Cite web|title=German Masters trophy named after Brandon Parker|url=https://wpbsa.com/german-masters-trophy-named-after-brandon-parker/|website=[[World Professional Billiards and Snooker Association]]|date=26 January 2021|access-date=1 February 2026|language=en-GB|first=Matt|last=Huart|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260418183948/https://www.wpbsa.com/german-masters-trophy-named-after-brandon-parker/|archive-date=18 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>
