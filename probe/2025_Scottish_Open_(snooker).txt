The '''2025 Scottish Open''' (officially the '''2025 BetVictor Scottish Open''')<ref name="s.org_M"/> was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 15 to 21 December 2025 at the [[Meadowbank Sports Centre]] in [[Edinburgh]], Scotland. Qualifying took place from 14 to 17 October at the Robin Park Leisure Centre in [[Wigan]], England. The 10th consecutive edition of the tournament since it was revived in [[2016 Scottish Open (snooker)|2016]], it was the 11th [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season]], following the [[2025 Snooker Shoot Out]] and preceding the [[2026 German Masters]]. It was the third of four tournaments in the season's [[Home Nations Series]], following the [[2025 English Open (snooker)|2025 English Open]] and the [[2025 Northern Ireland Open]] and preceding the [[2026 Welsh Open (snooker)|2026 Welsh Open]]. The tournament was broadcast by [[TNT Sports]] in the United Kingdom and Ireland, by [[Eurosport]] in mainland Europe, by local channels in China and elsewhere in Asia, and by [[WST Play]] in all other territories. The winner received £100,000 from a total prize fund of £550,400. 

[[Lei Peifan]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Wu Yize]] 9–5 in the [[2024 Scottish Open (snooker)|2024 final]], but he lost 3–4 to [[Yuan Sijun]] in the last 32. The final was contested between the world number 17 [[Chris Wakelin]] and the world number 70 [[Chang Bingyu]], who reached his maiden ranking final. Wakelin won eight consecutive frames to defeat Chang 9–2, securing his first Scottish Open title and second ranking title. The tournament produced 67 [[Century break|century breaks]], 18 in the qualifiers in Wigan and 49 at the main stage in Edinburgh, of which the highest was a 146 by [[Zhao Xintong]] in his second-round match with [[Joe O'Connor (snooker player)|Joe O'Connor]]. The final was the last professional match officiated by [[Leo Scullion]], who retired from refereeing after 26 years on the professional circuit.

== Overview ==
The tournament originated as the non-ranking [[1981 International Open]], staged at the [[Derby Assembly Rooms|Assembly Rooms]] in [[Derby]], England. [[Steve Davis]] won the event, defeating [[Dennis Taylor]] 9–0 in the final. The tournament became a [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] the following year, the first event after the [[World Snooker Championship]] to gain ranking status. Staged annually under various names (with the exception of the years from 1990–92, when it was not held), the tournament moved to Scotland in 1997 and was first branded as the [[Scottish Open (snooker)|Scottish Open]] in 1998.<ref>{{cite web |last=Turner |first=Chris |title=International Open, Goya Matchroom Trophy |url=http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/int.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120216155037/http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/int.html |archive-date=16 February 2012 |access-date=4 November 2011 |work=cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk |publisher=Chris Turner's Snooker Archive}}</ref> It was discontinued after the 2004 edition, apart from one staging in [[2012 Scottish Open|2012]] as a [[List of minor-ranking tournaments|minor-ranking tournament]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Betfair European Tour Event Five (2012) |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=203 |access-date=2025-09-15 |website=snooker.org |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420074446/https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=203|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> The tournament was restored to the calendar as a full ranking event in 2016 as part of the newly created [[Home Nations Series]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=2015-04-29 |title=World Championship: Snooker tour to be revamped in 2016 |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/32519199 |access-date=2025-09-15 |work=BBC Sport |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420073715/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/32519199|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Marco Fu]] won the [[2016 Scottish Open (snooker)|2016 edition]], recovering from 1–4 behind to beat [[John Higgins]] 9–4 in the final; he was presented with the newly named [[Stephen Hendry]] Trophy by the seven-time World Champion personally.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2016-12-18 |title=Scottish Open: Marco Fu fights back to stun John Higgins in final in Glasgow |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/38357882 |access-date=2025-09-15 |work=BBC Sport |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420073728/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/38357882|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Format ===
The tournament used a tiered format first implemented for the [[Home Nations Series]] in the [[2024–25 snooker season]]. In the first qualifying round, players [[seeding (sports)|seeded]] 65–96 faced those seeded 97 and under, including selected amateurs. In the second qualifying round, the 32 winners from the first qualifying round faced players seeded 33–64. At the last-64 stage, the 32 winners from the second qualifying round faced the top 32 seeds.<ref>{{cite web |date=5 April 2024 |title=Tiered format for Home Nations and German Masters in 2024/25 |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/april/05/tiered-format-for-home-nations-and-german-masters-in-2024-25/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405185554/https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/april/05/tiered-format-for-home-nations-and-german-masters-in-2024-25/ |archive-date=5 April 2024 |access-date=5 April 2024 |work=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref> All matches were played as best of 7 {{cuegloss|frame|frames}} until the quarter-finals, which were the best of 9. The semi-finals were the best of 11, and the final was a best-of-17-frame match played over two {{cuegloss|session|sessions}}.

A number of prominent players were absent from the tournament. [[Judd Trump]], [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]], and [[Ding Junhui]] opted not to enter. [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]], [[Jak Jones]], [[Neil Robertson]], and [[Robert Milkins]] all withdrew prior to the main stage, as a result of which their respective opponents—[[Ben Mertens]], [[Matthew Stevens]], [[He Guoqiang (snooker player)|He Guoqiang]], and [[Zhao Xintong]]—all received [[walkover]]s to the last 32.<ref>{{Cite web |date=15 December 2025 |title=BetVictor Scottish Open withdrawals |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/15/betvictor-scottish-open-withdrawals/ |access-date=17 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420074512/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/15/betvictor-scottish-open-withdrawals/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Haigh |first=Phil |date=15 December 2025 |title=Scottish Open hit with string of withdrawals as big names pull out |url=https://metro.co.uk/2025/12/15/scottish-open-hit-string-withdrawals-big-names-pull-25497552/ |access-date=17 December 2025 |website=[[Metro (British newspaper)|Metro]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420074553/https://metro.co.uk/2025/12/15/scottish-open-hit-string-withdrawals-big-names-pull-25497552/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Round two (last 32) ===
[[File:Zhao Xintong PHC 2016-2.jpg|thumb|The reigning World Champion [[Zhao Xintong]] ''(pictured in 2016)'' made a 146 {{cuegloss|break}} in his second-round match against [[Joe O'Connor (snooker player)|Joe O'Connor]].]]
The second-round matches were played on 17 December.<ref name="s.org_M" /><ref name="TS_draw_etc" /> The defending champion [[Lei Peifan]] led [[Yuan Sijun]] 3–2, but Yuan tied the scores at 3–3 and then won the deciding frame with a century of 126.<ref>{{cite web |date=17 December 2025 |title=2025 Scottish Open: Lei Peifan v Yuan Sijun |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/7b8ed648-b7bb-40ab-bccb-ff00080b034f |access-date=17 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084908/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/7b8ed648-b7bb-40ab-bccb-ff00080b034f|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Noppon Saengkham]] made breaks including 69 and 100 as he whitewashed Higgins, only the 15th time in his career that Higgins had been whitewashed in a [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]]. "It isn't easy to beat [Higgins] in Scotland," said Saengkham afterwards. "This is his home event and there are a lot of fans supporting him."  [[Chris Wakelin]] also whitewashed McGill. After the defeats by Higgins and McGill, no Scottish players remained in the event. Selby advanced with a whitewash victory over [[Hossein Vafaei]], while Allen extended his position as frontrunner for the £150,000 [[Home Nations Series]] bonus as he whitewashed Wang, making breaks of 57, 65, and 109. "Look I'm aware of the bonus, I'm not going to lie," Allen said afterwards. "But ultimately you have to go out there to win matches and win tournaments." In another whitewash victory, [[Zhang Anda]] defeated [[Matthew Stevens]].<ref name="WST_day3">{{cite web |date=17 December 2025 |title=BetVictor Scottish Open day three |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/17/betvictor-scottish-open-day-three/ |access-date=17 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084745/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/17/betvictor-scottish-open-day-three/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

The reigning World Champion [[Zhao Xintong]] made a 146 break in the opening frame of his match with O'Connor. However, O'Connor produced breaks including 97 and 51 as he won four of the next five frames for a 4–2 victory. "I enjoy playing the best in the world," said O'Connor. "I've followed [Zhao's] journey right from the amateurs and I knew he was good then. He was always very nice to watch. He is very smooth and you could see he stood above the rest of the field." Kyren Wilson progressed with a 4–1 victory over [[Pang Junxu]], making a highest break of 106, and Bingham defeated Murphy by the same score. [[Zhou Yuelong]] also advanced, beating [[Xiao Guodong]] 4–2.<ref name="WST_day3" /> [[Chang Bingyu]] made back-to-back centuries of 105 and 110 in the last two frames as he completed a 4–1 win over [[Si Jiahui]].<ref>{{cite web |date=17 December 2025 |title=2025 Scottish Open: Si Jiahui v Chang Bingyu |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/c6ff3eb6-bd40-4678-b13f-925fc20a3775 |access-date=18 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084724/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/c6ff3eb6-bd40-4678-b13f-925fc20a3775|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Round three (last 16) ===
[[File:Mark Selby 2020.jpg|thumb|[[Mark Selby]] ''(pictured in 2020)'' recorded his 12th consecutive professional victory by whitewashing [[Noppon Saengkham]], who failed to pot a ball in the match.]]
The third-round matches were played on 18 December.<ref name="s.org_M" /><ref name="TS_draw_etc" /> Chang made a century of 125 to win the first frame against the world number two Wilson. Wilson tied the scores by winning frame two, but Chang won frames three and four with breaks of 49 and 104 and completed a 4–1 victory on the last pink in frame five. Chang reached the quarter-finals of a ranking event for the first time and also secured his place in the 2026 World Grand Prix. Saengkham failed to pot a ball in his match against Selby, who made breaks of 94, 104, and 71 as he secured a whitewash victory, his 12th consecutive professional win. Selby attempted a [[maximum break]] in frame three but missed the 14th {{cuegloss|red}}.<ref name="s.org_M" /> Saengkham's only four {{cuegloss|points}} in the match came courtesy of a {{Cuegloss|foul}} by Selby in frame three. "The tempo I played in the last two games is how I play when I'm practising," said Selby afterwards. "It is only myself that sometimes stops that. I can get in my own way. I want to be getting on with it but sometimes mentally I stop myself. I seem to have my confidence up now and I fancy making the shots." [[Matthew Selt]] defeated Wu 4–1 to reach his first ranking quarter-final in over a year, and [[Jiang Jun]] beat Un-Nooh by the same score.<ref>{{cite web |date=18 December 2025 |title=BetVictor Scottish Open day four |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/18/betvictor-scottish-open-day-four/ |access-date=18 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084656/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/18/betvictor-scottish-open-day-four/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Day |first=Michael |date=18 December 2025 |title=Mark Selby stretches winning streak as he leaves opponent potless at Scottish Open |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/scottish-open/in-form-mark-selby-completes-perfect-dozen-as-he-leaves-opponent-potless-at-the-2025-scottish-open-5448585 |access-date=18 December 2025 |website=Totally Snookered |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084617/https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/scottish-open/in-form-mark-selby-completes-perfect-dozen-as-he-leaves-opponent-potless-at-the-2025-scottish-open-5448585|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

Allen and Zhang shared the first two frames of their match before Allen moved ahead with a century of 140. Zhang made a 71 to tie the scores at 2–2 at the mid-session interval, but Allen restored the lead with a century of 115 in frame five. Zhang won frame six to force a decider, but Allen secured victory, helped by a 58 break. "Both of us played a lot of good stuff there," said Allen. "I'm happy with the way I scored. There were just one or two mistakes and that was all it took for it to be 3–3. [Zhang] is a great player, so I'm happy to get over the line in any manner." Wakelin made a highest break of 74 in his 4–2 victory over Yuan Sijun; he reached the quarter-finals for a third consecutive year. Zhou defeated Bingham in a deciding frame with a 133 break.<ref>{{cite web |date=18 December 2025 |title=2025 Scottish Open: Stuart Bingham v Zhou Yuelong |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/ce9ee531-85bd-41d1-804e-30ea70a2dad8 |access-date=19 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084615/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/ce9ee531-85bd-41d1-804e-30ea70a2dad8|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="WST_day4_ev">{{cite web |date=18 December 2025 |title=BetVictor Scottish Open day four evening |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/18/betvictor-scottish-open-day-four-evening/ |access-date=19 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084611/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/18/betvictor-scottish-open-day-four-evening/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> O'Connor won the first three frames against [[Zak Surety]], making a century of 100 in the opener. Surety took frame four and then came from behind to win frame five on the last black, but O'Connor completed a 4–2 victory in frame six.<ref name="WST_day4_ev" />

=== Quarter-finals ===
[[File:German Masters 2015-Day 2-Session 1-11 (LezFraniak).jpg|thumb|[[Matthew Selt]] ''(pictured in 2015)'' defeated [[Jiang Jun]]. He credited reaching the semi-finals to a new coaching regimen.]]
The quarter-finals were played on 19 December as the best of nine frames.<ref name="s.org_M" /><ref name="TS_draw_etc" /> Facing Selby, Chang won the first two frames with breaks of 72 and 135, but Selby produced breaks of 92 and 76 to tie the scores at the mid-session interval. Chang took frame five with a 91 break, but Selby made a 67 to take frame six. Chang then made breaks of 95 and 77 to win the match 5–3 and reach his maiden ranking semi-final.<ref>{{cite web |date=19 December 2025 |title=2025 Scottish Open: Chang Bingyu v Mark Selby |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/b4e6e836-2f01-4285-b4a1-474e6e5d7184 |access-date=19 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084726/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/b4e6e836-2f01-4285-b4a1-474e6e5d7184|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> Wakelin made breaks of 102, 75, and 76 as he took a 4–1 lead over Zhou. In frame five, Wakelin was awarded a {{Cuegloss|free ball}} with all 15 reds remaining and had the opportunity to make a break of 153 and then 151 before he missed the ninth red. A [[Maximum break#Breaks exceeding 147|break exceeding 147]] had been achieved only once in professional competition, when [[Jamie Burnett]] made a 148 in the [[2004 UK Championship]] qualifiers. Zhou won frames six and seven with breaks of 99 and 89, but Wakelin secured a 5–3 victory in frame eight.<ref>{{cite web |date=19 December 2025 |title=2025 Scottish Open: Chris Wakelin v Zhou Yuelong |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/de6f476f-69f6-4ed4-b4bf-a70e80815cc3 |access-date=19 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084606/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/de6f476f-69f6-4ed4-b4bf-a70e80815cc3|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> "It was a magic opportunity [in the fifth frame]," Wakelin said afterwards. "I'm gutted I didn't make it. Just speaking to [[Jimmy White]] there and these opportunities come around once or twice in your career. I'm delighted to get to the semis, but that would have been really cool to make a break above 147."<ref>{{cite web |date=19 December 2025 |title=BetVictor Scottish Open quarter-finals: afternoon session |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/19/betvictor-scottish-open-quarter-final--afternoon-session/ |access-date=19 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084254/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/19/betvictor-scottish-open-quarter-final--afternoon-session/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

Selt made breaks of 104, 92, 54, and 56 as he defeated Jiang 5–2, but he downplayed his chances of winning the event, claiming he had "no chance". Commenting on his time spent rebuilding his {{cuegloss|cue action}} with coach Chris Henry and practising with Higgins, he said: "I'm just concentrating on pulling my cue back the desired amount. The rest of it is falling into place....To be in the semi-finals is instant feedback on what we are trying to do [technically]. I felt really tight and snatchy when I was trying to get over the line, but that is something I will work on before tomorrow." Allen made a 139 break as he defeated O'Connor, also by a 5–2 scoreline.<ref>{{cite web |date=19 December 2025 |title=BetVictor Scottish Open quarter-finals: evening session |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/19/betvictor-scottish-open-quarter-finals--evening-session/ |access-date=20 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084251/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/19/betvictor-scottish-open-quarter-finals--evening-session/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Semi-finals ===
[[File:Mark Allen PHC 2016-3.jpg|thumb|[[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]] ''(pictured in 2016)'' won the season's first [[Home Nations Series]] event, the [[2025 English Open (snooker)|2025 English Open]]. He lost to [[Chang Bingyu]] in the semi-finals.]]
The semi-finals were played on 20 December as the best of 11 frames.<ref name="s.org_M" /><ref name="TS_draw_etc" /> Facing Selt in the first semi-final, Wakelin made breaks of 80, 108, and 58 as he took a 4–2 lead. Selt won frame seven with a {{cuegloss|clearance}} of 64 after Wakelin missed the black while on a break of 56. Wakelin won frame eight with a century of 104 to lead 5–3 and took a 56-point lead in frame nine, but Selt won the frame with a clearance of 70. Selt led in frame 10, but Wakelin made a 70 break to win the match 6–4. "Only a couple of weeks ago, things were tough and on the slide," said Wakelin, who had missed out on being seeded at the 2025 UK Championship and failed to qualify for the [[2026 Masters (snooker)|2026 Masters]] after falling to 17th in the [[snooker world rankings|world rankings]]. "I'd gone from climbing the rankings to three or four bad months. To come here, with not very much confidence and be in the final, I have far surpassed my expectations. I will be going for it tomorrow. There will be plenty of aggression from me and I'll be trying to win it."<ref name="WST_Wakelin_Selt">{{cite web |date=20 December 2025 |title=Wakelin holds off Selt to make final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/20/wakelin-holds-off-selt-to-make-final/ |access-date=20 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084253/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/20/wakelin-holds-off-selt-to-make-final/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

Facing Allen in the second semi-final, Chang made back-to-back centuries of 134 and 108 in the first two frames and made three other half-centuries of 67, 68, and 57 as he secured a 6–1 victory. Allen, who used a replacement cue during the match as his main cue was being repaired, gave the cue to a young fan at the conclusion of the match. "I didn't expect to play so well today," said Chang afterwards. "I just kept the same mindset as my previous matches. After that 134 break in the first frame, I felt completely locked in and focused. I feel like I'm playing better each match. Competing at a high intensity against these top players helps improve my game. I do feel like my {{cuegloss|positional play}} still needs improvement, but today my touch was pretty good."<ref>{{cite news |title=Allen beaten by Chang in Scottish Open semi-finals |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cvgj47dxnypo |work=[[BBC Sport]] |date=20 December 2025 |access-date=21 December 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420073801/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cvgj47dxnypo|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="WST_Chang_Allen">{{cite web |date=20 December 2025 |title=Sensational Chang storms to maiden rankin final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/20/sensational-chang-storms-to-maiden-final/ |access-date=21 December 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420084248/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/20/sensational-chang-storms-to-maiden-final/|archive-date=20 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>
