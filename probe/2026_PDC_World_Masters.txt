The '''2026 PDC World Masters''' (known for sponsorship reasons as the '''2026 Winmau World Masters''') was a professional [[darts]] tournament that was held from 29 January to 1 February 2026 at [[Arena MK]] in [[Milton Keynes]], England. The preliminary rounds of the tournament were held at Arena MK on 28 January 2026. Organised by the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC), it was the second edition of the [[PDC World Masters]], previously called the Masters, since the event's rebrand in [[2025 PDC World Masters|2025]]. The winner received £100,000 from a total prize fund of £500,000.

The main tournament featured a 32-player field. The top 24 players on the [[PDC Order of Merit]] following the [[2026 PDC World Darts Championship|2026 PDC World Championship]] automatically qualified for the main tournament. They were joined by eight qualifiers who advanced from the preliminary rounds, which featured players from outside of the top 24 and players from the PDC's affiliated tours. Two-time world champion [[Peter Wright (darts player)|Peter Wright]] failed to qualify for the tournament for the first time, while [[Gian van Veen]] reached the semi-finals in his World Masters debut.

[[Luke Humphries]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Jonny Clayton]] 6–5 in the [[2025 PDC World Masters|2025 final]]. Humphries reached his second successive final, where he was defeated 6–5 by [[Luke Littler]], who won his first World Masters title. Littler survived [[Glossary of darts#Match dart|match darts]] against [[Mike De Decker]] and [[Gerwyn Price]] during his campaign.

Humphries hit a [[nine-dart finish]] during his second-round victory over [[Luke Woodhouse]].

==Overview==
=== Background ===
[[File:Marshall Arena Milton Keynes 6 July 2020 (cropped).jpg|right|thumb|The preliminary rounds and main tournament were held at [[Arena MK]] in [[Milton Keynes]], England.]]
The inaugural edition of the [[PDC World Masters|Masters]], the [[2013 Masters (darts)|2013 Masters]], was staged by the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC) from 1 to 3 November 2013 at the [[Royal Highland Centre]] in [[Edinburgh]], Scotland.<ref>{{Cite web |date=14 June 2013 |title=PDC & ITV Sign New Four-Event Deal |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2013/06/14/pdc-itv-sign-new-four-event-deal |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250129132814/https://www.pdc.tv/news/2013/06/14/pdc-itv-sign-new-four-event-deal |url-status=dead |archive-date=29 January 2025 |access-date=10 January 2025 |website=[[Professional Darts Corporation]]}}</ref> [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]] won the first final, defeating [[Adrian Lewis]] 10–1.<ref>{{Cite web |date=4 November 2013 |title=Masters: Phil Taylor thrashes Adrian Lewis in all-Stoke final |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/darts/24804052 |access-date=10 January 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |archive-date=22 January 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250122115412/https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/darts/24804052 |url-status=live }}</ref> Since 2015, the tournament has been held in [[Milton Keynes]], England.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2 February 2015 |title=Michael van Gerwen beats Van Barneveld to win first Masters title |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/darts/31092634 |access-date=10 January 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]]}}</ref> The tournament initially featured the top 16 players in the world before increasing to the top 24 in 2021.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Allen |first=Dave |date=5 January 2021 |title=Ladbrokes Masters expands to 24 players in 2021 |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2021/01/05/ladbrokes-masters-expands-24-players-2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210105152529/https://www.pdc.tv/news/2021/01/05/ladbrokes-masters-expands-24-players-2021 |url-status=dead |archive-date=5 January 2021 |access-date=10 January 2025 |website=[[Professional Darts Corporation]]}}</ref>

In August 2024, it was announced that the field would expand to 32 players and the Masters would become a ranked event, starting from the [[2025 PDC World Masters|2025 edition]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Allen |first=Dave |date=29 August 2024 |title=The Masters expands to 32-player ranked event in 2025 |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2024/august/29/masters-expands-32-player-ranked-event-2025 |access-date=31 December 2024 |website=[[Professional Darts Corporation]]}}</ref> This preceded a bigger announcement that was made on 28 October 2024. The PDC announced a complete revamp of the competition, rebranding it from the Masters to the World Masters, emulating the [[World Masters (darts)|World Masters]] which was first held in 1975 and organised by the [[British Darts Organisation]] and later the [[World Darts Federation]].<ref name="RebrandPDC">{{Cite web |last=Phillips |first=Josh |date=28 October 2024 |title=Winmau World Masters to return as PDC event undergoes radical rebrand |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2024/october/28/winmau-world-masters-return-pdc-event-undergoes-radical-rebrand |access-date=31 December 2024 |website=Professional Darts Corporation}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Shaw |first=Jamie |date=28 October 2024 |title=Winmau World Masters to return in 2025 as PDC rebrand historic tournament |url=https://www.live-darts.com/pdc-darts-news/the-masters/winmau-world-masters-to-return-in-2025/ |access-date=10 January 2025 |website=Live Darts}}</ref> The revamp saw a change in format from [[Leg (darts)|leg]] play to [[Set (darts)|set]] play, and saw preliminary rounds introduced to determine the eight qualifiers who would join the top 24 players on the [[PDC Order of Merit]] in the main competition.<ref name="RebrandPDC" />

Sponsored by [[darts]] manufacturer [[Winmau]], the 2026 PDC World Masters was held from 29 January to 1 February 2026, preceded by the preliminary rounds held on 28 January; all matches were held at [[Arena MK]] in Milton Keynes.<ref name="WMSky">{{cite web |date=6 January 2026 |title=Winmau World Masters 2026: Luke Littler chasing maiden title in Milton Keynes |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12040/13491274/winmau-world-masters-2026-luke-littler-chasing-maiden-title-in-milton-keynes |website=Sky Sports |access-date=6 January 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=PDC World Masters 2026 |url=https://mastercaller.com/tournaments/the-masters/2026 |website=Mastercaller |access-date=6 January 2026}}</ref> [[Luke Humphries]] entered the tournament as defending champion, having defeated [[Jonny Clayton]] 6–5 in the 2025 final.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Keogh |first1=Frank |last2=Higham |first2=Paul |date=2 February 2025 |title=Humphries survives Clayton comeback to win epic Masters |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cr46kv0ggl9o |website=BBC Sport |access-date=6 January 2026 |archive-date=7 June 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250607053837/https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cr46kv0ggl9o |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Format===
The top 16 players on the PDC Order of Merit were [[Seeding (sport)|seeded]] for the first round and were drawn against the remaining 16 participants.<ref name="Preq"/> All matches were played in traditional World Masters set format, where all sets are played to the best of three legs. The number of sets required to win increased as the tournament progressed.<ref name="RebrandPDC" /><ref name="WMSky" />

===Final===
[[File:2025-04-03 Premier League Darts Berlin 2025 by Sandro Halank–124.jpg|thumb|[[Luke Littler]] won the World Masters for the first time, his 11th PDC major title and his fourth in a row.]]
The final between reigning world champion Luke Littler and defending champion Luke Humphries, the tournament's first and second seeds, took place in the evening session on 1 February.<ref name="PDCSunday"/> Humphries contested his second successive World Masters final, having defeated Jonny Clayton to win the 2025 edition, while Littler reached his first final. It was the seventh major final contested between the pair, with a tied head-to-head record of 3–3 in their previous six meetings.<ref name="IndSF"/> Humphries aimed to retain his title, while Littler looked to achieve a fourth consecutive major win.<ref name="ESPNFinal">{{cite web |date=1 February 2026 |title=Luke Littler beats Luke Humphries in World Masters final, who calls 19-year-old 'greatest ever' |url=https://www.espn.com/darts/story/_/id/47801062/luke-littler-luke-humphries-masters-darts-report |website=ESPN |access-date=2 February 2026}}</ref> Commentator [[Mark Webster (darts player)|Mark Webster]] called it "the perfect final", also saying that Littler needed a mix of luck and class to make it to the deciding match.<ref name="Blog"/><ref name="BBCBlog"/>

The defending champion claimed the opening set but Littler responded with a 153 checkout to open the next, soon levelling the contest at 1–1.<ref name="SkyFinal"/> The world champion established a two-set advantage by pinning back-to-back 121 checkouts in the fourth set, with the latter being finished on the bullseye.<ref name="PDCSunday"/><ref name="ESPNFinal"/> Humphries then won the next four legs to restore parity at 3–3.<ref name="PDCSunday"/> He squandered an opportunity to lead by missing three darts to win the seventh set, which Littler capitalised on by going in front once more.<ref name="PDCSunday"/> Littler missed three darts for another two-set lead, allowing Humphries to equalise again at 4–4.<ref name="BBCFinal"/> Humphries took a 5–4 lead to go one set away from victory, but Littler completed 13-dart and 12-dart legs to force a deciding set.<ref name="BBCFinal"/> Littler secured a break of throw before pinning double 10 to win the match 6–5.<ref name="ESPNFinal"/> Littler finished the final with a three-dart average of 104.72, slightly less than Humphries's 105.51, and hit 13 of the match's 25 maximums.<ref name="BBCFinal"/><ref name="SLFinal">{{cite web |last=Hammer |first=Chris |date=1 February 2026 |title=Darts results: Luke Littler beats Luke Humphries in an epic World Masters final to win his 11th major title |url=https://www.sportinglife.com/darts/news/darts-results-luke-littler-beats-luke-humphries-in-an-epic-world-masters-final-to-win-his-11th-major-title/230134 |website=Sporting Life |access-date=2 February 2026}}</ref>

Littler won the World Masters for the first time, his 11th PDC major title.<ref name="SkyFinal"/> A total of 11 major titles put him joint-third in the [[Professional Darts Corporation#Roll of Honour|PDC's all-time list]] alongside James Wade, only beaten by [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]]'s 79 and Michael van Gerwen's 48. Winning the World Masters also meant that Littler had won all but one of the available PDC major singles titles, only missing the [[European Championship (darts)|European Championship]] from his list of honours.<ref name="BBCFinal"/><ref name="SLFinal"/> "I'm shattered, absolutely knackered. Going into the last break at 5-4 [down], I said I have nothing left, absolutely nothing, but I just managed to dig deep," commented Littler afterwards, adding: "It has been me and Luke [Humphries] in the first major of the year and I'm sure it will continue."<ref name="BBCFinal"/> Humphries said the three missed darts to win the seventh set were the "only hole" of his game, claiming: "If I hit that shot, the possibility is I go on to win the game."<ref name="SkyFinal"/> "He [Luke Littler] has shown that true class. I said to him at the end I don't think he has a heart—he never folds under pressure," Humphries said about his opponent. "It may be premature, but I think he's the greatest darts player that's ever lived."<ref name="SkyFinal"/>
