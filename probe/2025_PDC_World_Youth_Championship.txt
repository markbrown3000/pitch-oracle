The '''2025 PDC World Youth Championship''' (known for sponsorship reasons as the '''2025 Winmau World Youth Championship''') was the fifteenth edition of the [[PDC World Youth Championship]], a [[darts]] tournament organised by the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC) for players aged between 16 and 24. The first stages of the tournament were played on 13 October 2025 at the Robin Park Leisure Centre in [[Wigan]], England. The final was played in between the semi-finals and final of the [[2025 Players Championship Finals|Players Championship Finals]] on 23 November 2025 at the [[Butlin's Minehead|Butlin's Resort]] in [[Minehead]], England. The total prize fund was £100,000, with the winner receiving £12,000.

The tournament featured 128 players, comprising [[List of players with a 2025 PDC Tour Card|PDC Tour Card holders]], players who competed on the [[2025 PDC Development Tour series|2025 PDC Development Tour]], and additional international qualifiers. Reigning world champion [[Luke Littler]], who won the [[2023 PDC World Youth Championship|2023 World Youth Championship]], took part in the tournament after winning the [[2025 World Grand Prix (darts)|World Grand Prix]] the previous night. He was defeated 6–5 in the semi-finals by [[Beau Greaves]], who became the first woman to reach the final of the tournament.<ref name="BBCSemis">{{cite web |last=Abraham |first=Timothy |date=13 October 2025 |title=Greaves stuns Littler a day after Grand Prix win  |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/darts/articles/c0jdp9dplg7o |publisher=BBC Sport |access-date=13 October 2025}}</ref><ref name="GuardianSemis">{{cite news |date=13 October 2025 |title=Beau Greaves stuns Luke Littler in PDC world youth championship thriller |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2025/oct/13/beau-greaves-luke-littler-pdc-world-youth-championship-darts |publisher=Guardian sport |agency=PA Media |access-date=13 October 2025}}</ref><ref name="WarringtonShock">{{cite news |last=Turner |first=Matt |date=13 October 2025 |title=Luke Littler's shock World Youth Championship defeat  |url=https://www.warringtonguardian.co.uk/sport/latest_sport/25540202.luke-littlers-shock-world-youth-championship-defeat/ |publisher=Warrington Guardian |access-date=13 October 2025}}</ref>

Defending champion [[Gian van Veen]], who defeated [[Jurjen van der Velde]] 6–5 to win the [[2024 PDC World Youth Championship|2024 final]], retained his title by defeating Greaves 6–3 in the final. He became the second player, after [[Dimitri Van den Bergh]], to defend the title.<ref>{{cite web |last=Mirza |first=Raz |date=23 November 2025 |title=Beau Greaves beaten by Gian van Veen as he successfully defends his World Youth Championship title in Minehead |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12040/13474454/beau-greaves-beaten-by-gian-van-veen-as-he-successfully-defends-his-world-youth-championship-title-in-minehead |website=Sky Sports |access-date=23 November 2025}}</ref>

==Overview==
===Background===
The [[PDC World Youth Championship|World Youth Championship]] was first held in 2010, following the expansion of the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC) in the wake of [[Split in darts#PDC bid to take over BDO|their rejected offer to purchase]] the [[British Darts Organisation]] (BDO), their rival darts organisation.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Jackson |first=Jamie |date=21 October 2009 |title=BDO founder rejects takeover by Barry Hearn's PDC |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2009/oct/21/barry-hearn-pdc-olly-croft-bdo |access-date=15 October 2025 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=16 February 2010 |title=PDC launch three new events |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/pdc-launch-three-new-events |access-date=15 October 2025 |website=Professional Darts Corporation |language=en}}</ref> [[Arron Monk]] won the inaugural tournament, defeating future world champion [[Michael van Gerwen]] in the final on 3 January 2011.<ref>{{cite web |date=3 January 2011 |title=Monk claims PDC Unicorn U21 title |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/monk-claims-pdc-unicorn-u21-title |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=15 October 2025}}</ref> As in all years but 2021, the tournament was held with a knockout stage behind closed doors while the final was televised at a later date – on that occasion before the final of the [[2011 PDC World Darts Championship|2011 World Championship]]. After the 2011 edition, the following finals were held on [[Premier League Darts|Premier League]] finals night, before settling in 2015 upon their current calendar position during the final session of the [[Players Championship Finals]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=19 October 2015 |title=Hopp & Aspinall reach World Youth final |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/pdc-unicorn-world-youth-championship-l64 |access-date=15 October 2025 |website=Professional Darts Corporation |language=en}}</ref> The fourteen previous editions saw thirteen different champions, with [[Dimitri Van den Bergh]] being the only player to successfully defend their title.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=14 December 2018 |title=Dimitri Van den Bergh retains World Youth Championship title with victory over Martin Schindler |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/11563866/dimitri-van-den-bergh-retains-world-youth-championship-title-with-victory-over-martin-schindler |access-date=15 October 2025 |website=Sky Sports}}</ref>

The first stages of the 2025 tournament—from the group stage to the semi-finals—were played behind closed doors at the Robin Park Leisure Centre in [[Wigan]], England on 13 October 2025. The final was played in between the semi-finals and final of the [[2025 Players Championship Finals]] on 23 November 2025 at the [[Butlin's Minehead|Butlin's Resort]] in [[Minehead]], England.<ref Following his [[2025 World Grand Prix (darts)|World Grand Prix]] victory on 12 October 2025, reigning world champion [[Luke Littler]], who won the [[2023 PDC World Youth Championship|2023 World Youth ===Format and qualification===
The [[PDC World Youth Championship]] is open to players aged between 16 and 24.<ref name="BBCWhy">{{cite web |last=Chowdhury |first=Tasnim |date=13 October 2025 |title=Why is Littler playing in World Youth Championship? |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/darts/articles/cpv1ykpe1dwo  |publisher=[[BBC Sport]] |access-date=13 October 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=PDC Rankings Rules |url=https://www.pdc.tv/pdc-order-merit-rules-1 |publisher=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=13 October 2025}}</ref> The tournament consisted of a group stage with four players in each group, followed by a knockout stage.<ref>{{cite web |last=Allen |first=Dave |date=9 October 2024 |title=2024 PDC Winmau World Youth Championship pre-qualified players confirmed |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2024/october/09/2024-pdc-winmau-world-youth-championship-pre-qualified-players-confirmed |publisher=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=13 October 2025}}</ref> The knockout stage was expanded to 64 players for the 2025 edition, with two players qualifying from each group.<ref>{{cite web |last=Allen |first=Dave |date=13 October 2025 |title=2025 PDC Order of Merit Rules confirmed |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/january/13/2025-pdc-order-merit-rules-confirmed |publisher=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=13 October 2025}}</ref> The group stage matches were played over the best of nine [[Leg (darts)|legs]] (first to five) and the knockout stage matches were played to the best of eleven (first to six).<ref name="SkyHeadline"/>

The tournament featured 128 players; all [[List of players with a 2025 PDC Tour Card|PDC Tour Card holders]] aged 23 or under at the start of the season were invited, and were joined by additional international qualifiers and players who competed on the [[2025 PDC Development Tour series|2025 PDC Development Tour]].<ref name="PDPA"/>
