The '''2026 Scottish Open''' (officially the '''2026 BetVictor Scottish Open''') is an upcoming professional [[snooker]] tournament that will take place from 14 to 20 December 2026 at the [[Meadowbank Sports Centre]] in [[Edinburgh]], Scotland. Qualifying took place from 20 to 23 September at the [[Leicester Arena]] in [[Leicester]], England. The 11th consecutive edition of the tournament since it was revived in [[2016 Scottish Open (snooker)|2016]], it will be the 11th [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2026–27 snooker season]], following the [[2026 Snooker Shoot Out]] and preceding the [[2027 German Masters]]. It will be the third of four tournaments in the season's [[Home Nations Series]], following the [[2026 English Open (snooker)|2026 English Open]] and the [[2026 Northern Ireland Open]] and preceding the [[2027 Welsh Open (snooker)|2027 Welsh Open]].

[[Chris Wakelin]] is the defending champion, having defeated [[Chang Bingyu]] 9–2 in the [[2025 Scottish Open (snooker)|2025 final]].

== Overview ==
The tournament originated as the non-ranking [[1981 International Open]], staged at the [[Derby Assembly Rooms|Assembly Rooms]] in [[Derby]], England. [[Steve Davis]] won the event, defeating [[Dennis Taylor]] 9–0 in the final. The tournament became a [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] the following year, the first event after the [[World Snooker Championship]] to gain ranking status. Staged annually under various names (with the exception of the three years from 1990 to 1992, when it was not held), the tournament moved to Scotland in 1997 and was first branded as the [[Scottish Open (snooker)|Scottish Open]] in 1998.<ref>{{cite web |last=Turner |first=Chris |title=International Open, Goya Matchroom Trophy |url=http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/int.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120216155037/http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/int.html |archive-date=16 February 2012 |access-date=4 November 2011 |work=cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk |publisher=Chris Turner's Snooker Archive}}</ref> It was discontinued after the 2004 edition, apart from one staging in [[2012 Scottish Open|2012]] as a [[List of minor-ranking tournaments|minor-ranking tournament]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Betfair European Tour Event Five (2012) |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=203 |access-date=15 September 2025 |website=snooker.org |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420074446/https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=203 |archive-date=20 April 2026 |url-status=live}}</ref> The tournament was restored to the calendar as a full ranking event in 2016 as part of the newly created [[Home Nations Series]].<ref>{{cite news |date=29 April 2015 |title=World Championship: Snooker tour to be revamped in 2016 |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/32519199 |access-date=15 September 2025 |work=[[BBC Sport]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420073715/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/32519199 |archive-date=20 April 2026 |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Marco Fu]] won the [[2016 Scottish Open (snooker)|2016 edition]], recovering from 1–4 behind to beat [[John Higgins]] 9–4 in the final; he was presented with the newly named [[Stephen Hendry]] Trophy by the seven-time World Champion personally.<ref>{{cite web |title=Scottish Open trophy named after Stephen Hendry |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=30 September 2016 |url=https://wst.tv/scottish-open-trophy-named-after-stephen-hendry/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200220231732/https://wst.tv/scottish-open-trophy-named-after-stephen-hendry/ |access-date=12 August 2023 |archive-date=20 February 2020 |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=18 December 2016 |title=Scottish Open: Marco Fu fights back to stun John Higgins in final in Glasgow |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/38357882 |access-date=15 September 2025 |work=[[BBC Sport]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260420073728/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/38357882 |archive-date=20 April 2026 |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Format ===
The tournament uses a tiered format first implemented for the [[Home Nations Series]] in the [[2024–25 snooker season]]. In round one, players seeded 65–96 face those [[seeding (sports)|seeded]] 97 and below, including selected amateurs. In round two, the winners from round one face players seeded 33–64. The winners from round two face the top 32 seeds.<ref>{{cite web |date=5 April 2024 |title=Tiered format for Home Nations and German Masters in 2024/25 |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/april/05/tiered-format-for-home-nations-and-german-masters-in-2024-25/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240405185554/https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/april/05/tiered-format-for-home-nations-and-german-masters-in-2024-25/ |archive-date=5 April 2024 |access-date=5 April 2024 |work=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref> All matches are played as the best of 7 {{cuegloss|frame|frames}} until the quarter{{nbh}}finals, which are the best of 11. The semi{{nbh}}finals are the best of 11, and the final is a best{{nbh}}of{{nbh}}17-frame match played over two {{cuegloss|session|sessions}}.<ref name="s.o_M" />
