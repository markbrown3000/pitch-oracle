}}The '''2025 World Snooker Championship''' (officially the '''2025 Halo World Snooker Championship''') was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 19 April to 5 May 2025 at the [[Crucible Theatre]] in [[Sheffield]], England, the 49th consecutive year the [[World Snooker Championship]] was staged at the venue. Qualifying took place from 7 to 16&nbsp;April at the [[English Institute of Sport, Sheffield|English Institute of Sport]] in Sheffield. Organised by the [[World Snooker Tour]] and broadcast by [[BBC Sport]] in the UK, [[Eurosport]] in Europe, and other broadcasters globally, it was the 18th and final [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking]] event of the [[2024–25 snooker season|2024–25 season]]. The winner received £500,000 from a total prize fund of £2,395,000.

A then-record ten Chinese players reached the main stage, while three players, [[Lei Peifan]], [[Zak Surety]], and [[Daniel Wells (snooker player)|Daniel Wells]], made their Crucible debuts. [[Kyren Wilson]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Jak Jones]] 18–14 in the [[2024 World Snooker Championship|2024 final]] to win his maiden world title, but he lost 9–10 to Lei in the first round, becoming the 20th player to experience the [[Crucible curse]]. Competing as an amateur after serving a 20-month ban, [[Zhao Xintong]] won four qualifying matches to reach the main stage. He beat [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] 17–7 in the semi-finals and [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] 18–12 in the final to become the first world champion from Asia. He was the fourth qualifier to win the title, after [[Alex Higgins]], [[Terry Griffiths]], and [[Shaun Murphy]]. At 50, Williams became the oldest world finalist, surpassing [[Ray Reardon]], who had been 49 at the [[1982 World Snooker Championship|1982 final]].

The qualifying rounds produced a then-record 143 [[Century break|century breaks]], and the main stage produced 107. Zhao made 18 centuries across qualifying and the main stage combined, equalling the record set by [[Ding Junhui]] at the [[2016 World Snooker Championship|2016 event]]. In the third qualifying round, [[Jackson Page]] became the first player to make two [[Maximum break|maximum breaks]] in a professional match. In the second round, [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]] made the 15th Crucible maximum, bringing the season's total to a then-record 15. [[Judd Trump]] finished the season with a record 107 centuries, surpassing [[Neil Robertson]]'s previous season record of 103. [[Dominic Dale]] and [[Joe Perry (snooker player)|Joe Perry]], both professionals since 1992, retired after the tournament.

==Background==
[[File:Crucible Theatre during the 2024 World Snooker Championship.jpg|alt=The Crucible Theatre from outside|thumb|For the 49th consecutive year, the main stage of the tournament was held at the [[Crucible Theatre]] ''(pictured in 2024)'' in [[Sheffield]], England.]]
The inaugural [[1927 World Snooker Championship]], then known as the Professional Championship of Snooker, took place at various venues in England between November 1926 and May 1927. [[Joe Davis]] won the final—held at [[Camkin's Hall]] in [[Birmingham]] from 9 to 12&nbsp;May 1927—and went on to win the tournament 15 consecutive times before retiring undefeated after the [[1946 World Snooker Championship|1946 edition]] (no tournaments were held from 1941 to 1945 because of [[World War II]]).<ref name=":25">{{cite book |last=Everton |first=Clive |title=The History of Snooker and Billiards |publisher=[[Transworld (publisher)|Partridge Press]] |location=[[Haywards Heath]] |date=1986 |isbn=978-1-85225-013-3 |page=50}}</ref><ref name=":41">{{cite web |last=Turner |first=Chris |title=World Professional Championship |url=http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/world.html |website=Chris Turner's Snooker Archive |year=2008 |access-date=31 March 2024 |archive-date=6 June 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160606102115/http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/world.html |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=11 July 1978 |title=Joe Davis, a snooker champion who retired unbeaten, Was 77 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1978/07/11/archives/joe-davis-a-snooker-champion-who-retired-unbeaten-was-77.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231022194142/https://www.nytimes.com/1978/07/11/archives/joe-davis-a-snooker-champion-who-retired-unbeaten-was-77.html |archive-date=22 October 2023 |access-date=31 March 2024 |work=[[The New York Times]] |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> The tournament went into abeyance after only two players contested the [[1952 World Snooker Championship|1952 edition]]. The six editions of the [[World Professional Match-play Championship|World Professional {{nowrap|Match-play}} Championship]] held between 1952 and 1957 are retroactively regarded as legitimate continuations of the World Snooker Championship, but that tournament was discontinued due to waning public interest in snooker in the post-war era. The world title was uncontested between 1958 and 1963.<ref name=":25" /><ref name=":41" />

Then-professional player [[Rex Williams]] was instrumental in reviving the World Snooker Championship on a challenge basis in 1964. [[John Pulman]], winner of the [[1957 World Professional Match-play Championship|1957 World Professional {{nowrap|Match-play}} Championship]], defended the world title across seven [[1964–68 World Snooker Championships|challenge matches between 1964 and 1968]]. The World Snooker Championship reverted to an annual knockout tournament for the [[1969 World Snooker Championship|1969 edition]], marking the beginning of the championship's "modern era".<ref>{{cite web |last1=Nunns |first1=Hector |last2=Hendon |first2=David |title=Full history |url=https://wpbsa.com/about-us/history/full-history/ |work=[[WPBSA]] |access-date=31 March 2024 |archive-date=31 March 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240331092827/https://wpbsa.com/about-us/history/full-history/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Wright |first=Joe |title=Who has won the most World Snooker Championship titles? ===Format===
The top 16 players in the [[snooker world rankings]], as they stood after the [[2025 Tour Championship]], were [[Seeding (sports)|seeded]] through to the main stage of the tournament.<ref>{{cite web |title=Johnstone's Paint world rankings update: Crucible seedings confirmed |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/april/06/johnstone-s-paint-world-rankings-update--crucible-seedings-confirmed/ |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=6 April 2025 |access-date=11 April 2025 |archive-date=11 April 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250411123037/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/april/06/johnstone-s-paint-world-rankings-update--crucible-seedings-confirmed/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Qualifying took place from 7 to 16&nbsp;April at the [[English Institute of Sport, Sheffield|English Institute of Sport]] in Sheffield, featuring 128 players, 16 of whom also reached the main stage.<ref name="WST_Q_draw">{{cite web |title=Halo World Championship qualifiers draw |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/march/27/halo-world-championship-qualifiers-draw/ |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=27 March 2025 |access-date=31 March 2025 |archive-date=27 March 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250327222531/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/march/27/halo-world-championship-qualifiers-draw/ |url-status=live}}</ref> The qualifiers were held over four rounds, with players ranked 49 to 80 seeded through to the second qualifying round, and players ranked 17 to 48 seeded through to the third qualifying round. All qualifying matches were played as the best of 19&nbsp;{{cuegloss|frames}}, held over two {{cuegloss|sessions}}.<ref name="WST_Q_draw" /> The {{nowrap|first-round}} draw, during which the 16 successful qualifiers were drawn at random against the top 16 seeds, was held on 17&nbsp;April, broadcast on [[BBC Radio 5 Live]] and the [[BBC Sport]] website.<ref>{{cite web |title=Halo World Championship format confirmed |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/april/11/halo-world-championship-format-confirmed/ |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=11 April 2025 |access-date=12 April 2025 |archive-date=12 April 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250412102041/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/april/11/halo-world-championship-format-confirmed/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=How to watch Judgement Day and the draw |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/april/14/how-to-watch-the-halo-world-championship-qualifiers-and-judgement-day/ |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=14 April 2025 |access-date=14 April 2025 |archive-date=14 April 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250414203337/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/april/14/how-to-watch-the-halo-world-championship-qualifiers-and-judgement-day/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="WST_Main_Draw" /> The main stage of the tournament took place from 19&nbsp;April to 5&nbsp;May at the Crucible Theatre.<ref>{{cite web |title=Halo World Championship 2025 tickets |url=https://www.wst.tv/tickets/world-championship-2025-tickets/ |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |access-date=3 April 2025 |archive-date=3 April 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250403102751/https://www.wst.tv/tickets/world-championship-2025-tickets/ |url-status=live}}</ref> {{nowrap|First-round}} matches were played as the best of 19&nbsp;frames, held over two sessions. {{nowrap|Second-round}} and {{nowrap|quarter-final}} matches were played as the best of 25&nbsp;frames, held over three sessions. The {{nowrap|semi-final}} matches were played as the best of 33&nbsp;frames, held over four sessions. The final was the best of 35&nbsp;frames, also held over four sessions.<ref name="s.o_Main" />

====Final====
[[File:Desislava Bozhilova European Masters 2023 (cropped).JPG|thumb|[[Desislava Bozhilova]] ''(pictured in 2023)'' became the second woman to referee a World Championship final and the first to referee the finals of all three [[Triple Crown (snooker)|Triple Crown]] events.]]
The best-of-35-frame final took place over four sessions on 4 and 5 May between the sixth seed Williams and qualifier Zhao.<ref name="s.o_Main" /><ref name="WST_matches_M" /> At age 50, Williams was the oldest player to contest a World Championship final, surpassing Reardon, who had played in the [[1982 World Snooker Championship|1982 final]] at age 49.<ref name=":67" /> He was competing in his fifth world final, having previously won the title in [[2000 World Snooker Championship|2000]], [[2003 World Snooker Championship|2003]], and [[2018 World Snooker Championship|2018]] and been runner-up in [[1999 World Snooker Championship|1999]].<ref name=":67" /> The second player from China to reach a world final, after Ding at the [[2016 World Snooker Championship|2016 event]], Zhao was competing in his third ranking final and second [[Triple Crown (snooker)|Triple Crown]] final, following his wins at the [[2021 UK Championship]] and the [[2022 German Masters]].<ref name=":64" /> The first final in World Championship history between two left-handed players,<ref name=":65" /> it also featured the largest age gap (22&nbsp;years) between two world finalists.<ref name=":67" /> Bulgarian referee [[Desislava Bozhilova]] officiated her first World Championship final. She was the second woman to referee a world final—after [[Michaela Tabb]] in [[2009 World Snooker Championship|2009]] and [[2012 World Snooker Championship|2012]]—and the first woman to officiate the finals of all three Triple Crown events, having previously taken charge of the [[2022 Masters (snooker)|2022 Masters]] and [[2022 UK Championship]] finals.<ref name="referee">{{cite web |date=2 April 2025 |title=Bozhilova to referee Crucible final for first time |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/april/02/bozhilova-to-referee-crucible-final-for-first-time/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250403170938/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/april/02/bozhilova-to-referee-crucible-final-for-first-time/ |archive-date=3 April 2025 |access-date=3 April 2025 |work=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref>

==== Rankings controversy ====
Zhao was the first player to win a ranking event while competing as an amateur,<ref name=":71" /> which led to a controversy about his world ranking for the following season. In a BBC interview with [[Hazel Irvine]] and Doherty, WPBSA chair Ferguson stated that Zhao would retain ranking points from the event.<ref name=":69" /><ref>{{Cite news |title=Zhao Xintong: Who is China's first World Snooker Champion and why was he banned? |archive-date=6 May 2025 |access-date=10 May 2025 |work=Sky Sports |language=en-GB |url-status=live }}</ref> However, Doherty stated that "a lot of players" were "up in arms" about the issue and questioned whether the rules had been changed, suggesting that Zhao "should be going back to scratch" in terms of ranking points.<ref name=":76" /><ref name=":78">{{Cite web |last1=Crawford |first1=Ben |last2=Gamp |first2=Oli |date=7 May 2025 |title=Zhao Xintong statement made after snooker controversy left players raging
