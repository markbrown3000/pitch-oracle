The '''2026 UK Open''' (known for sponsorship reasons as the '''2026 Ladbrokes UK Open''') was a professional [[darts]] tournament that took place from 6 to 8 March 2026 at the [[Butlin's Minehead|Butlin's Resort]] in [[Minehead]], England. It was the 24th staging of the [[UK Open]] by the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC). The winner received £120,000 from a total prize fund of £750,000.

The tournament's 157-player field consisted of 126 [[List of players with a 2026 PDC Tour Card|PDC Tour Card holders]], 15 top players from the 2025 editions of the [[2025 PDC Development Tour series|PDC Development Tour]] and the [[2025 PDC Challenge Tour series|PDC Challenge Tour]], and 16 amateur qualifiers. Players entered the tournament incrementally based on their [[PDC Order of Merit]] ranking, with an open draw conducted for each round.

[[Luke Littler]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[James Wade]] 11–2 in the [[2025 UK Open|2025 final]]. In a repeat of the previous year's final, he retained his title by beating Wade 11–7. Littler became the fourth player to defend the title, after [[Raymond van Barneveld]], [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]] and [[Michael van Gerwen]], and was the first to achieve the feat since Van Gerwen in [[2016 UK Open|2016]].

[[Danny Noppert]] hit a [[nine-dart finish]] to win his fourth-round match against [[Dimitri Van den Bergh]].

== Overview ==
=== Background ===
The 2026 [[UK Open]] was the 24th edition of the tournament to be staged by the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC) since the [[2003 UK Open|inaugural edition]] in 2003.<ref name="UKOMC">{{cite web |title=UK Open |url=https://mastercaller.com/tournaments/uk-open |website=Mastercaller |access-date=12 February 2026 |archive-date=2 January 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260102172218/https://mastercaller.com/tournaments/uk-open |url-status=live }}</ref> The tournament is commonly referred to as the "[[FA Cup]] of [[darts]]" due to its round-by-round open draw system.<ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2023 |title=Andrew Gilding shocks Michael van Gerwen to win UK Open |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/64859851 |website=BBC Sport |access-date=12 February 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Liew |first=Jonathan |date=3 March 2025 |title=‘FA Cup of darts’ keeps grassroots flame alive as big-money era shapes to snuff it out |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2025/mar/03/uk-open-darts-minehead-somerset |website=The Guardian |access-date=12 February 2026}}</ref> The first event was held at the [[Reebok Stadium]] in [[Bolton]], England, and was won by [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]], who defeated [[Shayne Burgess]] 18–8 in the final.<ref>{{cite web |date=2 June 2003 |title=Darts: Taylor's power firing on all cylinders again |url=https://www.belfasttelegraph.co.uk/news/darts-taylors-power-firing-on-all-cylinders-again/a/119940407.html |website=Belfast Telegraph |access-date=12 February 2026}}</ref> Taylor remains the most successful player in the event's history, winning it a total of five times.<ref name="2026BBC">{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=What is the UK Open? |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cj4zzjrd8l5o |website=BBC Sport |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> Following a decade of being held in Bolton, the event moved to the [[Butlin's Minehead|Butlin's Resort]] in [[Minehead]] in [[2014 UK Open|2014]] and has stayed there, with the exception of the [[2021 UK Open|2021 edition]] held in [[Milton Keynes]].<ref>{{cite web |date=10 March 2014 |title=Adrian Lewis storms to fourth major title |url=https://www.sthelensstar.co.uk/sport/11064426.adrian-lewis-storms-to-fourth-major-title/ |website=St Helens Star |access-date=12 February 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=26 January 2021 |title=The UK Open to move to the Marshall Arena in Milton Keynes this March |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/12199276/the-uk-open-to-move-to-the-marshall-arena-in-milton-keynes-this-march |website=Sky Sports |access-date=12 February 2026}}</ref>

The 2026 edition was held from 6 to 8 March 2026 at the Butlin's Resort in Minehead. British gambling company [[Ladbrokes]] continued its sponsorship of the event.<ref>{{cite web |title=UK Open 2026 |url=https://mastercaller.com/tournaments/uk-open/2026 |website=Mastercaller |access-date=12 February 2026}}</ref> [[Luke Littler]] entered the tournament as defending champion, having defeated [[James Wade]] 11–2 in the [[2025 UK Open|2025 final]] to win his first UK Open title.<ref name="2025Final">{{cite web |last=Abraham |first=Timothy |date=2 March 2025 |title=Littler thrashes Wade to win UK Open in style |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/c36w5e1w3p2o |website=BBC Sport |access-date=11 February 2026 |archive-date=26 July 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250726013619/https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/c36w5e1w3p2o |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Format ===
All 157 participants were unseeded, although players entered the tournament incrementally: [[List of players with a 2026 PDC Tour Card|PDC Tour Card holders]] ranked 97–128 on the [[PDC Order of Merit]] entered in the first round along with all other qualifiers; those ranked 65–96 entered in the second round; those ranked 33–64 entered in the third round; and those ranked 1–32 entered in the fourth round. An open draw was conducted for each round.<ref name="PDPA">{{cite web |title=UK Open 2026 |url=https://pdpa.co.uk/event/uk-open-2026/ |website=PDPA |access-date=11 February 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260226015918/https://pdpa.co.uk/event/uk-open-2026/ |archive-date=26 February 2026 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=25 February 2025 |title=UK Open Darts: Rounds 1-3 explained |url=https://www.target-darts.co.uk/blog/uk-open-darts-rounds-1-3-explained.html |website=Target Darts |access-date=12 February 2026 |archive-date=13 March 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250313182029/https://www.target-darts.co.uk/blog/uk-open-darts-rounds-1-3-explained.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

The tournament operated under a multi-board system: eight boards were used for matches in the first, second, third and fourth rounds; four boards were used for matches in the fifth round; two boards were used for matches in the sixth round; and the quarter-finals, semi-finals and final all took place on the main stage. All matches were in [[Leg (darts)|leg]] play format, with the number of legs required to win increasing as the tournament progresses:<ref name="Draw">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=2 March 2026 |title=2026 Ladbrokes UK Open draw & stage allocations confirmed |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/march/02/2026-ladbrokes-uk-open-draw---stage-allocations-confirmed/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=2 March 2026}}</ref>

* '''First, second and third rounds''': Best of 11 legs
* '''Fourth round, fifth round, sixth round and quarter-finals''': Best of 19 legs
* '''Semi-finals and final''': Best of 21 legs

== Participants ==
The 126 [[List of players with a 2026 PDC Tour Card|Tour Card holders]] competing in the tournament had a staggered entry based on their [[PDC Order of Merit]] ranking on 26 February 2026.<ref>{{cite web |title=UK Open Race |url=https://www.dartsrankings.com/ukopen |website=dartsrankings.com |access-date=16 February 2026}}</ref> They were joined by the top eight non-qualified players from both the 2025 [[2025 PDC Challenge Tour series|Challenge Tour]] and [[2025 PDC Development Tour series|Development Tour]] Orders of Merit, as well as 16 amateur qualifiers.<ref name="PDPA"/>

Viktor Tingström and [[Danny van Trijp]] both withdrew from the tournament due to personal reasons. Van Trijp's scheduled opponent Scott Campbell received a [[Bye (sports)|bye]] to the second round, while the winner of a first-round tie between Lewis Pearse and Niall Culleton received a bye to the third round due to Tingstrom's withdrawal. Matthias Ehlers also withdrew due to injury, meaning his scheduled opponent Marvin Kraft received a bye to the second round.<ref>{{cite web |last=Simpson |first=Will |date=5 March 2026 |title=2026 Ladbrokes UK Open preview |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/march/05/2026-ladbrokes-uk-open-preview/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=5 March 2026}}</ref>

=== Opening rounds ===
[[File:Raymond van Barneveld in 2019.png|thumb|Two-time UK Open champion [[Raymond van Barneveld]] ''(pictured in 2019)'' came back from 5–2 down to defeat [[Karel Sedláček]] 6–5 in the third round, before being eliminated in his next match.]]
The first, second, and third rounds were played in the afternoon session on 6 March.<ref name="PDCDay1">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=6 March 2026 |title=Littler & Humphries start in style as Noppert nails the nine |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/march/06/littler-humphries-start-style-noppert/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> In the first round, [[2025 PDC Challenge Tour series|Challenge Tour]] qualifier Sam Spivey earned a 6–2 win over 16-year-old Kyle Gilding, the youngest player in the tournament, while 62-year-old amateur qualifier Clive Langford lost in a [[Glossary of darts#Decider|deciding leg]] to Stephen Rosney.<ref name="2026BBC"/><ref>{{cite web |last=Muzzupappa |first=Luca |date=6 March 2026 |title=Factory to the oche: Langford, 62, set for major debut |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cp322130wwlo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> David Sharp rebounded from 5–2 down to take Adam Leek to a deciding leg where Leek claimed a 6–5 victory, ending the match with a [[three-dart average]] of 99.56.<ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=Adam Leek v David Sharp - 2026 UK Open |url=https://recap.dartconnect.com/matches/69aad26a843039e10aeffad6 |website=DartConnect |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> From 4–1 behind, amateur qualifier Samuel Whittaker won five consecutive [[Leg (darts)|legs]] to defeat former [[World Matchplay (darts)|World Matchplay]] semi-finalist [[Jeffrey de Zwaan]] 6–4.<ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=Samuel Whittaker v Jeffrey de Zwaan - 2026 UK Open |url=https://recap.dartconnect.com/matches/69aab496843039e10aefea30 |website=DartConnect |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> [[Scott Waites]], the [[2010 Grand Slam of Darts|2010 Grand Slam]] champion and two-time [[BDO World Darts Championship|BDO world champion]], lost 6–4 to [[Jeffrey Sparidaans]]. Tyler Thorpe achieved the only [[Whitewash (sport)|whitewash]] win of the first round, beating Pero Ljubić 6–0. Amateur qualifiers Jack Todd, Oliver King, and [[Ron Meulenkamp]] also progressed past the first round.<ref name="MCResults"/> Scott Campbell and Marvin Kraft received [[Bye (sports)|byes]] to the second round following the withdrawals of [[Danny van Trijp]] and Matthias Ehlers. Niall Culleton, who defeated Lewis Pearse 6–3, went through to the third round due to his second-round opponent Viktor Tingström's withdrawal.<ref name="PDCDay1"/>

In the second round, three-time women's world champion [[Beau Greaves]] lost her opening match 6–4 to [[Darryl Pilgrim]].<ref name="BBCDay1">{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=Littler's UK Open defence off to winning start |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cn5g3p6v7v2o |website=BBC Sport |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> [[Jimmy van Schie]] and [[Shane McGuirk]], the two previous [[WDF World Darts Championship|WDF world champions]], were eliminated by Tom Bissell and Thomas Lovely, respectively.<ref name="DNews">{{cite web |last=Gill |first=Samuel |date=6 March 2026 |title=UK Open Friday afternoon session Round-Up - Peter Wright joins Raymond van Barneveld in reviving hopes, Beau Greaves falters early |url=https://dartsnews.com/pdc/uk-open-friday-afternoon-session-round-up-peter-wright-joins-raymond-van-barneveld-in-reviving-hopes-beau-greaves-falters-early |website=dartsnews.com |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> [[Sebastian Białecki]] took a 5–1 lead against [[2012 BDO World Darts Championship|2012]] BDO world champion [[Christian Kist]] but conceded the next four legs; however, Białecki prevailed in the deciding leg.<ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=Sebastian Białecki v Christian Kist |url=https://recap.dartconnect.com/matches/69aadb55843039e10af0004c |website=DartConnect |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> Pascal Rupprecht also halted a comeback from 5–1 down by [[Cam Crabtree]], winning 6–5.<ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=Pascal Rupprecht v Cam Crabtree |url=https://recap.dartconnect.com/matches/69aaf19d843039e10af00d5b |website=DartConnect |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> [[Michael Unterbuchner]] and [[2025 PDC Development Tour series|Development Tour]] qualifier Patrik Williams both overturned 5–2 deficits to defeat [[Maik Kuivenhoven]] and [[Cor Dekker]].<ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=Maik Kuivenhoven v Michael Unterbuchner |url=https://recap.dartconnect.com/matches/69aacf1f843039e10aeff8f7 |website=DartConnect |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=Patrik Williams v Cor Dekker |url=https://recap.dartconnect.com/matches/69aacd70843039e10aeff7e6 |website=DartConnect |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> [[Adam Gawlas]], [[Jim Long (darts player)|Jim Long]] and [[Adam Lipscombe]] earned respective whitewash victories against Tavis Dudeney, [[Tytus Kanik]] and Todd; Gawlas averaged 100.20 in his win. Amateur qualifiers Todd, Whittaker, King, and Meulenkamp were all eliminated in the second round.<ref name="MCResults"/>

In the third round, [[Karel Sedláček]] missed five [[Glossary of darts#Match dart|match darts]] to defeat two-time UK Open champion and five-time world champion [[Raymond van Barneveld]], who came back from 5–2 down to win 6–5.<ref name="PDCDay1"/> Following [[Charlie Manby]]'s 6–4 victory over [[James Hurrell]], the pair were involved in an altercation where the referee had to intervene. In a post-match interview, Manby suggested that Hurrell objected to his loud celebrations during the contest, insisting that he and Hurrell were "mates" and that there are "no friends on the [[oche]]".<ref>{{cite web |date=7 March 2026 |title=Charlie Manby and James Hurrell involved in heated exchange after UK Open clash |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/sport/luke-littler-itv-luke-humphries-michael-van-gerwen-chris-dobey-b2933856.html |website=The Independent |access-date=7 March 2026 |agency=Press Association}}</ref> [[Ricky Evans (darts player)|Ricky Evans]] and [[William O'Connor (darts player)|William O'Connor]] recorded averages over 100 in their respective wins against [[Brendan Dolan]] and [[Ryan Meikle]].<ref name="DNews"/> The [[2017 UK Open]] champion and two-time world champion [[Peter Wright (darts player)|Peter Wright]] began his campaign by defeating [[Darius Labanauskas]] 6–3. Development Tour qualifier [[Henry Coates]] advanced to the fourth round by beating Michael Unterbuchner 6–4, while Challenge Tour qualifier Scott Campbell also progressed by defeating [[Ian White (darts player)|Ian White]] 6–2. Niall Culleton capitalised on his bye to the third round, winning 6–4 against [[Owen Bates]] to reach the last 64.<ref name="MCResults"/>

=== Fourth round ===
[[File:Danny Noppert 2025.png|thumb|upright=0.85|The [[2022 UK Open|2022]] champion [[Danny Noppert]] ''(pictured in 2025)'' hit a [[nine-dart finish]] to complete a 10–4 win over [[Dimitri Van den Bergh]] in the fourth round.]]
The fourth round was played in the evening session on 6 March, where the world's top 32 players entered the tournament.<ref name="PDCDay1"/> The defending champion and world number one [[Luke Littler]] faced [[Damon Heta]] in his opening tie, a rematch of their [[2024 UK Open]] quarter-final meeting. Littler, who had won a night of the [[2026 Premier League Darts|Premier League]] the day prior, landed a [[Glossary of darts#Maximum check|170 checkout]] to go 7–2 ahead before eventually winning 10–3, avenging his 2024 loss to Heta.<ref name="PDCDay1"/> Three-time UK Open and world champion [[Michael van Gerwen]] initially trailed the [[2019 UK Open|2019]] champion [[Nathan Aspinall]] 4–2, but later secured a 10–8 victory. Van Gerwen commented that he gave Aspinall too many chances during the match, labelling his opponent "a fighter".<ref name="BBCDay1"/> World number two [[Luke Humphries]] established a 7–3 lead against [[Luke Woodhouse]], including a run of three consecutive legs that saw him hit [[Glossary of darts#Checkout|checkouts]] of 144 and 118. He then claimed another three in a row, converting a 116 checkout to complete a 10–3 win.<ref name="SkyDay1">{{cite web |last=Wellens |first=Megan |date=6 March 2026 |title=UK Open 2026: Luke Littler demolishes Damon Heta to get defence up and running as Michael van Gerwen battles past Nathan Aspinall |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13516205/uk-open-2026-luke-littler-demolishes-damon-heta-to-get-defence-up-and-running-as-michael-van-gerwen-battles-past-nathan-aspinall |website=Sky Sports |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> World number three [[Gian van Veen]] was the highest-ranked player to get knocked out in the fourth round, losing 10–7 to [[2018 PDC World Darts Championship|2018]] world champion [[Rob Cross]].<ref name="PDCDay1"/> The [[2022 UK Open|2022]] champion [[Danny Noppert]] hit a [[nine-dart finish]] in the final leg of his match against [[2024 UK Open|2024]] champion [[Dimitri Van den Bergh]], sealing a 10–4 victory.<ref name="SkyDay1"/> The loss for Van den Bergh resulted in him dropping out of the world's top 32 on the [[PDC Order of Merit]], falling to world number 37.<ref>{{cite web |date=9 March 2026 |title=UK Open shakes up PDC rankings as Wade applies pressure and Van den Bergh pays a heavy price |url=https://dartsplanet.tv/uk-open-shakes-up-pdc-rankings/ |website=Darts Planet TV |access-date=16 March 2026}}</ref>

Peter Wright continued his run by defeating [[Cameron Menzies]] 10–6 in an all-Scottish tie, while Raymond van Barneveld lost 10–1 to fellow Dutchman [[Wessel Nijman]]. The [[2018 UK Open|2018]] champion and two-time world champion [[Gary Anderson (darts player)|Gary Anderson]] registered a three-dart average of 107.42 on his way to beating [[Dirk van Duijvenbode]] 10–6.<ref name="SkyDay1"/><ref>{{cite web |last=Wood |first=Kieran |date=6 March 2026 |title=UK Open 2026 Roundup - Littler, Humphries and Van Gerwen safely through as Noppert hits nine-darter in Minehead |url=https://dartsnews.com/pdc/uk-open-2026-roundup-littler-humphries-and-van-gerwen-safely-through-as-noppert-hits-nine-darter-in-minehead |website=dartsnews.com |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> William O'Connor lost in a deciding leg to [[Josh Rock]], whose Northern Irish [[PDC World Cup of Darts|World Cup]] partner [[Daryl Gurney]] defeated [[Niels Zonneveld]] 10–7.<ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=Keane Barry, Josh Rock and Daryl Gurney advance at 'FA Cup of darts' |url=https://www.rte.ie/sport/darts/2026/0306/1562101-barry-rock-and-gurney-advance-at-fa-cup-of-darts/ |website=RTÉ Sport |access-date=7 March 2026}}</ref> Three-time champion [[James Wade]] survived a deciding leg of his own as he triumphed against [[Chris Dobey]], making him one of six former champions to advance to the fifth round, alongside Littler, Van Gerwen, Noppert, Wright, and Anderson. [[Gabriel Clemens]] landed a 145 checkout to beat [[Richard Veenstra]] 10–9, joining [[Martin Schindler]], [[Dominik Grüllich]] and Kai Gotthardt as the four German players in the fifth round.<ref name="PDCDay1"/>

=== Fifth and sixth rounds ===
[[File:2025-04-03 Premier League Darts Berlin 2025 by Sandro Halank–172.jpg|thumb|[[Gerwyn Price]] ''(pictured in 2025)'' came from 9–4 behind to defeat [[Keane Barry]] 10–9 in the sixth round.]]
The fifth round (afternoon session) and sixth round (evening session) were played on 7 March.<ref name="PDCDay2">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=7 March 2026 |title=Price battles back from the brink to reach UK Open last eight |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/march/07/Price-battles-back-from-brink/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=8 March 2026}}</ref> After getting past [[Kevin Doets]] 10–8, Luke Littler faced Gary Anderson, who averaged 105.80 to defeat Martin Schindler 10–7. Anderson took the first leg but Littler then took a 4–1 lead, extending it to 7–3 before eventually securing a 10–5 victory.<ref>{{cite web |last=van Wijk |first=Jim |date=7 March 2026 |title=Luke Littler stays on track to defend UK Open title by beating Gary Anderson |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/sport/luke-littler-gary-anderson-scores-rob-cross-world-b2934075.html |website=The Independent |access-date=8 March 2026}}</ref> Danny Noppert followed a 10–2 win over Peter Wright by eliminating Luke Humphries, triumphing 10–6 in his first major victory over the world number two. On his title chances, Noppert said: "I have lifted it once, why not twice?"<ref>{{cite web |last=Wellens |first=Megan |date=7 March 2026 |title=UK Open 2026: Danny Noppert defeats Luke Humphries to set up quarter-final with Luke Littler as Gerwyn Price, James Wade also through |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13516515/uk-open-2026-danny-noppert-defeats-luke-humphries-to-set-up-quarter-final-with-luke-littler-as-gerwyn-price-james-wade-also-through |website=Sky Sports |access-date=8 March 2026}}</ref> Celebrating his 41st birthday, Gerwyn Price beat Wessel Nijman 10–5 in the fifth round before producing a comeback from 9–4 down to defeat [[Keane Barry]] 10–9. "It was a frustrating game at times," commented Price, who said he was thankful to get through the match and that the crowd's support for him was "like playing in Wales".<ref name="PDCDay2"/>

James Wade beat Gabriel Clemens 10–2 and advanced to the quarter-finals by defeating fellow three-time champion Michael van Gerwen 10–3, averaging 105.83 in the latter contest. Premier League leader [[Jonny Clayton]] hit eight [[Glossary of darts#Maximum|maximums]] on his way to beating [[Martin Lukeman]] 10–7, the same scoreline as his fifth-round win over Darryl Pilgrim. Rob Cross defeated former [[World Grand Prix (darts)|World Grand Prix]] champions [[Mike De Decker]] and Daryl Gurney to reach his first major quarter-final since November 2024; De Decker missed a match dart as Cross overturned a 9–7 deficit to win 10–9. Josh Rock achieved consecutive 10–7 victories against Dominik Grüllich and fellow Premier League player [[Stephen Bunting]], while [[Krzysztof Ratajski]] reached his second UK Open quarter-final with wins over [[Oskar Lukasiak]] and [[Ryan Searle (darts player)|Ryan Searle]].<ref name="PDCDay2"/><ref>{{cite web |date=7 March 2026 |title=Littler into UK Open quarter-finals but Humphries out |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/clygpw1g5vno |website=BBC Sport |access-date=8 March 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Gill |first=Samuel |date=7 March 2026 |title=UK Open 2026 Saturday afternoon Round-Up - Luke Littler survives scare as Luke Humphries thrashes Dave Chisnall |url=https://dartsnews.com/pdc/uk-open-2026-saturday-afternoon-round-up-luke-littler-survives-scare-as-luke-humphries-thrashes-dave-chisnall |website=dartsnews.com |access-date=8 March 2026}}</ref>

=== Quarter-finals and semi-finals===
[[File:2022-06-13 Play-offs (2022 Premier League Darts) by Sandro Halank–085.jpg|thumb|Three-time champion [[James Wade]] ''(pictured in 2022)'' reached his fifth UK Open final.]]
The quarter-finals (afternoon session) and semi-finals (evening session) were played on 8 March.<ref name="PDCDay3">{{cite web |last=Simpson |first=Will |date=8 March 2026 |title=Littler downs Wade to claim consecutive UK Open titles |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/march/08/Littler-downs-Wade-to-claim-consecutive-UK-Open-titles/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=9 March 2026}}</ref> In the quarter-finals, Josh Rock secured a place in his second consecutive UK Open semi-final by defeating Krzysztof Ratajski 10–7, having trailed 6–4 after 10 legs. James Wade landed a 107 checkout to win a deciding leg against Rob Cross. Luke Littler claimed five of the last six legs of his match against Danny Noppert, winning 10–6 to continue his title defence. To complete the final four, Gerwyn Price defeated his World Cup teammate Jonny Clayton 10–8, reaching his first UK Open semi-final since 2021.<ref name="PDCDay3"/><ref>{{cite web |last=Gill |first=Samuel |date=8 March 2026 |title=UK Open Quarter-Finals Round-Up - Gerwyn Price takes down Jonny Clayton in all-Welsh battle as semi-finalists set |url=https://dartsnews.com/pdc/uk-open-quarter-finals-round-up-gerwyn-price-takes-down-jonny-clayton-in-all-welsh-battle-as-same-semi-finalists-as-2025-set |website=dartsnews.com |access-date=9 March 2026}}</ref>

In the semi-finals, James Wade faced Gerwyn Price and Luke Littler faced Josh Rock. Price took an early 3–2 lead over Wade, who levelled the score at 5–5 with a 136 checkout. From there, Wade took control to lead 9–5, a run of legs that included a 167 checkout. Although Price reduced his deficit to 10–8, Wade took the next leg to complete an 11–8 victory, finishing the match with 11 maximums and a three-dart average of 105.53. Rock established a 5–1 lead against Littler in the second semi-final, capitalising on Littler only hitting one of seventeen attempts at double in the early stages of the match. Littler won the next four legs to level at 5–5. Rock went two legs away from victory at 9–7 but Littler equalised once again, finding a [[Glossary of darts#Break|break of throw]] in the next leg to lead before winning 11–9. Littler extended his unbeaten record in major semi-finals to 16, while also making it seven wins out of seven meetings against Rock.<ref name="SkyFinal">{{cite web |last=Wellens |first=Megan |date=8 March 2026 |title=UK Open 2026: Luke Littler crowned back-to-back champion with victory over James Wade in final |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13516983/uk-open-2026-luke-littler-crowned-back-to-back-champion-with-victory-over-james-wade-in-final |website=Sky Sports |access-date=9 March 2026}}</ref><ref name="BBCFinal">{{cite web |last=Drury |first=Sam |date=8 March 2026 |title=Littler 'digs deep' to go back-to-back at UK Open |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cn8792njdylo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=9 March 2026}}</ref><ref name="ESPNFinal">{{cite web |date=8 March 2026 |title=UK Open darts: Luke Littler overcomes James Wade to defend title |url=https://www.espn.com/darts/story/_/id/48147287/uk-open-darts-luke-littler-overcomes-james-wade-defend-title |website=ESPN |access-date=9 March 2026}}</ref>

=== Final ===
[[File:Luke Littler with UK Open trophy 2025.png|thumb|[[Luke Littler]] ''(pictured with the UK Open trophy in 2025)'' became the fourth player to defend the UK Open title.]]
The final between Luke Littler and James Wade was played in the evening session on 8 March. This was a rematch of the [[2025 UK Open|previous year's final]], where Littler won his first UK Open title by defeating Wade 11–2.<ref name="2025Final"/> The pair also contested the [[2025 World Matchplay|World Matchplay]] final in July 2025, where Littler also triumphed.<ref>{{cite web |last=Blanche |first=Phil |date=28 July 2025 |title=Luke Littler beats James Wade to win first World Matchplay crown in Blackpool |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/sport/darts/luke-littler-james-wade-blackpool-sky-sports-phil-taylor-b2797006.html |website=The Independent |access-date=9 March 2026}}</ref> Competing in his fifth UK Open final, Wade aimed to be crowned champion for the fourth time after winning the tournament in [[2008 UK Open|2008]], [[2011 UK Open|2011]] and [[2021 UK Open|2021]], while Littler looked to retain his title in his second straight final.<ref name="SkyFinal"/><ref>{{cite web |last=Barry |first=Brian |date=7 March 2021 |title=UK Open Darts: James Wade produces brilliant best to claim title and move back into world's top four |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/12238727/uk-open-darts-james-wade-produces-brilliant-best-to-claim-title-and-move-back-into-worlds-top-four |website=Sky Sports |access-date=9 March 2026}}</ref>

Littler began the match by taking a 3–0 lead, but Wade levelled at 3–3 despite averaging around 80.<ref name="SkyFinal"/><ref name="BBCFinal"/> The defending champion responded with another three-leg extension, which Wade matched for 6–6 before they traded legs to bring the score to 7–7.<ref name="ESPNFinal"/> The match was characterised by the 12-point difference in averages between the two during the early proceedings, as Littler contrasted his 100 average with poor doubling that saw Wade keep himself in the contest.<ref name="PDCDay3"/><ref name="SkyFinal"/><ref name="BBCFinal"/> In the 15th leg, Wade missed a shot at the [[Bullseye (target)|bullseye]] to take the lead, allowing Littler to convert a 116 checkout to restore initiative.<ref name="ESPNFinal"/> He broke throw in the 16th leg and completed a run of four consecutive legs to win the match 11–7.<ref name="SkyFinal"/> Littler finished the final with a three-dart average of 99.58, hitting 35.48 per cent of his double attempts.<ref name="BBCFinal"/>

Littler defended his title to win his second UK Open, his fifth consecutive major title. The victory saw him reach a total of 16 PDC major titles, surpassing Wade in the [[Professional Darts Corporation#Roll of Honour|all-time list]] to move into third place, behind [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]] and Michael van Gerwen.<ref name="BBCFinal"/><ref name="SL">{{cite web |last=Hammer |first=Chris |date=8 March 2026 |title=Darts results: Luke Littler makes more history as he defeats James Wade to defend his UK Open title in Minehead |url=https://www.sportinglife.com/darts/news/darts-results-luke-littler-makes-more-history-as-he-defeats-james-wade-to-defend-his-uk-open-title-in-minehead/230793 |website=Sporting Life |access-date=9 March 2026}}</ref> He also became the fourth player to retain the UK Open, after Raymond van Barneveld, Taylor, and Van Gerwen, and was the first to achieve the feat since Van Gerwen in [[2016 UK Open|2016]].<ref name="PDCDay3"/><ref name="ESPNFinal"/> "It hasn't been my best tournament average-wise, but as a player you've got to dig deep," said Littler after the final.<ref name="ESPNFinal"/> On his opponent's performance, he stated: "That is what James [Wade] does, he is always behind you and I said to him after the game that if he had hit those double tops [double 20] then it is a completely different game."<ref name="SkyFinal"/> Speaking in defeat, Wade said that Littler was "too good" for him at the end, commenting: "He [Littler]’s the best player in the world at the moment, and unfortunately I’ve lost to him in three finals." He added: "I'm happy to be in the final. I hate losing more than anyone, but I lost to the better player on the day."<ref name="PDCDay3"/>
