The '''2025 Wuhan Open''' (officially the '''2025 Optics Valley of China Wuhan Open''')<ref>{{cite web |title=OVC to hold 2025 China World Snooker Wuhan Open |url=https://english.wuhan.gov.cn/H_1/NWP/202508/t20250801_2627882.shtml |website=wuhan.gov.cn |date=1 August 2025 |access-date=19 August 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416141844/https://english.wuhan.gov.cn/H_1/NWP/202508/t20250801_2627882.shtml|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 24 to 30 August 2025 at the [[Optics Valley of China|Optics Valley Gymnasium]], [[Huazhong University of Science and Technology]] in [[Wuhan]], China. The qualifiers took place from 22 to 24 June at the [[Leicester Arena]] in [[Leicester]], England. The third consecutive edition of the tournament since it was first staged in [[2023 Wuhan Open (snooker)|2023]], it was the third [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season]], following the [[2025 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters]] and preceding the [[2025 English Open (snooker)|2025 English Open]]. The tournament was broadcast by local channels in China and elsewhere in Asia; by [[Discovery+]] and [[HBO Max]] in Europe; by [[TNT Sports]] and Discovery+ in the United Kingdom and Ireland; and by [[WST Play]] in all other territories. The winner received £140,000 from a total prize fund of £700,000. 

[[Luca Brecel]], the [[2023 World Snooker Championship|2023 World Champion]], failed to appear for his qualifying match in Leicester. [[Neil Robertson]] and [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]], the world numbers three and four, withdrew from the event before the main stage in Wuhan, as did [[Ryan Day (snooker player)|Ryan Day]]. [[Xiao Guodong]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Si Jiahui]] 10–7 in the [[2024 Wuhan Open (snooker)|2024 final]]. Xiao retained the title, defeating [[Gary Wilson (snooker player)|Gary Wilson]] 10–9 in the final to win the second ranking tournament of his professional career. The fourth player to retain his maiden ranking title—after [[John Parrott]], [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]], and Gary Wilson—he advanced to a career high of eighth in the world rankings following his win. By reaching the final, Wilson re-entered the top 16, while [[Shaun Murphy]] fell out of the top 16 for the first time in 19 years.

The tournament produced 90 [[century break]]s—29 in the qualifiers held in Leicester and 61 at the main stage in Wuhan—of which the highest were two [[maximum break]]s. Xiao made the second maximum of his career during his held-over qualifying match against [[Mink Nutcharut]], and [[Thepchaiya Un-Nooh]] made the sixth maximum of his career in his last-64 match against [[Pang Junxu]]. The maximums were respectively the sixth and seventh of the season and the 223rd and 224th official maximums in professional snooker history.

==Overview==
The tournament was the third consecutive edition of the [[Wuhan Open (snooker)|Wuhan Open]], which was first held in [[2023 Wuhan Open (snooker)|2023]]. The inaugural champion was [[Judd Trump]], who won the 2023 event with a 10–7 victory over [[Ali Carter]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=15 October 2023 |title=Wuhan Open: Judd Trump beat Ali Carter to seal back-to-back titles |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/67117784 |access-date=21 August 2025 |work=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416141837/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/67117784|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> The defending champion was [[Xiao Guodong]], who won the first ranking tournament of his professional career at the [[2024 Wuhan Open (snooker)|2024 edition]], defeating [[Si Jiahui]] 10–7 in the final.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-10-12 |title=Wuhan Open: Xiao wins Wuhan Open to end 17-year title wait |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c4gr71zrymvo |access-date=2026-04-16 |website=BBC Sport |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416141920/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c4gr71zrymvo|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

The main stage of the event took place from 24 to 30 August 2025 at the [[Optics Valley of China|Optics Valley]] Gymnasium, [[Huazhong University of Science and Technology]], in [[Wuhan]], China.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |title=Tournaments 2025–26 |url=https://www.wst.tv/tournaments |access-date=2025-07-20 |website=World Snooker Tour |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416142223/https://www.wst.tv/tournaments/|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Wuhan Open 2025 tickets |url=https://www.wst.tv/tickets/wuhan-open-2025-tickets |access-date=15 August 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416142236/https://www.wst.tv/tickets/wuhan-open-2025-tickets/|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> Qualifiers were held from 22 to 24 June at the [[Leicester Arena]] in [[Leicester]], England.<ref name="s.o_calendar">{{cite web |title=Calendar 2025/2026|url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?template=2&season=2025 |access-date=19 August 2025 |website=snooker.org|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416142249/https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?template=2&season=2025|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> The tournament was the third [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season|2025–26 snooker season]], following the [[2025 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters]] and preceding the [[2025 English Open (snooker)|2025 English Open]].<ref name="s.o_calendar" />

===Format===
All matches up to and including the quarter-finals were played as the best of nine {{cuegloss|frame|frames}}. The semi-finals were the best of 11 frames, and the final was the best of 19 frames, played over two {{cuegloss|session|sessions}}.<ref name="s.o_main">{{cite web |title=Optics Valley of China Wuhan Open 2025 |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2333 |website=snooker.org |access-date=20 August 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416142517/https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2333|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Qualifiers ===
In the qualifiers held at the [[Leicester Arena]] from 22 to 24 June 2025, [[Class of '92 (snooker)|Class of '92]] players [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] and [[John Higgins]], both of whom had recently turned 50, began their 34th professional season with wins. Williams recorded a [[Whitewash (sport)|whitewash]] over [[Chatchapong Nasa]] while Higgins defeated [[Huang Jiahao]] 5–2. [[Reanne Evans]], a 12-time [[World Women's Snooker Championship|World Women's Champion]], trailed [[David Lilley (snooker player)|David Lilley]], a former [[World Seniors Championship|World Seniors Champion]], by two {{Cuegloss|frame|frames}} at 1–3, but she recovered to win the match in a {{Cuegloss|deciding frame}}. The previous year's runner-up, [[Si Jiahui]], defeated [[Bulcsú Révész]] 5–2. [[Joe O'Connor (snooker player)|Joe O'Connor]] and [[Elliot Slessor]] both recovered from 2–4 behind to beat their respective opponents, [[Jiang Jun]] and [[Oliver Lines]], in deciding frames, with Slessor winning his decider on the last {{Cuegloss|pink ball|pink}} and {{Cuegloss|black ball|black}}. === Last 64 ===
[[File:Thepchaiya Un-Nooh at Snooker German Masters (DerHexer) 2013-01-30 21.jpg|thumb|[[Thepchaiya Un-Nooh]] ''(pictured in 2013)'' made the sixth [[maximum break]] of his career and his second of the season.]]

Williams won the first three frames against 19-year-old [[Liam Davies (snooker player)|Liam Davies]] with consecutive century breaks of 125, 100, and 137 and also took the fourth for a 4–0 lead. Davies attempted a comeback, making breaks including 61 and 94 as he won three consecutive frames, but Williams secured a 5–3 victory with a 70 break in frame eight. Williams praised his opponent afterwards, saying: "He never threw the towel in and he made some great breaks to get to 4–3." [[Barry Hawkins]] won a deciding frame on the {{Cuegloss|colours}} to defeat [[Jamie Jones (snooker player)|Jamie Jones]]. Five of Hawkins's last eight professional matches had gone to deciding frames, including 5–6 losses to [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] at the [[2025 Shanghai Masters]] and Wakelin at the [[2025 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters]]. "I felt all over the place and had no confidence at all," he said afterwards. "Every ball felt like a pint of blood. What I do is just battle and battle." [[Mark Selby]] advanced with a 5–2 win over [[Michael Holt (snooker player)|Michael Holt]].<ref name=":0" /> Fu also progressed, defeating White by the same score.<ref name=":1" />

[[Thepchaiya Un-Nooh]] defeated [[Pang Junxu]] 5–1, making the sixth maximum break of his career in the final frame of the match. It was {{nowrap|Un-Nooh's}} second maximum of the season, following his 147 at the 2025 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters. "When I arrived in Wuhan I made two on the practice tables," said Un-Nooh afterwards. "That gave me a lot of confidence to make one. Everything was open and the balls were perfect. I knew I could do it like I did when I was practising." The maximums by Xiao and Un-Nooh at the event were the sixth and seventh of the season and the 223rd and 224th in professional snooker history.<ref name="Max 224">{{cite web |title=Un-Nooh makes second 147 of 2025 Wuhan Open |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/25/un-nooh-makes-second-147-of-2025-wuhan-open-1/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=25 August 2025 |access-date=25 August 2025 |archive-date=4 September 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250904085936/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/25/un-nooh-makes-second-147-of-2025-wuhan-open-1/ |url-status=live}}</ref> The world number seven [[Ding Junhui]] made a 117 break to win the first frame against 18-year-old [[Stan Moody]], the world number 61. However, Moody made breaks of 64, 65, 53, and 131 as he won five of the next seven frames for a 5–3 victory. "It was a fantastic experience," said Moody, commenting on playing a Chinese favourite. "I knew what it would be like, with the crowd clapping every shot. When he got {{Cuegloss|fluke|flukes}} they were cheering. I tried to enjoy it, view myself as the [[underdog]] and take the pressure off." Higgins advanced with a 5–1 win over [[Dylan Emery]] but commented afterwards that he was "struggling for motivation" after celebrating his 50th birthday with a summer holiday in [[Dallas]]. [[Aaron Hill (snooker player)|Aaron Hill]], a quarter-finalist at the [[2023 Wuhan Open (snooker)|2023 edition]], advanced with a 5–2 victory over [[Kyren Wilson]], the world number two. "[Wilson] is probably the best player in the world at the moment, so I knew I had to play my best snooker to win and I did that," commented Hill afterwards. Trump defeated [[Liu Hongyu (snooker player)|Liu Hongyu]] 5–1, and Murphy whitewashed [[Jordan Brown (snooker player)|Jordan Brown]].<ref name=":4">{{Cite web |date=25 August 2025 |title=Wuhan Open day two: Moody stuns home hero Ding |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/25/wuhan-open-day-two--moody-stuns-home-hero-ding/ |access-date=25 August 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416144507/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/25/wuhan-open-day-two--moody-stuns-home-hero-ding/|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Last 32 ===
[[File:Shaun Murphy PHC 2015-3.jpg|thumb|[[Shaun Murphy]] ''(pictured in 2015)'' lost to [[Gary Wilson (snooker player)|Gary Wilson]] in the last 32. He fell to 17th in the [[Snooker world rankings|world rankings]], after spending 19 continuous years in the top 16.]]

Xiao made breaks including 103 and 90 as he won three in a row to move one from victory at 9–8, but Wilson forced a deciding frame with a 52 break. After Xiao made a break of 48 in the decider, Wilson had a scoring opportunity but he missed a pot on a red. Xiao secured the frame to win 10–9 and become only the fourth player—after [[John Parrott]], Allen, and Gary Wilson—to retain his maiden ranking title.<ref name=":16" /><ref name=":17">{{cite web |title=Xiao defends Wuhan crown |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/30/xiao-defends-wuhan-crown/ |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=30 August 2025 |access-date=30 August 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416152712/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/30/xiao-defends-wuhan-crown/|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=30 August 2025 |title=Snooker: Xiao edges out Wilson to retain Wuhan Open title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c0r7ldpj1zno |access-date=16 April 2026|website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416153214/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c0r7ldpj1zno|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> He advanced from 14th to a career high of 8th in the world rankings.<ref name=":18">{{Cite web |date=1 September 2025 |title=Johnstone's Paint world rankings update: Xiao leaps to eighth |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/september/01/johnstone-s-paint-world-rankings-update--xiao-leaps-to-eighth/ |access-date=1 September 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260416152639/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/september/01/johnstone-s-paint-world-rankings-update--xiao-leaps-to-eighth/|archive-date=16 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

"Winning a first ranking title is hard, winning a second is even harder," said Xiao afterwards. "I've been a professional for 17, now 18 years, so I'm not a young player anymore. As an experienced player, winning two ranking titles at this stage feels a bit late, but in another way the timing is just right. Over the years I've built up experience, and I think this breakthrough is the result of all the work and the lessons I've learned."<ref name=":17" /> As runner-up, Wilson advanced from 18th to 16th in the rankings after the tournament.<ref name=":18" /> He said after the final: "[Xiao] having the majority of the support doesn't bring pressure. If anything it should, if you have a competitive nature like I have, spur you on to try and upset that. It didn't happen today. Now that it is finished and there is nothing more I can do about it, I just want to say well done to Xiao. I know how it feels to defend a title and he has done that in his home tournament."<ref name=":17" />
