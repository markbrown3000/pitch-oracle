The '''2026 World Matchplay''' (known for sponsorship reasons as the '''2026 Betfred World Matchplay''') was a professional [[darts]] tournament that took place from 18 to 26 July 2026 at the [[Winter Gardens, Blackpool|Winter Gardens]] in [[Blackpool]], England. It was the 33rd staging of the [[World Matchplay (darts)|World Matchplay]] by the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC). The event's prize fund was an increased total of £1,000,000, with the winner receiving £225,000. The [[2026 Women's World Matchplay|fifth edition]] of the [[Women's World Matchplay]] also took place on 26 July and was won by [[Beau Greaves]].

Sponsored by [[Betfred]], the tournament featured 32 players, consisting of the top 16 players in the two-year [[PDC World Rankings]] and the top 16 players from the one-year [[PDC Pro Tour]] rankings who had not yet qualified. [[Kevin Doets]], [[William O'Connor (darts player)|William O'Connor]], [[Niko Springer]] and [[Niels Zonneveld]] made their debuts at the event. The [[2021 World Matchplay|2021]] champion [[Peter Wright (darts player)|Peter Wright]] missed out on qualification for the first time since 2012, while [[2007 World Matchplay|2007]] champion [[James Wade]] qualified for the 21st consecutive year.

[[Luke Littler]] was the defending champion, having defeated Wade 18–13 in the [[2025 World Matchplay|2025 final]]. He hit the 11th [[nine-dart finish]] in World Matchplay history in the opening [[Leg (darts)|leg]] of his second-round match against [[Nathan Aspinall]]. He went on to reach his second straight final, a run that saw him become the first player to pass the £3 million mark in the PDC World Rankings. He faced [[Gerwyn Price]], who reached his second World Matchplay final.

Littler defended his title by defeating Price 18–9 to win the tournament for the second time. He became the fourth player to retain the [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]] Trophy and the first to do so since [[Michael van Gerwen]] in [[2016 World Matchplay|2016]]. He set new records for the highest [[three-dart average]] in a World Matchplay final and the highest winning tournament average in World Matchplay history, both previously held by Taylor. He also surpassed his own record for the most [[Glossary of darts#Maximum|maximums]] (180s) in a World Matchplay campaign, improving it from 64 to 66.

=== Format ===
The top 16 players in the [[PDC World Rankings]] were [[Seeding (sport)|seeded]], and were drawn to compete against the 16 qualifiers from the [[PDC Pro Tour]] rankings in the first round.<ref name="BBCFormat"/> All matches were in [[Leg (darts)|leg]] play format. All matches had to be won by two clear legs; if the players could not be separated, the match was decided by a [[Sudden death (sport)|sudden-death]] leg.<ref name="SkyF">{{cite web |date=12 July 2026 |title=World Matchplay Darts 2026: Blackpool dates, draw, format, favourites and prize money for tournament at Winter Gardens |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13562772/world-matchplay-darts-2026-blackpool-dates-draw-format-favourites-and-prize-money-for-tournament-at-winter-gardens |website=Sky Sports |access-date=25 July 2026}}</ref>

The number of legs required to win increased as the tournament progressed:<ref name="BBCFormat">{{cite web |last=Colman |first=Jonty |date=16 July 2026 |title=World Matchplay Darts 2026 - Schedule, draw and prize money |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/c93204p8y4eo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=25 July 2025}}</ref><ref name="SkyF"/>
* '''First round:''' Best of 19 legs (sudden death at 12–12)
* '''Second round:''' Best of 21 legs (sudden death at 13–13)
* '''Quarter-finals:''' Best of 31 legs (sudden death at 18–18)
* '''Semi-finals:''' Best of 33 legs (sudden death at 19–19)
* '''Final:''' Best of 35 legs (sudden death at 20–20)

== Summary ==
=== First round ===
[[File:2026-03-26 Premier League Darts – Night 8 – Berlin 2026 by Sandro Halank–085.jpg|thumb|In the first round, ninth seed [[Stephen Bunting]] ''(pictured in 2026)'' won a [[Sudden death (sport)|sudden-death]] leg against debutant [[Niels Zonneveld]], who missed three [[Glossary of darts#Match dart|match darts]].]]
The first round (best of 19 [[Leg (darts)|legs]]) began on 18 July.<ref name="PDC_18J">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=18 July 2026 |title=Imperious Littler sets up Aspinall showdown in Blackpool |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/july/18/imperious-littler-sets-aspinall-showdown-blackpool/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=19 July 2026}}</ref> Reigning champion and world number one [[Luke Littler]] began his title defence with a 10–6 victory over German debutant [[Niko Springer]]. The Englishman trailed Springer 3–2 at the first interval, but won five of the last six legs from 5–5 to secure the win.<ref name="BBC_18J">{{cite web |last=Battison |first=Paul |date=18 July 2026 |title=Littler starts World Matchplay defence with Springer win |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cp9e5ge3e3eo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=19 July 2026}}</ref> Littler, who ended the match with a [[three-dart average]] of 109.53, found it "annoying" to not have the lead heading into the second break but was satisfied with his performance from then on, stating: "From leg 11 I was on 120 average which goes to show how well I was playing. If I get better I'll be happier."<ref name="Sky_18J">{{cite web |last=Mirza |first=Raz |date=19 July 2026 |title=World Matchplay Darts: Luke Littler kicks off defence of title in stunning fashion as Stephen Bunting survives huge scare |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13564693/world-matchplay-darts-luke-littler-kicks-off-defence-of-title-in-stunning-fashion-as-stephen-bunting-survives-huge-scare |website=Sky Sports |access-date=19 July 2026}}</ref> The [[2023 World Matchplay|2023]] champion [[Nathan Aspinall]] averaged 105.32 and landed a 167 [[Glossary of darts#Checkout|checkout]] as he defeated [[Joe Cullen (darts player)|Joe Cullen]] 10–5 to set up a meeting with Littler in the second round.<ref name="Sky_18J"/> [[Stephen Bunting]] led [[Niels Zonneveld]] 8–5 before the Dutch debutant completed back-to-back 10-dart legs and converted a 164 checkout to level the match at 8–8. Zonneveld went on to miss three [[Glossary of darts#Match dart|match darts]] as the pair entered a [[Sudden death (sport)|sudden-death]] leg, where Bunting sealed a 13–12 win.<ref>{{cite web |date=18 July 2026 |title=Luke Littler wins World Matchplay opener after 'annoying' setback |url=https://www.rte.ie/sport/darts/2026/0718/1584029-littler-wins-opener-after-annoying-setback-in-blackpool/ |website=RTÉ Sport |access-date=19 July 2026 |agency=Press Association}}</ref> [[Josh Rock]] was a 10–4 winner against [[Luke Woodhouse]], punishing Woodhouse's 20 missed darts at [[Glossary of darts#Double|double]].<ref name="Sky_18J"/>

Three-time champion [[Michael van Gerwen]] initially went 4–1 behind against [[Andrew Gilding]], but the Dutchman won nine of the last 11 legs to prevail 10–6. On missing double 12 for a [[nine-dart finish]] during the match, Van Gerwen claimed the dart slipped out of his hand, adding: "For me as a dart player, it doesn't really matter if it's nine darts or it's 30 darts."<ref name="RTE_19J">{{cite web |date=20 July 2026 |title=Gary Anderson storms into second round at World Matchplay |url=https://www.rte.ie/sport/darts/2026/0720/1584120-anderson-storms-into-second-round-at-world-matchplay/ |website=RTÉ Sport |access-date=20 July 2026 |agency=Press Association}}</ref> The [[2018 World Matchplay|2018]] champion [[Gary Anderson (darts player)|Gary Anderson]] averaged 109.96 and compiled finishes of 144 and 141 in his 10–2 victory over [[Ryan Joyce]], winning five consecutive legs to close the match.<ref name="BBC_19J">{{cite web |last=Battison |first=Paul |date=19 July 2026 |title=Anderson thrashes Joyce at World Matchplay |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cr59mr5yq3eo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=20 July 2026}}</ref> [[Chris Dobey]] hit 13 [[Glossary of darts#Maximum|maximums]] during his contest with [[Dirk van Duijvenbode]], but the Englishman missed four match darts before entering a [[tie-break]] scenario at 9–9, where Van Duijvenbode achieved the two clear legs required to win 13–11 and eliminate the 13th seed.<ref name="PDC_19J">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=19 July 2026 |title=Inspired Anderson produces vintage display at Betfred World Matchplay |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/july/19/inspired-anderson-produces-vintage-display-betfred-world-matchplay/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=20 July 2026}}</ref> [[Jonny Clayton]] converted a 144 checkout to complete a 10–7 win against [[Damon Heta]], advancing to a second-round match against Anderson, who Clayton called his "hero".<ref name="RTE_19J"/>

[[File:2025-04-03 Premier League Darts Berlin 2025 by Sandro Halank–082.jpg|thumb|The [[2019 World Matchplay|2019]] champion [[Rob Cross]] ''(pictured in 2025)'' was one of three unseeded players to win in the first round.]]

Littler established a 4–1 lead in the final's opening session, including a 167 checkout, which was his first bullseye finish of the tournament.<ref name="Sky_Final">{{cite web |last=Stafford |first=Ali |date=26 July 2026 |title=World Matchplay Darts: Luke Littler thrashes Gerwyn Price 18-9 to defend title with record-breaking display in Blackpool |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13567159/world-matchplay-darts-luke-littler-thrashes-gerwyn-price-18-9-to-defend-title-with-record-breaking-display-in-blackpool |website=Sky Sports |access-date=27 July 2026}}</ref> From 7–3 in front, Littler completed the next four legs in a total of 47 darts, hitting checkouts of 128 and 127 on his way to an 11–3 lead.<ref name="PDC_Final"/> Price won six of the next 10 legs to cut the gap to 15–9. Littler then converted a 148 checkout, his sixth and final [[Glossary of darts#Ton|ton]]-plus finish of the match, followed by a break of throw. He completed an 18–9 victory, his 10th consecutive win against Price, with an 81 checkout on the bullseye.<ref name="PDC_Final"/><ref name="Sky_Final"/> Littler finished the final with a three-dart average of 111.53, setting a new record for the highest average in a World Matchplay final, beating Phil Taylor's 111.23 average in the [[2013 World Matchplay|2013 final]].<ref name="BBC_Final">{{cite web |last=Matthews |first=Callum |date=26 July 2026 |title=Littler wins Matchplay and smashes Taylor record |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cwym032rlkvo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=27 July 2026}}</ref> He hit 12 maximums to take his total count for the tournament to 66, improving on his record of 64 from the previous year.<ref name="SL_Final">{{cite web |last1=Hammer |first1=Chris |date=26 July 2026 |title=The highest winning tournament averages of all time as Luke Littler goes in at number two behind Phil Taylor with record-breaking World Matchplay win |url=https://www.sportinglife.com/darts/news/the-highest-winning-tournament-averages-of-all-time-as-luke-littler-goes-in-at-number-two-behind-phil-taylor-with-record-breaking-world-matchplay-win/233452 |website=Sporting Life |access-date=27 July 2026}}</ref> Price averaged 104.92 in defeat, which became the second-highest losing average in a World Matchplay final.<ref name="PDC_Final"/>

Littler won his second World Matchplay title and his 14th PDC major singles title.<ref name="SL_Final"/> The 19-year-old continued his streak of claiming ranking major titles in 2026, having already won the [[2026 PDC World Darts Championship|World Championship]], [[2026 PDC World Masters|World Masters]], and the [[2026 UK Open|UK Open]], along with the unranked Premier League and [[2026 PDC World Cup of Darts|World Cup]].<ref name="PDC_Final"/><ref name="BBC_Final"/> He became the fourth player to retain the title, after [[Rod Harrington]], Taylor, and Van Gerwen, as well the first to achieve the feat since Van Gerwen in [[2016 World Matchplay|2016]].<ref name="BBC_Final"/><ref>{{cite web |last=Parker |first=Ian |date=26 July 2026 |title=Clean sweep? Luke Littler adds World Matchplay to World Championship, UK Open, Premier League and Masters haul |url=https://www.independent.ie/sport/other-sports/darts/clean-sweep-luke-littler-adds-world-matchplay-to-world-championship-uk-open-premier-league-and-masters-haul/a/159228030.html |website=Irish Independent |access-date=27 July 2026}}</ref> He finished the competition with an overall tournament average of 111.04, surpassing Taylor's 106.31 mark in 2010 as the highest winning tournament average in World Matchplay history.<ref name="PDC_Final"/><ref name="Sky_Final"/><ref name="SL_Final"/> Littler's run to the final resulted in him becoming the first player to pass £3 million in the PDC World Rankings; he received the top prize of £225,000 for the victory, taking his total ranking to £3,143,500.<ref name="BBC_Final"/> Speaking after the final, Littler called his World Matchplay campaign "one of the best weeks of [his] life" where he "played some of the best darts of [his] life".<ref>{{cite web |date=26 July 2026 |title=Luke Littler hails World Matchplay defence as one of the 'best weeks of his life' after historic Gerwyn Price victory |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13567163/luke-littler-hails-world-matchplay-defence-as-one-of-best-week-of-his-life-after-historic-victory-over-gerwyn-price |website=Sky Sports |access-date=27 July 2026}}</ref> He discussed the possibility of winning all PDC major titles in 2026, reiterating his promise to participate in [[2026 PDC Pro Tour#Players Championships|Pro Tour events]] in order to qualify for the [[Players Championship Finals]] in November. "I don't think my dad's too happy because we were going to go to Spain and I think we've got to go to [[Leicester]] now and play some Pro Tours, but it is what it is," he said.<ref name="BBC_Final"/> Speaking in defeat, Price praised his final opponent, calling him "one in a million" and someone who is "fantastic for this sport". "I hope there is nobody else like him coming through, because he is ridiculous," he added.<ref name="Sky_Final"/>
