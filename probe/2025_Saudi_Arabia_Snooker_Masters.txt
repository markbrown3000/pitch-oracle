The '''2025 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters''' was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 8 to 16 August 2025 at Green Halls, [[Prince Abdullah Al-Faisal Sports City]], in [[Jeddah]], Saudi Arabia. The second and last edition of the tournament, which had first been staged in [[Riyadh]] in [[2024 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters|2024]], it was the second [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season]], following the [[2025 Championship League (ranking)|2025 Championship League]] and preceding the [[2025 Wuhan Open (snooker)|2025 Wuhan Open]]. It featured 144 participants, including 16 regional wildcard players. The tournament was broadcast by local channels in Saudi Arabia and by other broadcasters globally. The winner received £500,000 from a total prize fund of £2,302,000. 

[[Judd Trump]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] 10–9 in the [[2024 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters|2024 final]], but he lost 3–5 to [[Oliver Lines]] in the fifth round (the last 32). [[Neil Robertson]] led [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] 7–2 in the best-of-19-frame final. O'Sullivan took seven of the next eight frames to lead 9–8, but Robertson won the last two frames for a 10–9 victory, securing the 26th ranking title of his professional career. As in 2024, the final of the event went to a {{cuegloss|deciding frame}}.

The tournament produced 91 [[Century break|century breaks]], including three [[maximum break]]s. [[Thepchaiya Un-Nooh]] made the fifth maximum of his career in his third-round match against [[Jordan Brown (snooker player)|Jordan Brown]], and O'Sullivan made the 16th and 17th maximums of his career in his semi-final match against [[Chris Wakelin]]. O'Sullivan became the second player to make two maximums in the same match, following [[Jackson Page]] at the [[2025 World Snooker Championship|2025 World Championship]] qualifiers, and the first to make two maximums in the same session or on the same day. He won a £147,000 bonus for making two maximums across the season's four eligible tournaments and earned two-thirds of the event's £50,000 maximum break prize, with the other third going to Un-Nooh. The maximums were O'Sullivan's first in almost seven years—following his 15th official 147 at the [[2018 English Open (snooker)|2018 English Open]]—and made him the oldest player to record an official maximum break, at the age of 49 years and 253 days. The maximums made at the event were respectively the third, fourth, and fifth of the season and the 220th, 221st, and 222nd official maximums in professional snooker history. O'Sullivan also made the 1,300th century break of his career at the tournament, while [[Mark Selby]] made his 900th career century.

=== Participants ===
A total of 144 players entered the tournament, including 16 regional wildcard players. The wildcards included eleven players from Saudi Arabia (Omar Alajlani, Ayman Alamri, Saud Albaker, Fahad Alghamdi, Musaad Almisfir, Abdullah Alotayyani, Ziyad Alqabbani, Abdullah Alshammari, Ahmed Aseeri, Abdulraouf Saigh, and Musab Sayegh), two from Pakistan (Adeel Aqdus and Asif Mukhtar), and one each from Bahrain, Egypt, and the United Arab Emirates (Habib Subah Humood, Hesham Shawky, and Mohammed Al Joker respectively).<ref name="WST_announcement"/>

Due to a fixture clash with the [[2025 World Games]], which took place from 7 to 17 August in [[Chengdu]], China, the world number 13 [[Xiao Guodong]] and the reigning World Women's Champion [[Bai Yulu]] did not enter.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-07-21 |title=Xiao Guodong and Bai Yulu skip Saudi Arabia Masters to play in a home World Games |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/saudi-arabia-snooker-masters/xiao-guodong-and-bai-yulu-skip-big-money-saudi-arabia-snooker-masters-to-play-in-world-games-on-home-soil-5232889 |access-date=2025-07-24 |website=Totally Snookered |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423084324/https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/saudi-arabia-snooker-masters/xiao-guodong-and-bai-yulu-skip-big-money-saudi-arabia-snooker-masters-to-play-in-world-games-on-home-soil-5232889|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> Both players went on to win gold medals in [[snooker at the 2025 World Games]], in the men's event and [[Snooker at the 2025 World Games – women's six-red|women's event]] respectively. On giving up the opportunity to compete in the Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters, Xiao said: "For me, national honour comes above everything else. Any time I get the chance to represent my country, I will give it my all—my only goal is the gold medal."<ref>{{cite web |date=14 August 2025 |title=Xiao and Bai strike gold for China |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/14/xiao-and-bai-strike-gold-for-china/ |access-date=14 August 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423084328/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/14/xiao-and-bai-strike-gold-for-china/|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Luca Brecel]], the [[2023 World Snooker Championship|2023 World Champion]], withdrew from the tournament for medical reasons and his third-round opponent, [[Sanderson Lam]], received a walkover.<ref name="brecel_wd" />

=== Format ===
The tournament used a tiered format similar to the [[World Snooker Championship|World Championship]] and [[UK Championship]], with the top 16 players in the [[snooker world rankings]], as they stood after the [[2025 Championship League (ranking)|2025 Championship League]], being [[Seeding (sports)|seeded]] through to the last 32, the fifth round of the tournament.{{efn|This included [[Ali Carter]] (ranked 17th) because of the withdrawal of [[Xiao Guodong]] (ranked 13th).}} The first four rounds featured all lower-ranked players as well as the invited regional wildcards and [[2025 Q School#Q School Order of Merit|Q School top-ups]]. Players seeded 49 to 80 received byes into the second round, and players seeded 17 to 48 received byes into the third round.<ref name="s.o_main"/>

The first three rounds were played as the best of 7 {{cuegloss|frame|frames}} while the fourth and fifth rounds were the best of 9 frames. The last 16, quarter-finals and semi-finals were the best of 11 frames and the final was the best of 19 frames, played over two {{cuegloss|session|sessions}}.<ref name="s.o_main"/>

=== Second round (last 112) ===

Slessor took frame eight with a 64 break, but Robertson made a 93 break in the ninth to secure a 6–3 victory.<ref>{{Cite web |date=15 August 2025 |title=2025 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters: Neil Robertson v Elliot Slessor |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/3e175586-3b18-4a3a-806e-e814f16006d3 |access-date=15 August 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423085319/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/3e175586-3b18-4a3a-806e-e814f16006d3|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> "The start was crucial today. Facing Wakelin in the second semi-final, O'Sullivan made a maximum break in the opening frame, the 16th of his professional career and his first in almost seven years, following his 15th official 147 at the [[2018 English Open (snooker)|2018 English Open]]. O'Sullivan then made another {{Cuegloss|total clearance}} of 142 to win the second frame. Wakelin won frame three with a 70 break, but O'Sullivan took frame four, also with a 70 break, to lead 3–1 at the mid-session interval. Wakelin won the fifth frame with a 125 century and also took the sixth to tie the scores at 3–3. In frame seven, O'Sullivan made his second maximum break of the match and the 17th of his career. He became the second player, after Page in the 2025 World Championship qualifiers, to make two maximums in the same match and the first player to make two maximums in the same session or on the same day. === Final ===
[[File:Neil Robertson at Snooker German Masters (DerHexer) 2015-02-05 02.jpg|thumb|[[Neil Robertson]] ''(pictured in 2015)'' defeated [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] 10–9 in the final to claim his 26th ranking title and advance from eighth to third in the world rankings.]]
The final was played on 16 August as the best of 19 frames, held over two sessions, between the world number five O'Sullivan and the world number eight Robertson.<ref name=":5" /> O'Sullivan contested his 65th ranking final, while Robertson played in his 39th. O'Sullivan had won 19 of their 31 previous meetings.<ref name=":16" /> Julian Bell, who had officiated on the World Snooker Tour since 2018, refereed his first ranking event final.<ref>{{Cite web |date=15 August 2025 |title=For Whom the Bell Tolls: Julian set for first final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/15/For-Whom-The-Bell-Tolls--Julian-Set-For-First-Final/ |access-date=16 August 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423085150/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/15/For-Whom-The-Bell-Tolls--Julian-Set-For-First-Final/|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> Robertson won the opening frame with a 76 break. O'Sullivan took the second with a 92 break, but Robertson responded with breaks of 108 and 81 to lead 3–1 at the mid-session interval. O'Sullivan won frame five with a 67 break, but Robertson took the sixth with breaks of 41 and 34. In the seventh frame, Robertson made a 39 break after a safety error from O'Sullivan; he later made a {{Cuegloss|plant}} and compiled a frame-winning break of 40. In the eighth, O'Sullivan was on a break of 24 when he missed a {{Cuegloss|cut shot|cut}} on the {{cuegloss|blue ball|blue}} to the middle pocket. Robertson took the frame with a 61 break to end the first session with a 6–2 lead.<ref name=":16">{{Cite web |date=16 August 2025 |title=Robertson in control of Jeddah final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/16/Robertson-In-Control-Of-Jeddah-Final/ |access-date=16 August 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en |archive-date=22 August 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250822114553/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/16/Robertson-In-Control-Of-Jeddah-Final/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=":17">{{Cite web |date=16 August 2025 |title=2025 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters: Neil Robertson v Ronnie O'Sullivan |url=https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/3fd2233c-c27a-4c56-89f8-07c3788655a4 |access-date=16 August 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en |archive-date=25 August 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250825111117/https://www.wst.tv/match-centre/3fd2233c-c27a-4c56-89f8-07c3788655a4 |url-status=live }}</ref>

When play resumed, Robertson took frame nine with a 97 break to lead by five at 7–2, but O'Sullivan won the 10th with a 139 total clearance, the 1,300th century break of his professional career,<ref name="s.o_main" /><ref name=":18">{{Cite web |date=16 August 2025 |title=Robertson beats O'Sullivan in immense Saudi final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/16/Robertson-Beats-O-Sullivan-In-Immense-Saudi-Final/ |access-date=16 August 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en |archive-date=17 August 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250817075455/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/16/Robertson-Beats-O-Sullivan-In-Immense-Saudi-Final/ |url-status=live }}</ref> and then made breaks of 97, 89, 57, and 80 to tie the scores at 7–7. Robertson made a 75 break in the 15th to regain the lead at 8–7. O'Sullivan won the 16th frame on the colours after laying a snooker on the brown and also took the 17th with a 64 break for a 9–8 advantage. Having lost seven of the last eight frames, Robertson made a 101 century in frame 18 to tie the scores at 9–9, meaning that the final of the event went to a deciding frame for a second consecutive year.<ref name=":19" /> O'Sullivan made a 31 break in the decider but failed to split the {{Cuegloss|pack}}; he subsequently made a safety error, and Robertson won the frame and tournament with an 87 clearance.<ref name=":17" /><ref name=":18" /> It was Robertson's 26th career ranking title. His winner's prize of £500,000 took him from eighth to third in the world rankings, while O'Sullivan as runner-up received £200,000 and advanced from fifth to fourth in the rankings.<ref name=":19" /><ref name=":20" /> "This surpasses winning the [[World Snooker Championship|World Championship]]," said Robertson afterwards. "I have had to answer a lot of questions like whether I can still beat someone like [O'Sullivan] in a final of this magnitude." O'Sullivan commented: "[Robertson] deserved to win. He was the better player. I just tried to hang on to him and make a game of it."<ref name=":18" />
