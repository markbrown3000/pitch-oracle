The '''2026 World Snooker Championship''' (officially the '''2026 Halo World Snooker Championship''') was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 18&nbsp;April to 4&nbsp;May 2026 at the [[Crucible Theatre]] in [[Sheffield]], England, the 50th consecutive year that the [[World Snooker Championship]] was staged at the venue. Qualifying took place from 6 to 15 April at the [[English Institute of Sport, Sheffield|English Institute of Sport]] in Sheffield. The 18th and final [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season]], the tournament was broadcast domestically by [[BBC Sport]], in Europe by [[Eurosport]], and globally by other broadcasters. The winner received £500,000 from a total prize fund of £2,395,000.

The main stage featured 32 players, including a record 11 participants from mainland China. Four players, [[He Guoqiang (snooker player)|He Guoqiang]], [[Antoni Kowalski]], [[Stan Moody]], and [[Liam Pullen]], made their Crucible debuts. [[Zhao Xintong]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] 18–12 in the [[2025 World Snooker Championship|2025 final]], but he lost 10–13 to [[Shaun Murphy]] in the quarter-finals, becoming the 21st player to experience the so-called [[Crucible curse]]. The 14th {{Cuegloss|frame}} of the semi-final match between [[Wu Yize]] and [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]] lasted 100 minutes and 21 seconds, a new record for the longest frame played at the Crucible. After Allen missed a {{Cuegloss|match ball|match-ball}} {{Cuegloss|black ball|black}} off {{Cuegloss|black spot|its spot}} in the penultimate frame, Wu prevailed to win 17–16. Wu went on to defeat Murphy 18–17 in the final, becoming the second World Champion from Asia, following Zhao, and the tournament's second-youngest winner, behind only [[Stephen Hendry]].

The event produced a record 258 [[Century break|century breaks]], including a record 177 in qualifying, of which the highest was a [[maximum break]] by [[Chang Bingyu]] in the third qualifying round. It was the 24th maximum of the season, a new record. The main stage featured 81 centuries, of which the highest was a 145 by Allen in the semi-finals. 

== Background ==
[[File:The Crucible Theatre during the 2026 World Snooker Championship.jpg|alt=The Crucible Theatre from outside|thumb|For the 50th consecutive year, the main stage of the tournament was held at the [[Crucible Theatre]] ''(pictured during the event)'' in [[Sheffield]], England.]]

The inaugural [[1927 World Snooker Championship]], then known as the Professional Championship of Snooker, took place at various venues in England between November 1926 and May 1927. [[Joe Davis]] won the final, held at [[Camkin's Hall]] in [[Birmingham]] from 9 to 12&nbsp;May 1927, and went on to win all of the first 15 stagings of the tournament before retiring undefeated after the [[1946 World Snooker Championship|1946 edition]] (no tournaments were held from 1941 to 1945 because of [[World War II]]).<ref name="Everton-1986">{{cite book |last=Everton |first=Clive |title=The History of Snooker and Billiards |publisher=[[Transworld (publisher)|Partridge Press]] |location=[[Haywards Heath]] |date=1986 |isbn=978-1-85225-013-3 |page=50}}</ref><ref name="Turner-2008">{{cite web |last=Turner |first=Chris |title=World Professional Championship |url=http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/world.html |website=Chris Turner's Snooker Archive |year=2008 |access-date=31 March 2024 |archive-date=6 June 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160606102115/http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/world.html |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=11 July 1978 |title=Joe Davis, a snooker champion who retired unbeaten, Was 77 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1978/07/11/archives/joe-davis-a-snooker-champion-who-retired-unbeaten-was-77.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231022194142/https://www.nytimes.com/1978/07/11/archives/joe-davis-a-snooker-champion-who-retired-unbeaten-was-77.html |archive-date=22 October 2023 |access-date=31 March 2024 |work=[[The New York Times]] |language=en-US}}</ref> The tournament went into abeyance after only two players contested the [[1952 World Snooker Championship|1952 edition]], due to a dispute between the Professional Billiards Players' Association (PBPA) and the [[Billiards Association and Control Council]] (BACC). The PBPA established an alternative tournament, the [[World Professional Match-play Championship|World Professional {{nowrap|Match-play}} Championship]], of which the six editions held between 1952 and 1957 are retroactively regarded as legitimate continuations of the World Snooker Championship. However, due to waning public interest in snooker during the {{nowrap|post-war}} era, that tournament was also discontinued, and the world title was uncontested between 1958 and 1963.<ref name="Everton-1986"/><ref name="Turner-2008"/>

Professional player [[Rex Williams]] was instrumental in reviving the World Snooker Championship on a challenge basis in 1964. [[John Pulman]], winner of the [[1957 World Professional Match-play Championship|1957 World Professional {{nowrap|Match-play}} Championship]], defended the world title across seven [[1964–68 World Snooker Championships|challenge matches between 1964 and 1968]]. The World Snooker Championship reverted to an annual knockout tournament for the [[1969 World Snooker Championship|1969 edition]], which marked the beginning of the championship's "modern era".<ref>{{cite web |last1=Nunns |first1=Hector |last2=Hendon |first2=David |title=Full history |url=https://wpbsa.com/about-us/history/full-history/ |website=[[WPBSA]] |access-date=31 March 2024 |archive-date=31 March 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240331092827/https://wpbsa.com/about-us/history/full-history/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Wright |first=Joe |title=Who has won the most World Snooker Championship titles? === Format ===
The top 16 players in the [[snooker world rankings]], as they stood after the 2026 Tour Championship, were [[Seeding (sports)|seeded]] through to the main stage at the Crucible Theatre.<ref>{{Cite web |date=31 March 2026 |title=Crucible top 16 confirmed |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/31/crucible-top-16-confirmed/ |access-date=17 April 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623080354/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/31/crucible-top-16-confirmed/|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> A further 16 players earned places at the Crucible through a qualifying competition that took place from 6 to 15 April at the [[English Institute of Sport, Sheffield|English Institute of Sport]] in Sheffield, featuring 128 players, including professionals outside the top 16 in the rankings and 16 amateurs selected by the [[World Professional Billiards and Snooker Association]] on the basis of their performances at recognised international events that season. Professional players who did not enter the event were replaced by amateur players from the [[2025 Q School]] Order of Merit.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Huart |first=Matt |date=2026-01-28 |title=WPBSA Qualifying Criteria Announced for 2026 World Snooker Championship |url=https://www.wpbsa.com/wpbsa-qualifying-criteria-announced-for-2026-world-snooker-championship/ |access-date=2026-06-24 |website=WPBSA |language=en-GB}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=25 February 2026 |title=2026 World Championship qualifying tickets on sale now |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/february/25/world-championship-qualifying-tickets-on-sale-now/ |access-date=17 April 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623085854/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/february/25/world-championship-qualifying-tickets-on-sale-now/|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> The qualifiers took place over four rounds, with players ranked 49 to 80 seeded through to the second qualifying round and players ranked 17 to 48 seeded through to the third qualifying round. All qualifying matches were played as the best of 19 {{cuegloss|frames}}, held over two {{cuegloss|sessions}}.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Day |first=Michael |date=14 April 2026 |title=World Snooker Championship Qualifying 2026: Draw, results, format in Crucible race |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/world-championship/world-snooker-championship-qualifying-2026-draw-results-format-in-race-to-the-crucible-6563628 |access-date=15 April 2026 |website=Totally Snookered |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623080408/https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/world-championship/world-snooker-championship-qualifying-2026-draw-results-format-in-race-to-the-crucible-6563628|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

The first-round draw, during which the 16 successful qualifiers were drawn at random against the top 16 seeds, took place on 16 April, broadcast on [[BBC Radio 5 Live]] and the [[BBC Sport]] website.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Emons |first=Michael |date=16 April 2026 |title=O'Sullivan to play China's He in Crucible round one |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cnv8pzvr1jpo |access-date=16 April 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623151534/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cnv8pzvr1jpo|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> First-round matches were played as the best of 19 frames, held over two sessions. Second-round and quarter-final matches were played as the best of 25 frames, held over three sessions. The semi-final matches were played as the best of 33 frames, held over four sessions. The final was the best of 35 frames, also held over four sessions.<ref>{{Cite web |date=16 April 2026 |title=World Snooker Championship 2026: Date, schedule, draw, how to watch, prize money, more |url=https://www.espn.co.uk/snooker/story/_/id/48484615/world-snooker-championship-2026-results-date-schedule-draw-how-watch-format-more-crucible-theatre-sheffield |access-date=16 April 2026 |website=[[ESPN]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623080500/https://www.espn.co.uk/snooker/story/_/id/48484615/world-snooker-championship-2026-results-date-schedule-draw-how-watch-format-more-crucible-theatre-sheffield|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="BBC_schedule">{{Cite web |date=16 April 2026 |title=Results and key moments as Wu wins 2026 World Championship|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cg73glyre3jo |access-date=16 April 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623103714/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cg73glyre3jo|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

Murphy narrowed Wu's lead to 9–7, but Wu made a 91 break in the last frame of the session for a three-frame overnight lead at 10–7.<ref>{{Cite web |date=3 May 2026 |title=Wu leads Murphy overnight |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/may/03/wu-leads-murphy-overnight/ |access-date=3 May 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260526102244/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/may/03/wu-leads-murphy-overnight/|archive-date=26 May 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="BBC_Wu_Murphy_01"/> 

Murphy won the first five frames of the third session, making breaks including 76, 52, 59, 55, and 60 as he moved 12–10 ahead. Wu won the last three frames of the session, making breaks including 64, 60, and 61 as he regained a one-frame lead at 13–12.<ref>{{Cite web |date=4 May 2026 |title=Wu rallies to secure narrow lead |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/may/04/wu-rallies-to-secure-narrow-lead/ |access-date=4 May 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260526102313/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/may/04/wu-rallies-to-secure-narrow-lead/|archive-date=26 May 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="BBC_Wu_Murphy_02">{{Cite web |last=Sutcliffe |first=Steve |date=4 May 2026 |title=Wu beats Murphy in decider to win world title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c202n0gkgvxo |access-date=4 May 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623142840/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c202n0gkgvxo|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> Wu also won the first frame of the final session with an 88 break, moving 14–12 ahead, but Murphy made an 82 break to win frame 27 and then tied the scores at 14–14. Wu won frame 29, but Murphy recovered from 70 points behind in frame 30 to level at 15–15. Wu took the lead again by winning frame 31, but Murphy responded with a century of 131 to tie the scores at 16–16. In frame 33, Murphy missed a red while on a break of 45, and Wu made a 91 break to move one frame from victory at 17–16. Wu had a match-winning opportunity in frame 34 but missed the black off its spot while on a break of 43, and Murphy levelled at 17–17 with a 75 break. It was the fourth time the World Championship final had gone to a deciding frame at the Crucible, following the [[1985 World Snooker Championship final|1985 final]] between [[Dennis Taylor]] and Steve Davis, the [[1994 World Snooker Championship|1994 final]] between Hendry and Jimmy White, and the [[2002 World Snooker Championship|2002 final]] between [[Peter Ebdon]] and Hendry. Wu won the decider with a break of 85 to secure an 18–17 victory. In all, the final featured three centuries and 29 other breaks of 50 or more.<ref name="WST_Wu_Murphy"/><ref name="BBC_Wu_Murphy_02"/>

Wu won his first world title, first Triple Crown title, and second ranking title. He became the second World Champion from Asia and the second from mainland China, following Zhao. At the age of {{age in years and days|2003-10-14|2026-05-04|sep=and}}, he became the second-youngest winner in the tournament's history, after Hendry, who had been {{age in years and days|1969-01-13|1990-04-29|sep=and}} old at the [[1990 World Snooker Championship|1990 edition]]. Wu was the fourth consecutive first-time winner, following Brecel in 2023, Kyren Wilson in 2024, and Zhao in 2025, setting a new record for the longest streak of maiden winners at the tournament.<ref name="WST_Wu_Murphy">{{Cite web |date=4 May 2026 |title=Wu rules the world after classic Crucible final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/may/04/Wu-Rules-The-World-After-Classic-Crucible-Final/ |access-date=4 May 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623102553/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/may/04/Wu-Rules-The-World-After-Classic-Crucible-Final/|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="BBC_Wu_Murphy_02"/> "I am so happy I could play like that today," Wu said after the match. "I played for my family, for myself and for China. My parents are the true champions. Since I made the decision to drop out of school, my dad has been by my side. My mum has also been through so much over the years. They are the source of my strength and I love them so much."<ref name="WST_Wu_Murphy"/> After losing his fourth world final out of five played, Murphy commented: "It was a great match. It had everything. Wu is one of the most talented players I have ever seen. I don't feel I lost the final—Wu won it. I couldn't have tried harder and I am very proud of the way I played." Wu finished the season at a career high of fourth in the [[snooker world rankings]], with Murphy advancing to sixth.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Rankings 2025/2026 |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?season=2025&template=31 |access-date=4 May 2026 |website=snooker.org |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623142717/https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?season=2025&template=31|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> Writing in the ''[[The New York Times|New York Times]]'', Tim Spiers called the championship "one of the best tournaments in recent years," and said "the two semis and the final were all bona fide classics."<ref>{{Cite news |last=Spiers |first=Tim |date=4 May 2026 |title=Wu Yize becomes second-youngest snooker world champion after 10-hour match |url=https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7253756/2026/05/04/wu-yize-world-snooker-championship-winner/ |access-date=5 May 2026 |work=[[The New York Times]] |language=en-US|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260623102547/https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7253756/2026/05/04/wu-yize-world-snooker-championship-winner/|archive-date=23 June 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>
