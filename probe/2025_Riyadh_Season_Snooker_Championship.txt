The '''2025 Riyadh Season Snooker Championship''' was a professional non-ranking [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 19 to 21 November 2025 at the Global Theatre in [[Boulevard City]], [[Riyadh]], Saudi Arabia. Organised by the [[World Snooker Tour]] as part of the [[2025–26 snooker season|2025{{nbnd}}26 snooker season]], the event featured twelve participants, comprising the defending champion, the reigning World Champion, the eight other highest-ranked players in the [[snooker world rankings]] as they stood after the [[2025 Xi'an Grand Prix]], and two local wildcard players.<ref>{{cite web |date=10 October 2025 |title=Race to Riyadh: Murphy and Wilson chasing spots |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/october/10/race-to-riyadh--murphy-and-wilson-chasing-spots/ |access-date=10 October 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref> 

The third edition of the tournament since it was first staged as the [[2024 World Masters of Snooker]], it took place during the [[Riyadh Season]] festival. It was broadcast by [[TNT Sports (United Kingdom)|TNT Sports]] in the UK and Ireland, by [[Eurosport]] in mainland Europe, by local broadcasters in Asia, and by [[WST Play]] in all other territories. The winner received £250,000 from a total prize fund of £785,000. Additionally, the tournament featured a 20-point gold ball that could be {{cuegloss|pot|potted}} only after a player had completed a [[maximum break]] to extend the break to 167. Any player who successfully potted the gold ball would win [[United States dollar|$]]1,000,000.<ref>{{cite web |last=Day |first=Michael |date=5 October 2025 |title=Gold ball snooker event to return with $1 million jackpot prize for 167 super-maximum |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/world-masters-of-snooker/saudi-arabia-gold-ball-snooker-event-to-return-with-1-million-jackpot-prize-for-167-super-maximum-5346534 |access-date=5 October 2025 |website=Totally Snookered |language=en}}</ref>

[[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Luca Brecel]] 5{{nbnd}}1 in the final of the tournament's [[2024 Riyadh Season Snooker Championship|previous edition]], but he lost 1–4 to [[Neil Robertson]] in the semi-finals. The reigning World Champion [[Zhao Xintong]] defeated Robertson 5–2 in the final to win his fourth professional title. The tournament produced 13 [[Century break|century breaks]], of which the highest was a 138 by Zhao in his semi-final match against [[Judd Trump]]. No player compiled a maximum break at the event; the best effort was nine {{Cuegloss|red ball|reds}} and nine {{Cuegloss|black ball|blacks}} by Allen in the semi-finals. As such, no player had the opportunity to pot the gold ball for a 167 break.

==Overview==
The tournament was first staged in March 2024 as the [[2024 World Masters of Snooker]], which was the first professional snooker event ever held in Saudi Arabia.<ref>{{cite web |date=18 January 2024 |title=First invitational event in Saudi announced |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/january/18/alalshikh-announces-agreement-with-world-snooker-to-host-first-ever-invitational-event/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240123134554/https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/january/18/alalshikh-announces-agreement-with-world-snooker-to-host-first-ever-invitational-event/ |archive-date=23 January 2024 |access-date=20 November 2025 |work=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref> The inaugural champion was [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]], who defeated [[Luca Brecel]] 5–2 in the final.<ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2024 |title=Rocket rules in Riyadh |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/march/06/day-three-gallery/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240307053455/https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/march/06/day-three-gallery/ |archive-date=7 March 2024 |access-date=20 November 2025 |work=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref>

The 2025 edition of the event—the third in the tournament's history—took place from 19 to 21 November in the [[Boulevard City]] entertainment district in [[Riyadh]], Saudi Arabia.<ref name="snooker.org_main">{{cite web |title=Riyadh Season Snooker Championship 2025 |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2544 |website=snooker.org |date=26 October 2025 |access-date=31 October 2025}}</ref> [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]] was the defending champion, having defeated Brecel 5{{nbnd}}1 in the final of the tournament's [[2024 Riyadh Season Snooker Championship|previous edition]].<ref>{{cite news |date=21 December 2024 |title=Allen beats O'Sullivan & Brecel to win Riyadh title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cly2knwk963o |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241221083900/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cly2knwk963o |archive-date=21 December 2024 |access-date=21 December 2024 |work=[[BBC Sport]]}}</ref>

All matches were played as the best of seven frames except the final, which was played as the best of nine frames.<ref name="snooker.org_main" /> The matches were played under regular snooker rules, but the event used a gold ball worth 20 points, called the "Riyadh Season ball". The gold ball was placed on the centre of the {{cuegloss|baulk cushion}}, in line with the {{cuegloss|brown ball|brown}}, {{cuegloss|blue ball|blue}}, {{cuegloss|pink ball|pink}}, and {{cuegloss|black ball|black}} balls. It could only be {{cuegloss|pot|potted}} after a player had completed a [[maximum break]] of 147, to extend the break to 167. {{cuegloss|foul|Fouling}} the gold ball awarded 4 {{cuegloss|penalty points}} to the opponent. The gold ball stayed on the table as long as it was possible for either player to complete a maximum break. When a maximum was no longer possible, the gold ball was removed from the table until the next frame.<ref>{{cite web |title=Saudi Arabia to host new snooker event with added 20-point golden ball |url=https://www.skysports.com/more-sports/snooker/news/12243/13051424/saudi-arabia-to-host-new-snooker-event-with-added-20-point-golden-ball |work=[[Sky Sports]] |date=19 January 2024 |access-date=27 January 2024 |archive-date=22 January 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240122073129/https://www.skysports.com/more-sports/snooker/news/12243/13051424/saudi-arabia-to-host-new-snooker-event-with-added-20-point-golden-ball |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Saudi Arabia set to welcome first pro snooker event with ground breaking 167 prize |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/february/29/Saudi-Arabia-Set-To-Welcome-First-Pro-Snooker-Event/ |work=[[World Snooker Tour]] |date=29 February 2024 |access-date=2 March 2024 |archive-date=2 March 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240302213946/https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/february/29/Saudi-Arabia-Set-To-Welcome-First-Pro-Snooker-Event/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

===Participants===
The event featured twelve players, comprising the defending champion [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]] ([[Seed (sports)|seeded]] first), the [[2025 World Snooker Championship|reigning World Champion]] [[Zhao Xintong]] (seeded second), the eight other highest-ranked players in the [[snooker world rankings]] as they stood after the [[2025 Xi'an Grand Prix]], and two local [[Wild card (sports)|wildcard players]], 15-year-old Ziyad Alqabbani and 22-year-old Ayman Alamri, both from Saudi Arabia.<ref name="wst_draw">{{cite web |title=All-time greats set for Riyadh Season Snooker Championship |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/october/23/all-time-greats-set-for-riyadh-season-snooker-championship/ |date=23 October 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=13 November 2025 |title=Riyadh Season Snooker Championship: Meet the wild cards |url=https://www.wst.tv//news/2025/november/13/riyadh-season-snooker-championship--meet-the-wild-cards/ |access-date=20 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref>

The tournament was broadcast by [[TNT Sports]] and [[Discovery+]] in the United Kingdom and Ireland; by [[Eurosport]] and locally relevant streaming platforms in mainland Europe (Discovery+ in Germany, Italy and Austria and [[HBO Max]] in all other markets); by the CBSA{{nbh}}WPBSA Academy [[WeChat]] Channel, the CBSA{{nbh}}WPBSA Academy [[Douyin]] and [[Huya Live]] in China; by [[Now TV (Hong Kong)|Now TV]] in Hong Kong; by [[Astro SuperSport]] in Malaysia; by [[True Sports]] in Thailand; by [[TAP Digital Media Ventures Corporation|TAP]] in the Philippines; and by Sportcast in Taiwan. In territories with no other broadcaster, the tournament was streamed via [[WST Play]].<ref>{{cite web |title=How to watch the Riyadh Season Snooker Championship |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/13/how-to-watch-the-riyadh-season-snooker-championship/ |date=13 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref>

===Prize money===
The event featured a total prize pool of [[Pound sterling|£]]785,000. An additional prize of [[United States dollar|$]]1,000,000 was available to any player who compiled a 167 break. In previous editions of the event, this prize was available only to the first player achieving the break.<ref name="wst_main">{{cite web |title=Riyadh Season Snooker Championship |url=https://www.wst.tv/riyadh-season-snooker-championship/ |access-date=31 October 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251031133500/https://www.wst.tv/riyadh-season-snooker-championship/ |archive-date=31 October 2025 |url-status=live}}</ref>

== Summary ==

=== Round one ===
The first round featured the ninth and tenth seeds against the two wildcard players. [[Shaun Murphy]] made {{Cuegloss|break|breaks}} including 128 and 109 as he [[Whitewash (sport)|whitewashed]] Ziyad Alqabbani. [[Ding Junhui]] made a highest break of 84 as he whitewashed Ayman Alamri.<ref name="WST_day_1">{{Cite web |date=19 November 2025 |title=Riyadh Season Snooker Championship day one |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/19/riyadh-season-snooker-championship-day-one/ |access-date=20 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref>

=== Round two ===
[[File:Shaun Murphy PHC 2015-3.jpg|thumb|[[Shaun Murphy]] ''(pictured in 2015)'' whitewashed [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]]. It was Murphy's first victory over O'Sullivan since 2017.]]
Round two featured the winners from round one against the seventh and eighth seeds. Facing [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]], Murphy won the opening frame, helped by a break of 53. He won the second frame after a {{Cuegloss|safety}} battle on the last {{Cuegloss|red ball|red}}, and extended his lead to 3–0 with a 93 break in frame three. Murphy completed a whitewash after O'Sullivan missed a {{Cuegloss|yellow ball|yellow}} in frame four. It was Murphy's first victory over O'Sullivan since 2017, following six consecutive defeats, and the first time since 2023 that O'Sullivan had failed to win a frame in a match of seven frames or more. "I'm delighted," Murphy said afterwards. "Any win over [O'Sullivan] has to be celebrated. I have been playing well all season and it has been fantastic to carry my form through." [[John Higgins]] also whitewashed Ding, making two consecutive [[Century break|century breaks]] of 106 in frames three and four. Afterwards, Higgins criticised the playing conditions, noting that [[Drink coaster|beermats]] had been placed beneath the table to raise it up. "Somebody really needs to get told," said Higgins afterwards. "I don't know who has passed that but it's pathetic for a million-pound tournament. It really is bad." [[Stephen Hendry]], commenting for [[TNT Sports (United Kingdom)|TNT Sports]], said: "I can only assume the floor is so uneven they have had to build the table up, but as a player you can really feel the difference."<ref name="WST_day_1" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Sutcliffe |first=Steve |date=19 November 2025 |title=Riyadh Season Snooker Championship: Shaun Murphy whitewashes Ronnie O'Sullivan at 'golden ball' event |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c4g3geky9p5o |access-date=20 November 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

=== Quarter-finals ===
[[File:Judd Trump at Snooker German Masters (Martin Rulsch) 2014-02-01 18 (cropped).jpg|thumb|[[Judd Trump]] ''(pictured in 2014)'' ended his "experiment" with a titanium-{{Cuegloss|ferrule}} cue. Playing with his old brass-ferrule cue, he whitewashed [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] in 48 minutes.]]
The quarter-finals featured the top six seeds, who entered the competition at that point, together with the winners from round two. [[Neil Robertson]], competing in the tournament for the first time, faced [[Kyren Wilson]]. Robertson made back-to-back centuries of 100 and 133 to win the first two frames. In frame three, he attempted a [[maximum break]], potting five {{Cuegloss|red ball|reds}} and five {{Cuegloss|black ball|blacks}} before running out of position after the sixth red. He potted the {{Cuegloss|blue ball|blue}} instead of the black and went on to make a frame-winning break of 88. In frame four, Robertson produced a 76 break to complete a whitewash victory. "It was pretty much perfect, every time I got a chance I won the frame," said Robertson afterwards. Commenting on frame three, he said: "I would have loved to see how far I could have got with the 167—if the {{Cuegloss|cue ball}} had run on another couple of inches it would have been perfect." Higgins and the defending champion [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]] shared the first two frames of their quarter-final. Allen attempted a maximum break in the third frame, but his effort ended on 48, and Higgins won the frame with an 82 {{Cuegloss|clearance}}. Allen won frame four after a safety battle on the last red to tie the scores at 2–2. He also took frame five with a 49 break, his highest of the match, and then won frame six to complete a 4–2 victory.<ref>{{Cite web |date=20 November 2025 |title=Robertson and Allen into semi-finals in Riyadh |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/20/Robertson-And-Allen-Into-Semi-Finals-In-Riyadh/ |access-date=20 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref>

[[Judd Trump]], who had switched to a cue with a titanium {{Cuegloss|ferrule}} at the beginning of the season, reverted to his old brass-ferrule cue for his match against [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]]. After taking a 2–0 lead, Trump made back-to-back centuries of 100 and 102 as he completed a whitewash victory, the match having lasted just 48 minutes. "The experiment with that cue did not succeed," Trump said afterwards. "It is hard to play your best when you are trying to find the right cue, but I'm out there battling. There is still going to be a bit of changing until I find the right cue, it is not perfect at the moment." Facing the reigning World Champion [[Zhao Xintong]], Murphy trailed by 49 points in the opening frame but won it with a clearance of 79. Zhao tied the scores at 1–1 with a 62 break. Murphy led by 58 points in frame three, but he missed a red, and Zhao took the frame with a 69 break. A century of 101 gave Murphy frame four, tying the scores at 2–2, but Zhao took frame five with a 73 break. In frame six, Murphy was on a break of 40 when he missed a {{Cuegloss|plant}}, and Zhao secured a 4–2 victory with a break of 88.<ref>{{Cite web |date=20 November 2025 |title=Cue swap pays off for Trump |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/20/Cue-Swap-Pays-Off-For-Trump/ |access-date=20 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Sutcliffe |first=Steve |date=20 November 2025 |title=Riyadh Season Snooker: Judd Trump beats Mark Williams after ending cue 'experiment' |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c20e0lrdwgwo |access-date=21 November 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

=== Semi-finals ===
[[File:Mark Allen PHC 2016-2.jpg|thumb|The defending champion [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]] ''(pictured in 2016)'' lost in the semi-finals but came closest to a [[maximum break]] at the event, potting nine reds and nine blacks.]]
In the first semi-final, the defending champion Allen faced Robertson, who made breaks including 73 and 67 as he won the first three frames. Allen came from 32 points behind to take frame four with a 91 clearance. In frame five, Allen attempted a maximum break, potting nine reds and nine blacks before he missed a red with the {{Cuegloss|rest}}, ending the break on 72. Robertson then produced a clearance of 73 to win the frame by one point and secure a 4–1 victory.<ref name="WST_Zhao_Trump">{{Cite web |date=21 November 2025 |title=Zhao to meet Robertson in Riyadh final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/21/Zhao-To-Meet-Robertson-In-Riyadh-Final/ |access-date=21 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref>

In the other semi-final, Trump made breaks of 80 and 56 as he won the first two frames against Zhao. In frame three, Trump led by 34 points when he missed a {{Cuegloss|long pot}} on a red. Zhao made a 66 break to win the frame, and then produced a century of 138 in frame four, the highest break of the tournament, to tie the scores at 2–2. Trump countered with a century of 103 to lead 3–2, but he missed opportunities to clinch the match in frame six, which Zhao eventually won on the {{Cuegloss|colours}}, forcing the only {{Cuegloss|deciding frame}} of the tournament. With two reds remaining in the decider, Zhao {{Cuegloss|snooker|snookered}} Trump behind the yellow; from the opportunity that followed, Zhao potted a red into a {{Cuegloss|middle pocket}} and went on to win the frame and match.<ref name="WST_Zhao_Trump" />

=== Final ===
[[File:Zhao Xintong PHC 2016-2.jpg|thumb|[[Zhao Xintong]] ''(pictured in 2016)'' defeated [[Neil Robertson]] in the final to win his fourth professional title.]]
The final was played as the best of nine frames between the world number three Robertson and the world number ten Zhao. The opening frame went to Zhao, who also had an opportunity to win the second from 46 points behind; however, he missed a pot on the black, and Robertson tied the scores at 1–1. In the third frame, Robertson attempted a maximum break, potting six reds and six blacks before losing position; Zhao recovered to win the frame on the colours and then made back-to-back centuries of 131 and 134 to move 4–1 ahead. Robertson won frame six with a 91 break and took a 20-point lead in frame seven before he missed a red to a middle pocket, allowing Zhao to secure a 5–2 victory with a 57 break. It was Zhao's first professional title since winning the 2025 World Championship and his fourth victory in his four professional finals. "I feel so happy, it's amazing to win this title," said Zhao afterwards. "I was nervous but I just tried to concentrate on the table. It's my first time in Riyadh so I will have a lot of good memories here and I look forward to coming back. Lots of people from China were here, they gave me huge support and that was so important to me."<ref>{{Cite web |date=21 November 2025 |title=Zhao rules in Riyadh |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/november/21/Zhao-Rules-In-Riyadh/ |access-date=21 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Sutcliffe |first=Steve |date=21 November 2025 |title=Zhao Xintong beats Neil Robertson to triumph in Riyadh |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cm2vnkzl355o |access-date=21 November 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>
