The '''2025 Championship League''' (officially the '''2025 BetVictor Championship League Snooker''') was a professional ranking [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 30 June to 23 July 2025 at the [[Leicester Arena]] in [[Leicester]], England.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Calendar 2025/2026 - snooker.org |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?template=2&season=2025 |access-date=19 May 2025 |website=www.snooker.org |language=en}}</ref> It was the sixth staging of the ranking edition of the tournament, which was first held in [[2020 Championship League (ranking)|2020]]. Organised by [[Matchroom Sport]], it was the first [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season]], preceding the [[2025 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters]]. The winner received £33,000 from a total prize fund of £328,000.

[[Ali Carter]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Jackson Page]] 3{{nbnd}}1 in the final of the [[2024 Championship League (ranking)|previous ranking edition]],<ref>{{Cite web |date=3 July 2024 |title=Carter in a league of his own |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/july/03/in-a-league-of-his-own/ |access-date=19 May 2025 |website=World Snooker Tour |language=en}}</ref> but he was eliminated in stage one.<ref name="WST_g6_g7" /> By topping her group in stage one, [[Ng On-yee]] became the first woman to reach the last 32 of a professional ranking event.<ref name="WST_g2_g32" /> [[Stephen Maguire]] won the tournament, beating [[Joe O'Connor (snooker player)|Joe O'Connor]] 3{{nbnd}}1 in the final to claim the seventh ranking title of his career and his first since winning the [[2020 Tour Championship]] over five years earlier. Maguire's win secured him a place in the [[2025 Champion of Champions]] tournament, while O'Connor's defeat meant that he had lost all three of the ranking finals he had played.<ref name="WST_gw1_gw2_final" /><ref>{{Cite web |date=23 July 2025 |title=Snooker: Stephen Maguire wins 2025 Championship League |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c5yl13pz0xgo |access-date=24 July 2025 |website=BBC Sport |language=en-GB}}</ref>

The event produced 109 [[century break]]s (86 in stage one, 19 in stage two, and 4 in stage three),<ref name="SI_cent" /> of which the highest was a [[maximum break]] by [[Fan Zhengyi]] in his stage one match against [[Xu Si]]. The second maximum of Fan's career, it was the first maximum of the season and the 218th official maximum in professional competition.<ref name="Max 218" />

==Format==
The competition began with 32 rounds of group matches with each group consisting of four players. Two groups were played to a finish every day using a two{{nbh}}[[Billiard table|table]] setup in the arena. The groups were contested using a [[Round-robin tournament|round{{nbh}}robin]] format, with six matches played in each group. All matches in group play were played as best{{nbh}}of{{nbh}}four {{cuegloss|frame|frames}}, with three points awarded for a win and one point for a draw. Group positions were determined by points scored, frame difference and then head{{nbh}}to{{nbh}}head results between players who were tied. Places that were still tied were then determined by the highest {{cuegloss|break}} made in the group. If the highest break was also tied, the next highest break made by the players was used.<ref name="CLS_ranking2">{{cite web |title=Championship League Snooker |url=https://championshipleaguesnooker.co.uk/ranking/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240704083417/https://championshipleaguesnooker.co.uk/ranking/ |archive-date=4 July 2024 |access-date=4 July 2024 |work=championshipleaguesnooker.co.uk}}</ref><ref name="WST_explainer">{{cite web |date=11 June 2024 |title=BetVictor Championship League explainer |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/june/11/betvictor-championship-league-explainer/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240611204205/https://www.wst.tv/news/2024/june/11/betvictor-championship-league-explainer/ |archive-date=11 June 2024 |access-date=11 June 2024 |work=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref>

The 32 players that topped the group tables qualified for the group winners' stage, consisting of eight groups of four players. The eight winners from the group winners' stage qualified for the two final groups, with the final taking place later on the same day.<ref name="CLS_ranking2" /> The winner received the Championship League title and a place in the [[2025 Champion of Champions]].

===Participants===
No player in the top eight of the [[Snooker world rankings|world rankings]] entered the event. Only five top-16 players featured—[[Barry Hawkins]], [[Zhang Anda]], [[Shaun Murphy]], [[Si Jiahui]], and [[Chris Wakelin]]. Outside the top 16, [[Hossein Vafaei]], [[Anthony McGill]], [[Ishpreet Singh Chadha]], [[Sunny Akani]], and [[Ken Doherty]] also did not enter. [[Luca Brecel]] and [[Jimmy White]] entered but subsequently withdrew. The tournament featured 24 amateur players, not currently on the [[World Snooker Tour]], indicated by (a) in the tables below.<ref name="s.o_main" />

==Stage one==
Stage one consisted of 32 groups, each having four players. Numbers in parentheses after the players' names denote the players' [[seed (sports)|seeding]], and (a) indicates amateur players not on the main [[World Snooker Tour]]. Unlike other tournaments, where the defending champion is usually the top seed, the players were seeded according to their ranking, and the top 32 seeds were allocated to the 32 groups. One exception to this was Group 27, because [[Luca Brecel]], seeded 27, withdrew.<ref name="s.o_main">{{cite web |title=2025 Championship League (Ranking) |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2337 |access-date=29 June 2025 |website=snooker.org}}</ref>
