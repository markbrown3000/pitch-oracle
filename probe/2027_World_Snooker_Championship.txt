The '''2027 World Snooker Championship''' is an upcoming professional [[snooker]] tournament that will take place from 17 April to 3 May 2027 at the [[Crucible Theatre]] in [[Sheffield]], England, the 51st consecutive year that the [[World Snooker Championship]] is staged at the venue. The tournament will be the centenary edition of the World Snooker Championship, which was first held in [[1927 World Snooker Championship|1927]]. Organised by the [[World Snooker Tour]], the tournament will be the 18th and final [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2026–27 snooker season|2026–‍27 snooker season]].

[[Wu Yize]] will be the defending champion, having defeated [[Shaun Murphy]] 18–17 in the [[2026 World Snooker Championship|2026 final]].

== Background ==
[[File:The Crucible Theatre during the 2026 World Snooker Championship.jpg|alt=The Crucible Theatre from outside|thumb|For the 51st consecutive year, the main stage of the tournament will be held at the [[Crucible Theatre]] (''pictured in 2026'') in [[Sheffield]], England.]]

The inaugural [[1927 World Snooker Championship]], then known as the Professional Championship of Snooker, took place at various venues in England between November 1926 and May 1927. [[Joe Davis]] won the final, held at [[Camkin's Hall]] in [[Birmingham]] from 9 to 12&nbsp;May 1927, and went on to win all of the first 15 stagings of the tournament before retiring undefeated after the [[1946 World Snooker Championship|1946 edition]] (no tournaments were held from 1941 to 1945 because of [[World War II]]).<ref name="Everton-1986">{{cite book |last=Everton |first=Clive |title=The History of Snooker and Billiards |publisher=[[Transworld (publisher)|Partridge Press]] |location=[[Haywards Heath]] |date=1986 |isbn=978-1-85225-013-3 |page=50}}</ref><ref name="Turner-2008">{{cite web |last=Turner |first=Chris |title=World Professional Championship |url=http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/world.html |website=Chris Turner's Snooker Archive |year=2008 |access-date=31 March 2024 |archive-date=6 June 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160606102115/http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/world.html |url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=11 July 1978 |title=Joe Davis, a snooker champion who retired unbeaten, Was 77 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1978/07/11/archives/joe-davis-a-snooker-champion-who-retired-unbeaten-was-77.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231022194142/https://www.nytimes.com/1978/07/11/archives/joe-davis-a-snooker-champion-who-retired-unbeaten-was-77.html |archive-date=22 October 2023 |access-date=31 March 2024 |work=[[The New York Times]] |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> The tournament went into abeyance after only two players contested the [[1952 World Snooker Championship|1952 edition]], due to a dispute between the Professional Billiards Players' Association (PBPA) and the [[Billiards Association and Control Council]] (BACC). The PBPA established an alternative tournament, the [[World Professional Match-play Championship|World Professional {{nowrap|Match-play}} Championship]], of which the six editions held between 1952 and 1957 are retroactively regarded as legitimate continuations of the World Snooker Championship. However, due to waning public interest in snooker during the {{nowrap|post-war}} era, that tournament was also discontinued, and the world title was uncontested between 1958 and 1963.<ref name="Everton-1986" /><ref name="Turner-2008" />

Professional player [[Rex Williams]] was instrumental in reviving the World Snooker Championship on a challenge basis in 1964. [[John Pulman]], winner of the [[1957 World Professional Match-play Championship|1957 World Professional {{nowrap|Match-play}} Championship]], defended the world title across seven [[1964–68 World Snooker Championships|challenge matches between 1964 and 1968]]. The World Snooker Championship reverted to an annual knockout tournament for the [[1969 World Snooker Championship|1969 edition]], which marked the beginning of the championship's "modern era".<ref>{{cite web |last1=Nunns |first1=Hector |last2=Hendon |first2=David |title=Full history |url=https://wpbsa.com/about-us/history/full-history/ |website=[[WPBSA]] |access-date=31 March 2024 |archive-date=31 March 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240331092827/https://wpbsa.com/about-us/history/full-history/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Wright |first=Joe |title=Who has won the most World Snooker Championship titles? === Format ===
The top 16 players from the [[snooker world rankings]], as they stand after the [[2027 Tour Championship]], will be seeded through to the main stage at the Crucible. The qualifying rounds will take place from 5 to 14 April at the [[English Institute of Sport, Sheffield|English Institute of Sport]] in Sheffield, featuring 128 professional and invited amateur competitors, 16 of whom will join the seeds at the Crucible.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2653|title=World Championship Qualifiers 2027|website=snooker.org|access-date=30 April 2026|language=en}}</ref>

=== Prize fund ===
The winner of the event will receive £625,000 from a total prize fund of £3,000,000.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/april/20/world-championship-and-uk-championship-prize-money-increased-for-2026-27/|title=World Championship and UK Championship Prize Money Increased For 2026/27|work=[[World Snooker Tour]]|date=20 April 2026|access-date=29 April 2026|language=en}}</ref> The winner's prize will increase from £500,000 to £625,000, the first change since [[2019 World Snooker Championship|2019]]. The total prize fund will increase from £2,395,000 to £3,000,000, the first change since [[2020 World Snooker Championship|2020]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Colman |first=Jonty |date=30 April 2026 |title=World Snooker Championship 2026: What is the prize money? |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cy71ln1dpn5o |access-date=1 May 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref><!--
