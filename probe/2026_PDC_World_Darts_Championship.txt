The '''2026 PDC World Darts Championship''' (known for sponsorship reasons as the '''2025/26 Paddy Power World Darts Championship''')<ref name="Ticket">{{cite web |last=Allen |first=Dave |date=17 July 2025 |title=2025/26 Paddy Power World Darts Championship ticket sale details confirmed |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/july/17/202526-paddy-power-world-darts-championship-ticket-sale-details-confirmed |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=17 July 2025}}</ref> was a professional [[darts]] tournament that took place from 11 December 2025 to 3 January 2026 at [[Alexandra Palace]] in London, England. The 33rd [[PDC World Darts Championship|World Darts Championship]] organised by the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC), it was the 19th to be staged at Alexandra Palace, where it was held in the venue's West Hall for the final time; subsequent editions will take place in the Great Hall. The tournament's £5,000,000 prize fund was double that of the previous year, as were the prizes for the winner, runner-up, semi-finalists, and quarter-finalists. The winner received £1,000,000, the largest prize ever awarded in the history of professional darts.

The tournament featured a 128-player field, expanded from 96. The top 32 players on the [[PDC Order of Merit]] were [[Seeding (sports)|seeded]] for the first round. A total of 28 players made their debut at the tournament, the most successful of whom was [[Justin Hood (darts player)|Justin Hood]], who reached the quarter-finals. At age 71, [[Paul Lim]] became the oldest player to win a match at the tournament when he defeated [[Jeffrey de Graaf]] in the first round. [[Nitin Kumar]], [[Stefan Bellmont]], and [[David Munyua]] became the first Indian, Swiss, and Kenyan players respectively to win matches at a PDC World Championship.

[[Luke Littler]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Michael van Gerwen]] 7–3 in the [[2025 PDC World Darts Championship|2025 final]]. He reached his third consecutive final, where he faced [[Gian van Veen]], who became the third Dutch player to reach a PDC World Championship final, following [[Raymond van Barneveld]] and Van Gerwen. Littler beat Van Veen 7–1 to win his second world title. He was the fourth player to retain the PDC world title, following [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]], [[Adrian Lewis]], and [[Gary Anderson (darts player)|Gary Anderson]], and the first to do so since Anderson in the [[2016 PDC World Darts Championship|2016]] championship. Littler lost only four [[Set (darts)|sets]] across the seven matches he played in the tournament. He also won the "Ballon d'Art" award for the most 180s at the tournament, with 73.

===Background===
[[File:APalace 1.jpg|thumb|The tournament was held at [[Alexandra Palace]] in London, England.]]
The [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC) was established under the World Darts Council name by the managers John Markovic, Tommy Cox and Dick Allix, along with the [[Darts world rankings|world's top 16 players]] in January 1992 as a separate body that [[Split in darts|broke away]] from the [[British Darts Organisation]] (BDO).<ref name=PDCHistory>{{cite web|title=PDC History|url=https://www.dartsworld.com/pdc-history/|work=Darts World|access-date=17 July 2025|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191108184503/https://www.dartsworld.com/pdc-history/|archive-date=8 November 2019}}</ref> The [[1994 WDC World Darts Championship|inaugural edition]] of the [[PDC World Darts Championship]] was held from December 1993 to January 1994 at the [[Circus Tavern]] in [[Purfleet]], Essex, England. It was won by [[Dennis Priestley]], who defeated [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]] in the final on 2 January 1994.<ref>{{cite web |date=3 January 1994 |title=Darts: Priestley is supreme as No 1 rebel |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/sport/darts-priestley-is-supreme-as-no-1-rebel-1397713.html |website=The Independent |access-date=25 August 2025}}</ref><ref name="Ball"/> Taylor would go on to win the tournament 14 times, adding to his [[BDO World Darts Championship]] wins in [[1990 BDO World Darts Championship|1990]] and [[1992 BDO World Darts Championship|1992]] for a record total of 16 world titles, including eight in a row from 1995 to 2002.<ref>{{cite web |last=Mirza |first=Raz |date=23 December 2017 |title=Phil Taylor: The greatest darts player to have ever graced the oche |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/11167035/phil-taylor-the-greatest-darts-player-to-have-ever-graced-the-oche |website=Sky Sports |access-date=25 August 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=20 November 2023 |title=Phil Taylor: 16-time world champion to retire at the end of 2024 |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/67480588 |website=BBC Sport |access-date=25 August 2025}}</ref> The PDC World Championship is one of two [[world championship]]s in the game of [[darts]]; the other being the [[WDF World Darts Championship]], which was first held in 2022 as the successor to the BDO event.<ref name="Ball">{{Cite web|url=https://www.balls.ie/other-sports/why-is-there-two-darts-world-championships-354404-354404|title="A Party Where All The Beautiful People Had Left"– The Story of How There Came To Be Two World Darts Championships|last=Neville|first=Connor|date=13 December 2016|publisher=Balls.ie|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171123041834/https://www.balls.ie/other-sports/why-is-there-two-darts-world-championships-354404-354404|archive-date=23 November 2017|url-status=live|access-date=17 July 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=10 April 2022 |title=Neil Duff: Northern Ireland man wins WDF World Championship title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/61062470 |website=BBC Sport |access-date=17 July 2025}}</ref>

===Format===
Under the new format, all players, including the 32 [[Seeding (sport)|seeds]], entered the tournament in the first round, a change from previous years where seeds entered in the second round.<ref name="PDCQ">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=17 June 2025 |title=Qualifying structure confirmed for Paddy Power World Darts Championship |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/06/17/qualifying-structure-confirmed-paddy-power-world-darts-championship |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250617155044/https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/06/17/qualifying-structure-confirmed-paddy-power-world-darts-championship |url-status=dead |archive-date=17 June 2025 |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=17 July 2025}}</ref> The matches were played in [[Set (darts)|set]] format, with the amount of sets required to win a match increasing as the tournament progressed.<ref>{{cite web |last=Chambers |first=Tom |date=26 November 2025 |title=World Darts Championship 2025-26: Schedule, how to watch, prize money, dates, more |url=https://www.espn.com/darts/story/_/id/46932213/pdc-world-darts-championship-2025-2026-schedule-results-how-watch-prize-money-dates-more |website=ESPN |access-date=29 November 2025}}</ref> All non-deciding sets were played to the best of five [[Leg (darts)|legs]]. In the deciding set, a [[tie-break]] rule was applied if the set score was 2–2, where the first player to lead by two legs won the set and the match; if the deciding set reached 5–5, the winner was decided by a [[Sudden death (sport)|sudden-death]] leg.<ref>{{cite web |title=World Darts Championship 2026 dates, format, schedule, how to watch and favourites - who can challenge Luke Littler? |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12040/13474781/world-darts-championship-2026-dates-format-how-to-watch-and-favourites-who-can-challenge-luke-littler |website=Sky Sports |access-date=14 December 2025}}</ref>

===Ranking===
The PDC's main world ranking system, the PDC Order of Merit (known for sponsorship reasons as the [[Werner Co.|Werner]] Rankings Ladder),<ref>{{cite web |date=3 July 2025 |title=PDC Join Forces with Werner and Launch their Rankings Ladder |url=https://dartsworld.com/2025/07/pdc-join-forces-with-werner-and-launch-their-rankings-ladder/ |website=Darts World |access-date=17 July 2025}}</ref> is calculated on a two-year cycle. Prize money won by players in ranking tournaments are removed from their ranking after 104 weeks, meaning players who participated in the 2024 World Championship, who did not lose their [[PDC Tour Card]] during the two-year period, 'defended' their prize money from that event.<ref>{{cite web |author=Paul Nicholson |author-link=Paul Nicholson (darts player) |date=11 April 2021 |title=Is the PDC Order of Merit a 'fair' world ranking system for darts or is it time for change? |url=https://www.sportinglife.com/darts/news/time-for-ranking-system-to-change/190881 |website=Sporting Life |access-date=17 July 2025}}</ref> At the end of the tournament, the prize money won at the 2026 tournament was added and the prize money won at the 2024 tournament was removed.<ref name="PDCOOM">{{cite web |title=PDC Rankings Rules |url=https://www.pdc.tv/pdc-order-merit-rules-1 |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=17 July 2025}}</ref><ref name="TSNOOM">{{cite web |last=O'Hara |first=Michael |date=4 February 2025 |title=2025 PDC darts rankings, explained: Order of Merit, prize money and tournaments included |url=https://www.sportingnews.com/ca/darts/news/2025-pdc-darts-world-rankings-order-merit-prize-money/1720b916991c196880a86434 |website=The Sporting News |access-date=17 July 2025}}</ref> After the tournament, the top 64 players in the PDC Order of Merit received a one-year extension on their Tour Card, joined by the [[List of players with a 2025 PDC Tour Card|players who earned two-year Tour Cards in 2025]] who entered their second year in 2026.<ref>{{cite web |title=PDC Tour Card Holders |url=https://www.pdc.tv/TourCardHolders |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=17 July 2025}}</ref> Players with two or more years on their Tour Card, who finished outside of the top 64, lost their Tour Card and saw their ranking reset to £0, along with all other players who earned prize money in ranking tournaments without holding a Tour Card.<ref name="PDCOOM"/><ref name="TSNOOM"/>

===Prize money===
On 31 March 2025, the PDC announced the biggest prize money increase in the organisation's history, starting from the 2026 season onward. Coinciding with the expansion of the field to 128 players, the World Championship's total prize money increased to £5,000,000, with the winner's share doubling from £500,000 to £1,000,000; this was the first time the tournament's prize money increased since the [[2019 PDC World Darts Championship|2019 edition]].<ref name="Reu"/><ref>{{cite web |date=31 March 2025 |title=The million-pound dart: PDC doubles prize money for world championship |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2025/mar/31/world-darts-championship-pdc-doubles-prize-money-million-pounds |website=The Guardian |access-date=17 July 2025 |agency=PA Media}}</ref> The winner also received the [[Sid Waddell]] Trophy, named in honour of the darts commentator who died in 2012.<ref>{{cite web |last=Ronay |first=Barney |date=2 January 2013 |title=Phil Taylor beats Michael van Gerwen to seal 16th world title |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2013/jan/02/phil-taylor-darts-world-championship |website=The Guardian |access-date=18 July 2025}}</ref> The prize money for the runner-up also doubled from £200,000 to £400,000; the prize money for semi-finalists doubled from £100,000 to £200,000; and prize money for the quarter-finalists doubled from £50,000 to £100,000.<ref name="PDCFund" /><ref>{{Cite web |title=World Darts Championship 2024/25 prize money: How much winner will earn, total prize fund for PDC event {{!}} Sporting News Australia |url=https://www.sportingnews.com/au/darts/news/world-darts-championship-prize-money-how-much-winner-earn-total/826597a194e5bf0a53f858a6 |access-date=2026-01-07 |website=www.sportingnews.com |date=30 December 2024 |language=en-au}}</ref> Like the previous year, tournament sponsor Paddy Power pledged that for every [[nine-dart finish]] hit during the tournament, they would award £60,000 to the player, a random fan, and [[Prostate Cancer UK]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Chambers |first=Tom |date=23 November 2025 |title=World Darts Championship: Fan to throw nine darts for £180,000 prize |url=https://www.espn.co.uk/darts/story/_/id/47048912/world-darts-championship-person-throw-nine-darts-180000-prize-final-explained |website=ESPN |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref>

He won two major titles, the [[2025 PDC World Masters|World Masters]] and the [[2025 Premier League Darts|Premier League]], during the season.<ref name="LHTitles">{{cite web |last=Chambers |first=Tom |date=22 November 2025 |title=Luke Humphries darts titles, prize money, career history, more |url=https://www.espn.co.uk/darts/story/_/id/46943269/luke-humphries-darts-titles-prize-money-career-history-pdc-world-darts-championship |work=ESPN |access-date=23 November 2025}}</ref> The third seed was three-time world champion [[Michael van Gerwen]], who ended a two-year major title drought by winning the [[2025 World Series of Darts Finals|World Series Finals]] in September.<ref>{{cite web |last=Mallows |first=Tom |date=14 September 2025 |title=Van Gerwen beats Littler to win World Series title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cy50r2gex46o |website=BBC Sport |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref> Four-time World Championship semi-finalist [[James Wade]] was seeded seventh in his 22nd appearance at the tournament. Tenth seed [[Gian van Veen]] achieved his first major title at the [[2025 European Championship (darts)|European Championship]] and retained his [[2025 PDC World Youth Championship|World Youth Championship]] during the year.<ref>{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=23 November 2025 |title=Van Veen defeats Greaves to retain Winmau World Youth Championship title |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/november/23/van-veen-defeats-greaves-retain-winmau-world-youth-championship-title |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref><ref name="Field" />

Former PDC world champions [[Gerwyn Price]], [[Gary Anderson (darts player)|Gary Anderson]], [[Rob Cross]], [[Michael Smith (darts player)|Michael Smith]] and [[Peter Wright (darts player)|Peter Wright]] were all amongst the seeded players. [[Josh Rock]] and [[Daryl Gurney]], who won the [[2025 PDC World Cup of Darts|World Cup]] for [[Northern Ireland at the PDC World Cup|Northern Ireland]], were the 11th and 22nd seeds.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Keogh |first1=Francis |last2=Cartwright |first2=Phil |date=15 June 2025 |title=Northern Ireland lift World Cup with epic last-leg win over Wales |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/c79e9qe8j8lo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=4 December 2025}}</ref> [[Nathan Aspinall]], the most prolific player on the [[2025 PDC Pro Tour#European Tour|2025 European Tour]] with three titles,<ref>{{cite web |last=Keogh |first=Frank |date=19 October 2025 |title=Aspinall wins third European event of season |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/ckgky1zx8dko |website=BBC Sport |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref> was the 15th seed after finishing as runner-up to Littler at the Players Championship Finals. [[Jermaine Wattimena]], the 19th seed, claimed his first two PDC ranking titles in [[2025 PDC Players Championship series|2025 Players Championship]] events.<ref>{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=14 October 2025 |title=Wattimena dispatches nine-dart king Aspinall to win PC31 in Wigan |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/october/14/wattimena-dispatches-nine-dart-king-aspinall-win-pc31-wigan |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref> The [[2022 Masters (darts)|2022 Masters]] champion [[Joe Cullen (darts player)|Joe Cullen]] secured the 32nd and final seeded position. Outside of the top 32 seeds, eight more players qualified through the PDC Order of Merit and were unseeded in the tournament draw, including five-time world champion [[Raymond van Barneveld]], who made his 33rd World Championship appearance across both the PDC and BDO tournaments.<ref name="Field" />

====International qualifiers====

There were 48 players who qualified for the tournament through international qualifiers.<ref name="Field" /> Three-time women's world champion [[Beau Greaves]] made her second appearance at the PDC World Championship after her debut at the [[2023 PDC World Darts Championship|2023 event]], having declined to participate in the last two editions in favour of competing in the [[WDF World Darts Championship|WDF World Championship]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Keogh |first=Frank |date=17 October 2025 |title=Greaves to play at worlds and on pro tour |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/ced69pvy365o |website=BBC Sport |access-date=17 October 2025}}</ref> A minimum of four women were guaranteed to participate in the tournament as part of the new World Championship qualification structure.<ref name="4W" /> Along with Greaves, [[2025 Women's World Matchplay|Women's World Matchplay]] champion [[Lisa Ashton]] and [[2025 PDC Women's Series|Women's Series]] players [[Fallon Sherrock]], [[Noa-Lynn van Leuven]] and [[Gemma Hayter (darts player)|Gemma Hayter]] qualified.<ref>{{cite web |date=19 October 2025 |title=Beau Greaves continues stunning Women's Series darts run as Noa-Lynn van Leuven, Gemma Hayter book World Championship spots |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13453098/beau-greaves-continues-stunning-womens-series-darts-run-as-noa-lynn-van-leuven-gemma-hayter-book-world-championship-spots |website=Sky Sports |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref> Sherrock entered as the only woman to have won matches at the tournament, while Van Leuven remained the only [[transgender]] player in the event's history.<ref>{{cite web |last=Keogh |first=Frank |date=24 November 2025 |title=Champion Littler starts Worlds against Labanauskas |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cpd28dg6l5eo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref>

[[Paul Lim]], who finished fifth on the [[2025 in PDC#Asian Tour|2025 PDC Asian Tour]], qualified for his first PDC World Championship since the [[2022 PDC World Darts Championship|2022 edition]]; at age 71, he extended his record as the oldest player to compete at the event.<ref name="Lim">{{cite web |date=12 September 2025 |title=World Darts Championship: Paul Lim qualifies aged 71 for Alexandra Palace return |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13429459/world-darts-championship-paul-lim-qualifies-aged-71-for-alexandra-palace-return |website=Sky Sports |access-date=13 September 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Phillips |first=Josh |date=13 December 2021 |title=Lim and Beaton among record-breakers in World Championship field |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2021/12/13/lim-and-beaton-among-record-breakers-world-championship-field |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211213212452/https://www.pdc.tv/news/2021/12/13/lim-and-beaton-among-record-breakers-world-championship-field |url-status=dead |archive-date=13 December 2021 |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=13 September 2025}}</ref> [[Simon Whitlock]], who finished as runner-up at the [[2010 PDC World Darts Championship|2010 World Championship]], confirmed his comeback by winning the newly-established [[2025 in PDC#ANZ Premier League|ANZ Premier League]], having missed the last World Championship to end a 15-year participation streak. Whitlock, [[Tim Pusey]] and Joe Comito comprised the Australian contingent to qualify, alongside seeded player [[Damon Heta]].<ref>{{cite web |last=Chadband |first=Ian |date=10 December 2025 |title=Back again: Wizard of Oz shoots for darts glory |url=https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9131494/back-again-wizard-of-oz-shoots-for-darts-glory/ |website=The Canberra Times |access-date=11 December 2025}}</ref> Spain's [[Cristo Reyes]] secured his return to the World Championship by winning the Mediterranean qualifier, having last competed at the [[2020 PDC World Darts Championship|2020 edition]].<ref name="Reyes17Nov">{{cite web |last=Simpson |first=Will |date=17 November 2025 |title=Reyes returns as six players seal Paddy Power World Darts Championship spot |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/november/17/reyes-returns-six-players-seal-paddy-power-world-darts-championship-spot |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=17 November 2025}}</ref>

Kenya and Argentina were represented for the first time after [[David Munyua]] and Jesús Salate earned their places in the tournament.<ref>{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=16 October 2025 |title=Further International Qualifiers confirmed for 2025/26 World Championship |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/october/16/further-international-qualifiers-confirmed-202526-world-championship |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=19 December 2025}}</ref> The final five places at the World Championship went to the successful players from the Tour Card holder qualifier: [[2020 Grand Slam of Darts|2020 Grand Slam]] champion [[José de Sousa]], Tavis Dudeney, [[Adam Hunt]], [[Stephen Burton (darts player)|Stephen Burton]] and [[Haupai Puha]].<ref name="TCHQ">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=24 November 2025 |title=De Sousa among five stars to secure final Alexandra Palace slots |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/november/24/de-sousa-among-five-stars-secure-final-alexandra-palace-slots |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref>

Searle claimed the opening set of his match with Littler, despite the defending champion producing a 10-dart leg and registering a set average of 105.<ref name="BBCSF">{{cite web |last=Drury |first=Sam |date=2 January 2026 |title=Littler to face Van Veen in PDC Worlds final |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/clyg258v51yo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=3 January 2026}}</ref> Littler responded with two breaks of throw on his way to winning the second set, soon taking control of the match and establishing a 4–1 lead.<ref name="SkySF">{{cite web |last=Wellens |first=Megan |date=3 January 2026 |title=World Darts Championship 2026: Luke Littler to face Gian van Veen in final after Dutchman holds off Gary Anderson in epic semi-final |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12040/13489762/world-darts-championship-2026-luke-littler-to-face-gian-van-veen-in-final-after-dutchman-holds-off-gary-anderson-in-epic-semi-final |website=Sky Sports |access-date=3 January 2026}}</ref> He completed a run of 10 consecutive legs won to extend his lead to 5–1, and although Searle capitalised on a failed nine-darter attempt from his opponent to land a 170 checkout, Littler added another set to win 6–1.<ref name="SF" /> He ended the match with a three-dart average of 105.35, his third average of the tournament above 105, and hit 10 maximums.<ref name="SF" /><ref name="RTESF">{{cite web |date=2 January 2026 |title=Luke Littler and rising star Gian van Veen set up PDC World Championship final showdown |url=https://www.rte.ie/sport/darts/2026/0102/1551260-littler-cruises-into-third-world-final-in-a-row/ |website=RTÉ Sport |access-date=3 January 2026 |agency=Press Association}}</ref> The win extended Littler's unbeaten record in major ranking semi-finals to 11.<ref name="BBCSF" /> Littler recounted his annoyance at losing the first set and telling himself that he would find the lead; he later said that Ryan Searle "can be proud" of the "amazing" tournament he had.<ref name="SkySF" />

In the second semi-final, Anderson took the first set but Van Veen started the second with a 10-dart leg, landing a 117 checkout to level the score at 1–1.<ref name="BBCSF" /> The Dutchman then won the next two sets to lead Anderson 3–1.<ref name="SkySF" /> Anderson opened the fifth set with a 10-dart break of throw and followed it with a 170 checkout to go 2–0 ahead in legs. However, Van Veen converted a 170 checkout of his own and claimed the set in a deciding leg to extend his advantage to 4–1, defying a set average of 117.44 from his opponent.<ref name="BBCSF" /> The sequence was dubbed a "set of darts from God" by Wayne Mardle on commentary.<ref name="SkySF" /> Anderson reduced the deficit to 4–3 after Van Veen missed darts at double to go one set away from victory, but Van Veen was able to claim the next two, completing a 6–3 win with a 13-dart leg.<ref name="BBCSF" /> The nine-set match, in which both players averaged over 102,<ref name="RTESF" /> was the longest World Championship semi-final in four years.<ref name="SkySF" /> Van Veen stated that he "enjoyed every single second" of the contest and claimed that his first dream was to simply be in a World Championship, rather than reach the final of one.<ref name="SF" />

Van Veen landed a 116 finish to win the opening leg of the match before claiming the set to take a 1–0 lead.<ref name="BBCFinal">{{cite web |last=Drury |first=Sam |date=3 January 2026 |title=Littler win the first two legs of the second set, but Littler rebounded and hit his own 116 finish to level at 1–1.<ref name="TelegraphFinal">{{cite web |last1=Wilson |first1=Jeremy |last2=McGrath |first2=Andy |date=3 January 2026 |title=Majestic Littler claims £1m windfall after retaining world title |url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/darts/2026/01/03/luke-littler-vs-gian-van-veen-live-score-world-championship/ |website=The Telegraph |access-date=4 January 2026}}</ref> He hit his second 170 checkout of the tournament to win the third set and lead 2–1.<ref name="PDCFinal" /> Littler claimed the fourth set without reply.<ref name="BBCFinal" /> Although Van Veen halted his momentum with a 137 checkout to open the fifth set, Littler won the next three legs to extend his lead to 4–1.<ref name="PDCFinal" /> The sixth set saw Van Veen leave a trace of blood on the dartboard, causing the board to be replaced; he later revealed that his little finger got stuck in a door while going to the toilet during the interval.<ref name="IndFinal">{{cite web |last=Ostlere |first=Lawrence |date=3 January Littler won the PDC World Championship for the second time, having only lost four sets across the tournament.<ref name="SkyNewsFinal">{{cite web |date=3 January 2026 |title=Luke Littler wins second straight World Darts Championship defeating Dutchman Gian van Veen |url=https://news.sky.com/story/luke-littler-wins-second-straight-world-darts-championship-13490124 |website=Sky News |access-date=4 January 2026}}</ref> He became the fourth player to win back-to-back PDC world titles—after Taylor, [[Adrian Lewis]] and Anderson—and the first player to retain the title since Anderson at the 2016 World Championship.<ref name="SkySFinal" /> He also became the seventh player to win the title on more than one occasion.<ref name="SkySFinal" /> His retention of the title ended a run of five successive different winners that had begun in 2021, the longest such sequence in the tournament's history.<ref>{{cite web |date=3 January 2025 |title=Littler wins PDC World Championship - 2025 results and roll of honour |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/darts/articles/cj6z73wj8klo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=5 January 2026}}</ref> Littler was the first recipient of the £1,000,000 top prize, extending his lead at the top of the PDC Order of Merit.<ref name="SkyNewsFinal" /> After being awarded the [[Sid Waddell]] Trophy by cyclist [[Chris Hoy]],<ref name="BBCFinal" /> Littler quoted boxer [[Anthony Joshua]] by saying: "The first time was so nice, I had to do it twice."<ref name="SkyNewsFinal" /> He emphasised his desire to follow his "very special" victory with more success, stating: "We've got to keep going, we've got to keep adding more titles. He's just the best."<ref name="BBCFinal" />

==Draw==
The draw for the tournament was held on 24 November 2025, conducted live on [[Sky Sports News]] and [[YouTube]] by former semi-finalists [[Wayne Mardle]] and [[Mark Webster (darts player)|Mark Webster]].<ref name="SkyQ">{{cite web |date=24 November 2025 |title=World Darts Championship: When is the draw? Who has qualified alongside Luke Littler, Luke Humphries for Alexandra Palace? |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12040/13472742/world-darts-championship-when-is-the-draw-who-has-qualified-alongside-luke-littler-luke-humphries-for-alexandra-palace |website=Sky Sports |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Braidwood |first=Jamie |date=24 November 2025 |title=Luke Littler discovers fate as World Darts Championship draw revealed |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/sport/darts/world-darts-championship-draw-luke-littler-first-match-b2871498.html |website=The Independent |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref><ref>{{Cite AV media |title=The Draw - 2025/26 Paddy Power World Darts Championship |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SIiSF8Nizzs |date=24 November 2025 |publisher=Professional Darts Corporation |via=YouTube |access-date=24 November 2025}}</ref> Of the 128 qualifiers, the top 32 players on the [[PDC Order of Merit]] were seeded in standard [[Seeding (sport)|seeding]] positions in the first round. The next qualifiers, ranked 33 to 64, were randomly drawn into the left hand side of the draw. These 64 players were matched up against the remaining 64 qualifiers, who were also drawn at random.<ref name="PDCQ"/>

Numbers to the left of a player's name show the 32 seeded players for the tournament. The 48 international qualifiers are indicated by 'Q'. The figures to the right of a player's name show their [[three-dart average]] in a match. Players in bold denote match winners.<ref name="Results"/><ref>{{cite web |title=PDC World Championship 11/12/2025 - 03/01/2026 |url=https://dartsdatabase.co.uk/display-event.php?eid=25678 |website=Darts Database |access-date=16 December 2025}}</ref>
