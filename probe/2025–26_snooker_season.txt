The '''2025–26 snooker season''' was a professional [[snooker]] season featuring tournaments played between June 2025 and May 2026, including the professional [[World Snooker Tour]], the second-tier [[Q Tour]] and featured events from [[World Women's Snooker]] and [[World Seniors Tour]].

== Overview ==
The professional tour produced 15 different ranking event winners, the most of any season,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Baker |first=Luke |date=26 December 2025 |title=The strange snooker stat set to shape the sport’s future |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/sport/snooker/snooker-tournament-winners-ronnie-osullivan-mark-selby-judd-trump-b2890158.html |access-date=6 May 2026 |website=[[The Independent]] |language=en}}</ref> with [[Wu Yize]] and [[Zhao Xintong]] the only players to win multiple ranking events. Wu secured his first ranking title at the [[2025 International Championship]], beating [[John Higgins]] 10–6 in the final,<ref>{{Cite web |date=9 November 2025 |title=Wu Yize beats John Higgins to win International Championship – his first ranking title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c07m2zjm83mo |access-date=10 November 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> and went on to win the [[2026 World Snooker Championship]], defeating [[Shaun Murphy]] 18–17 in the final. Wu became the second-youngest World Champion, after [[Stephen Hendry]] in [[1990 World Snooker Championship|1990]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sutcliffe |first=Steve |date=4 May 2026 |title=World Snooker Championship 2026: Wu Yize beats Shaun Murphy 18–17 to become second-youngest Crucible champion |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c202n0gkgvxo |access-date=5 May 2026 |website=BBC Sport |language=en-GB}}</ref> Zhao won the [[2026 World Grand Prix (snooker)|2026 World Grand Prix]], [[2026 Players Championship]], and [[2026 Tour Championship]], becoming the first player to win all three [[Players Series|Player Series]] tournaments in the same season.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Tour|first=World Snooker|date=5 April 2026|title=Zhao Beats Trump To Complete Players Series Slam|url=https://www.wst.tv//news/2026/april/05/zhao-beats-trump-to-complete-players-series-slam/|access-date=7 April 2026|website=World Snooker Tour|language=en}}</ref> After losing his first six ranking finals, [[Jack Lisowski]] won his maiden ranking title at the [[2025 Northern Ireland Open]], defeating [[Judd Trump]] 9–8 in the final.<ref>{{Cite web |last=McCabe |first=Jana |date=26 October 2025 |title=Northern Ireland Open: Jack Lisowski beats Jude Trump to seal first ranking event title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c4gp8rxly5jo |access-date=10 November 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> [[Alfie Burden]] claimed his first ranking title at the [[2025 Snooker Shoot Out|2025 Shoot Out]]; at the age of 48 years and 364 days, he became the oldest maiden ranking event winner.<ref>{{Cite web |date=13 December 2025 |title=Jubilant Burden wins first ranking title at Shoot Out |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/december/13/Jubilant-Burden-Wins-First-Ranking-Title-At-Shoot-Out-/ |access-date=24 January 2025 |website=World Snooker Tour}}</ref> For the first time, three consecutive ranking finals—the [[2025 Saudi Arabia Snooker Masters|2025 Saudi Arabia Masters]], [[2025 Wuhan Open (snooker)|2025 Wuhan Open]], and [[2025 English Open (snooker)|2025 English Open]]—went to {{Cuegloss|deciding frame|deciding frames}}.<ref name="EnglishOpen" /> The 2026 World Championship final was the fourth in Crucible history to go to a <dfn>deciding frame</dfn>, following the finals of the [[1985 World Snooker Championship final|1985]], [[1994 World Snooker Championship|1994]], and [[2002 World Snooker Championship|2002]] editions. 

The [[Class of '92 (snooker)|Class of '92]] continued to set records for their longevity in the sport. [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] won the [[2025 Xi'an Grand Prix]] aged 50 years and 206 days, becoming the oldest ranking event winner. He surpassed [[Ray Reardon]], who had won the [[1982 Professional Players Tournament]] aged 50 years and 14 days.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Cartwright |first=Phil |date=13 October 2025 |title=Xi'an Grand Prix 2025: Mark Williams, 50, wins title to become oldest ranking event winner |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cze65163222o |access-date=17 October 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> At the 2025 Saudi Arabia Masters, aged 49 years and 253 days, [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] became the oldest player to record an official [[maximum break]].<ref name="WST_ROS_147s" /> At the [[2026 Masters (snooker)|2026 Masters]], aged 50 years and 245 days, [[John Higgins]] became the oldest finalist in any [[Triple Crown (snooker)|Triple Crown]] tournament, again surpassing Reardon, who had been 50 years and 114 days old when he played in the [[1983 Masters (snooker)|1983 Masters final]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=17 January 2026 |title=Higgins into final with epic win over Trump |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/17/Higgins-Into-Final-With-Epic-Win-Over-Trump/ |access-date=26 January 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref> At the [[2026 Players Championship (snooker)|2026 Players Championship]], aged 50 years and 280 days, Higgins became the second-oldest finalist at any ranking tournament, surpassed only by [[Rex Williams]], who had been 53 years and 98 days old when he played in the final of the [[1986 Grand Prix (snooker)|1986 Grand Prix]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=21 February 2026 |title=Higgins pips Trump in thriller |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/february/21/higgins-pips-trump-in-thriller/ |access-date=26 February 2026 |website=World Snooker Tour |language=en}}</ref> 

The season set a new record for the most maximum breaks in a single season, with 24, surpassing the previous record of 15 set in the [[2024–25 snooker season|previous season]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=26 January 2026 |title=147s: The record that keeps being broken |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/26/147s--The-Record-That-Keeps-Being-Broken-/ |access-date=28 January 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref> O'Sullivan became the first player to make two maximum breaks in a one-session match, or on the same day, when he compiled the 16th and 17th maximums of his career in his 2025 Saudi Arabia Masters semi-final against [[Chris Wakelin]].<ref name="WST_ROS_147s">{{cite web |date=15 August 2025 |title=O'Sullivan makes two 147s in same match to earn huge bonus, and reaches final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/15/O-Sullivan-Makes-Two-147s-In-Same-Match-To-Earn-Huge-Bonus--And-Reaches-Final/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250815210327/https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/august/15/O-Sullivan-Makes-Two-147s-In-Same-Match-To-Earn-Huge-Bonus--And-Reaches-Final/ |archive-date=15 August 2025 |access-date=10 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref> At the [[2026 World Open (snooker)|2026 World Open]], O'Sullivan made the highest break in professional snooker history, a {{cuegloss|16-red clearance}} of 153 in his quarter-final match against [[Ryan Day (snooker player)|Ryan Day]]. It was only the second [[Maximum break#Breaks exceeding 147|break exceeding 147]] compiled in professional competition, following [[Jamie Burnett]]'s 148 in the qualifying rounds of the [[2004 UK Championship]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Salley |first=Emily |date=20 March 2026 |title=World Open: Ronnie O'Sullivan makes highest-ever professional break with historic 153 |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cn4391l3lvxo |access-date=20 March 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> At the [[2025 International Championship]] qualifiers in Sheffield, [[Neil Robertson]] made the 1,000th [[century break]] of his career, becoming the fourth player to reach that milestone, after O'Sullivan, Higgins, and Trump.<ref>{{Cite web |date=30 September 2025 |title=Robertson crafts 1,000th career century |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2025/september/30/robertson-crafts-1000/ |access-date=10 November 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en}}</ref> At the main stage of the same tournament, [[Bai Yulu]] made a century of 145, setting a new record for the highest break by a female player in professional competition.<ref>{{cite web |date=2 November 2025 |title=Bai makes history with 145 break |url=https://www.womenssnooker.com/bai-makes-history-with-145-break/ |access-date=10 November 2025 |website=[[World Women's Snooker]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>

[[Panchaya Channoi]] won the [[2026 World Women's Snooker Championship]], defeating [[Reanne Evans]] 6–2 in the final to claim her first women's world title.<ref>{{Cite web |date=19 May 2026 |title=Women's World Snooker Championship: Reanne Evans beaten 6-2 in final by Thai teenager Panchaya Channoi |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cwy2l2g1x57o |access-date=20 June 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> O'Sullivan made his [[World Seniors Tour]] debut at the [[2026 World Seniors Championship]] and went on to win the event, defeating [[Joe Perry (snooker player)|Joe Perry]] 10–4 in the final.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Collins |first=Ben |date=10 May 2026 |title=World Seniors Snooker Championship: Ronnie O'Sullivan beats Joe Perry 10-4 in final |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cr5p87qz91vo |access-date=20 June 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref>
