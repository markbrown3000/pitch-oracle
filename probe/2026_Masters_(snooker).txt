The '''2026 Masters''' (officially the '''2026 Johnstone's Paint Masters''') was a professional {{nowrap|non-ranking}} [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 11 to 18 January 2026 at [[Alexandra Palace]] in London, England. The second [[Triple Crown (snooker)|Triple Crown]] event of the [[2025–26 snooker season]], following the [[2025 UK Championship]] and preceding the [[2026 World Snooker Championship|2026 World Championship]], the tournament was the 52nd edition of the [[Masters (snooker)|Masters]], which had been held annually since [[1975 Masters (snooker)|1975]]. It was broadcast by the [[BBC]] and [[TNT Sports]] domestically, by [[Eurosport]] in mainland Europe, by local channels in China and elsewhere in Asia, and by [[WST Play]] in all other territories. The winner received £350,000 from a total prize fund of £1,015,000.

The top 16 players in the [[snooker world rankings]], as they stood after the UK Championship, were invited to the event. [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] withdrew on medical grounds and was replaced in the draw by [[Chris Wakelin]], who had been 17th in the world rankings after the UK Championship. [[Wu Yize]] and [[Xiao Guodong]] made their Masters debuts at the event, where they were among a record five participants from mainland China. Wu reached the semi-finals, the first debutant to do so since [[Yan Bingtao]] in [[2021 Masters (snooker)|2021]].

[[Shaun Murphy]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Kyren Wilson]] 10–7 in the [[2025 Masters (snooker)|2025 final]], but Wu beat him 6–2 in the first round. All eight first-round matches ended in 6–2 scorelines, the [[odds]] of which were estimated at 220,000 to 1. [[John Higgins]] made a record-extending 32nd Masters appearance and reached the final, becoming the oldest Triple Crown finalist in snooker history, aged {{Age in years and days|18 May 1975|18 January 2026|sep=and}}. Kyren Wilson reached his third Masters final, where he defeated Higgins 10–6 to win his first Masters title and second Triple Crown title. The final of the event was the last match to feature televised commentary by broadcaster [[John Virgo]], who died 17 days later. The tournament produced 24 [[century break]]s, of which the highest was a 137 by Wu in his first-round match against Murphy.

=== Broadcasters and viewership ===
The tournament was broadcast in the United Kingdom by the [[BBC]] and in the UK and Ireland by [[TNT Sports]] and [[Discovery+]]. It was broadcast in mainland Europe by [[Eurosport]], with streaming coverage on Discovery+ in Germany, Italy, and Austria and on [[HBO Max]] in other European territories. It was broadcast in mainland China by [[CCTV-5]], [[Huya Live]], {{ill|Migu (streaming service)|lt=Migu|zh|咪咕}}, the CBSA‑WPBSA Academy [[WeChat]] Channel, and the CBSA‑WPBSA Academy [[Douyin]]. It was broadcast in Hong Kong by [[Now TV (Hong Kong)|Now TV]], in Thailand by [[True Sports|TrueSports]], in Taiwan by Sportcast, in Mongolia by N Sports, in the Philippines by [[TAP Sports]], and in Malaysia and Brunei by [[Astro SuperSport]]. In territories where no other coverage was available, the tournament was streamed by [[World Snooker Tour|WST Play]].<ref>{{cite web |date=7 January 2026 |title=How to watch the 2026 Johnstone's Paint Masters |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/07/how-to-watch-the-2026-johnstone-s-paint-masters-/ |access-date=8 January 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423082104/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/07/how-to-watch-the-2026-johnstone-s-paint-masters-/|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

For the first three days of the tournament, some viewers, including competitor [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]], reported that the table appeared on their screens as square rather than rectangular. At the beginning of the 14 January afternoon session, BBC commentator [[John Virgo]] confirmed that adjustments had been made to the camera angles used for filming.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Colman |first=Jonty |date=13 January 2026 |title=Why did the table at the Masters look square on TV? |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c7v0rejrrg8o |access-date=23 April 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423074026/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c7v0rejrrg8o|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> The tournament attracted a peak audience of 2.1&nbsp;million for the final on the BBC, an increase of 2 percent over the peak audience for the previous year's final. Total domestic viewer hours over the entire tournament were 50.4&nbsp;million across the BBC and TNT Sports combined, an increase over the 43&nbsp;million recorded the previous year. The 4.65&nbsp;million streams recorded on [[BBC iPlayer]] represented a 22 percent increase over the previous year's figure.<ref>{{Cite web |date=26 January 2026 |title=Increase in viewing figures for Masters snooker with peak over two million |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/26/increase-in-viewing-figures-for-masters-snooker-with-peak-over-two-million/ |access-date=26 January 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423082103/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/26/increase-in-viewing-figures-for-masters-snooker-with-peak-over-two-million/|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== First round ===
[[File:Ebdon Murphy Masters 2025.jpg|thumb|The defending champion [[Shaun Murphy]] ''(pictured right at the previous year's event)'' lost 2–6 in the first round to debutant [[Wu Yize]].]]
The first-round matches were played from 11 to 14 January.<ref name="BBC_schedule" /><ref name="s.o_main" /> Facing the defending champion [[Shaun Murphy]], tournament debutant [[Wu Yize]] won the first three {{cuegloss|frames}}, making a [[century break]] of 137 in frame two. Murphy took frame four, but Wu won frame five on the last {{Cuegloss|black ball|black}} to lead 4–1. Murphy won frame six, but Wu took the last two frames for a 6–2 win, his first victory over Murphy. "After the first frame, I felt a bit more relaxed and I totally enjoyed the atmosphere," Wu said afterwards. Murphy, who made a highest break of just 49, said: "I'm very disappointed. I've been working very hard [...] so I didn't expect to play like that and I'm as shocked as anyone."<ref name="BBC_26-01-11">{{Cite web |last=Emons |first=Michael |date=11 January 2026 |title=Murphy & Selby lose on thrilling Masters first day|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c93vy5j73vxo |access-date=23 April 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423074003/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c93vy5j73vxo|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=11 January 2026 |title=Rising star Wu ends Murphy's reign |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/11/Rising-Star-Wu-End-Murphy-s-Reign/ |access-date=11 January 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423081121/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/11/Rising-Star-Wu-End-Murphy-s-Reign/|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=11 January 2026 |title=Shaun Murphy crashes out of Masters on opening day of title defence |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/jan/11/champion-shaun-murphy-crashes-out-of-masters-on-opening-day |access-date=12 January 2026 |work=[[The Guardian]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423081123/https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/jan/11/champion-shaun-murphy-crashes-out-of-masters-on-opening-day|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> The eighth seed [[Mark Selby]] faced the other tournament debutant, [[Xiao Guodong]], who made breaks of 95, 62, 76, and 77 as he won the first four frames. Selby produced breaks of 101 and 76 to win frames five and six, but Xiao took frame seven with a century of 118 after Selby missed a {{Cuegloss|blue ball|blue}} to a {{Cuegloss|centre pocket}}. Xiao secured a 6–2 victory in frame eight after Selby missed a {{Cuegloss|yellow ball|yellow}} along the {{Cuegloss|baulk cushion}}. "In the first four frames I put [Selby] under pressure and didn't miss many balls," said Xiao afterwards. "But I knew he would come back. The seventh frame was very important and when he missed the blue I made a century. At 4–3 it would have been very different." Selby commented: "If I had won [frame seven] I would have fancied winning the match because I had the momentum. But Xiao did what he did all night, punished my mistakes." The victories by Wu and Xiao marked the first time since the [[2009 Masters (snooker)|2009 event]] that two Masters debutants had won their opening matches.<ref name="BBC_26-01-11" /><ref>{{Cite web |date=11 January 2026 |title=Xiao shines on debut to stun Selby |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/11/Xiao-Shines-On-Debut-To-Stun-Selby/ |access-date=11 January 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423081119/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/11/Xiao-Shines-On-Debut-To-Stun-Selby/|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

The sixth seed [[Mark Williams (snooker player)|Mark Williams]] faced [[Mark Allen (snooker player)|Mark Allen]], who had been ill with food poisoning for a week before the tournament. Allen took the opening frame with a 67 break, but Williams won the next two. Allen then produced breaks including 54, 60, 125, and 70 as he won five consecutive frames for a 6–2 victory, beating Williams at the event for the first time and reaching his 11th Masters quarter-final. "I couldn't prepare for this at all as I've been lying in bed all week with food poisoning, so I just thought 'go out there and give my best'. === Final ===
[[File:Kyren Wilson Masters 2026 3.jpg|thumb|[[Kyren Wilson]] ''(pictured at the event)'' played in his third Masters final. He defeated [[John Higgins]] 10–6 to win his first Masters title and second [[Triple Crown (snooker)|Triple Crown]] title.]]
The final took place on 18 January as the best of 19 frames, played over two {{cuegloss|sessions}}, between the fourth seed Kyren Wilson and the seventh seed John Higgins.<ref name="BBC_schedule" /><ref name="s.o_main" /> Wilson was competing in his third Masters final, having previously been runner-up in [[2018 Masters (snooker)|2018]] and [[2025 Masters (snooker)|2025]]. He was the first player since O'Sullivan in [[2016 Masters (snooker)|2016]] and [[2017 Masters (snooker)|2017]] to reach back-to-back Masters finals.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://snookerhq.com/records/masters-snooker-champions/|title=Complete List of Masters Snooker Champions|website=SnookerHQ|access-date=24 August 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260824074039/https://snookerhq.com/records/masters-snooker-champions/|archive-date=24 August 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> Higgins played in his sixth Masters final, having won the title in [[1999 Masters (snooker)|1999]] and [[2006 Masters (snooker)|2006]] and been runner-up in [[1995 Masters (snooker)|1995]], [[2005 Masters (snooker)|2005]], and [[2021 Masters (snooker)|2021]]. At the age of {{Age in years and days|18 May 1975|18 January 2026|sep=and}}, he became the oldest finalist in any Triple Crown tournament, surpassing [[Ray Reardon]], who had been {{Age in years and days|8 October 1932|30 January 1983|sep=and}} old when he played in the [[1983 Masters (snooker)|1983 Masters final]].<ref name="WST_Higgins_F" /> Higgins became the first player to contest Masters finals in his teens, 20s, 30s, 40s, and 50s.<ref name="WST_Wils_Higg_1" /> It was the first time the two players had met at the Masters or in any tournament final.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Day |first=Michael |date=18 January 2026 |title=Who is playing in Sunday's 2026 Masters snooker final? Preview of Wilson v Higgins |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/masters/who-is-playing-in-sundays-2026-masters-snooker-final-preview-of-wilson-v-higgins-in-london-5479795 |access-date=18 January 2026 |website=Totally Snookered |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423075642/https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/masters/who-is-playing-in-sundays-2026-masters-snooker-final-preview-of-wilson-v-higgins-in-london-5479795|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Tatiana Woollaston]] refereed her first Masters final.<ref>{{Cite tweet |user=WeAreWST |number=2012829650645287014 |title=A first Masters final for @tatiana_referee!|access-date=23 April 2026}}</ref> The final was the last match to feature televised commentary by [[John Virgo]], who died 17 days later, on 4 February, at age 79.<ref>{{Cite news |last=McLaughlin |first=Luke |date=2026-02-04 |title=John Virgo, former snooker player and broadcaster, dies aged 79 |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/feb/04/john-virgo-former-snooker-player-and-broadcaster-dies |access-date=2026-02-04 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423075534/https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/feb/04/john-virgo-former-snooker-player-and-broadcaster-dies|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

Higgins won the opening frame with a 58 break. He made another {{cuegloss|half-century}} in frame two, but Wilson took the frame with a 58 clearance after Higgins missed two pots with the {{Cuegloss|rest}}. Wilson also won frame three. Higgins was on a 58 break in frame four when he missed a red, and Wilson cleared again for a 3–1 lead at the mid-session interval. Higgins won the 38-minute fifth frame and came from behind to take the sixth, tying the scores at 3–3. Wilson then produced back-to-back centuries of 103 and 111 to end the afternoon session with a 5–3 lead. Wilson's 103 in frame seven was the 750th century break in Masters history.<ref name="WST_Wils_Higg_1">{{Cite web |date=18 January 2026 |title=Wilson 5–3 up in Masters final |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/18/Wilson-5-3-Up-In-Masters-Final/ |access-date=18 January 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423075814/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/18/Wilson-5-3-Up-In-Masters-Final/|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

When play resumed for the second session, Higgins made a break of 71 to win frame nine, but Wilson took frame ten after Higgins missed a double on the penultimate red, moving 6–4 ahead. In the 11th frame, Higgins was leading by 46 points when he missed the black. Wilson got the snooker he required and attempted a clearance but missed the last pink, leading to a safety exchange; Higgins secured the frame after Wilson went in {{Cuegloss|in-off}}. Wilson won the 12th frame to lead 7–5 at the mid-session interval. In frame 13, Higgins made a safety error on the last brown, and Wilson took a three-frame lead for the first time at 8–5. Wilson made a 78 break in frame 14 to move one frame from victory at 9–5. He had a match-winning opportunity in frame 15 but missed the black off its spot, and Higgins took the frame with a 70 break. In frame 16, Wilson took a 32-point lead and made a further break of 24 to win the match 10–6, securing his first Masters title. It was Wilson's second Triple Crown title, following his win at the [[2024 World Snooker Championship|2024 World Championship]].<ref name="BBC_26-01-18">{{Cite web |last=Sutcliffe |first=Steve |date=18 January 2026 |title=Wilson defeats Higgins to claim first Masters title |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cm2jlvmz1xdo |access-date=23 April 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423074105/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/cm2jlvmz1xdo|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="WST_Wils_Higg_2">{{Cite web |date=18 January 2026 |title=Wilson lands maiden Masters |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/18/Wilson-Lands-Maiden-Masters/ |access-date=18 January 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260423075729/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/january/18/Wilson-Lands-Maiden-Masters/|archive-date=23 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

"In 2018 when I lost in the final I cried like a little girl, and I am trying not to cry now because it means so much to me," said Wilson after the match. "[Higgins] is an idol of mine. It was a dog fight from the start today and I just tried to be as dogged as [Higgins] has been over the years. [...] I will be proud of this win for the rest of my life." Higgins, whom BBC Sport journalist Steve Sutcliffe described as "visibly weakening" and making "uncharacteristic mistakes" as the match progressed, commented afterwards: "The crowd were fantastic and so were the conditions, but I was useless. [Wilson] was by far the better player. He was totally dominant. The scoreline is 10–6 but it wasn't as close as that. It's disappointing because the last two Masters finals I have played in, I haven't kept the same form which got me to the final."<ref name="BBC_26-01-18" /><ref name="WST_Wils_Higg_2" />
