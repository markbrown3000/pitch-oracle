The '''2026 Poland Darts Open''' (known for sponsorship reasons as the '''2026 SUPERBET Poland Darts Open''') was a professional [[darts]] tournament that took place at the [[EXPO Kraków]] in [[Kraków]], Poland, from 20 to 22 February 2026.<ref>{{cite web |date=26 January 2026 |title=Historic debut in Kraków: Just one month until Poland hosts its first European Tour |url=https://www.pdc-europe.tv/news/one-month-to-go-superbet-poland-darts-open-2026-to-turn-krakow-into-a-darts-hotspot/ |website=PDC Europe |access-date=6 February 2026}}</ref> It was the first of fifteen [[PDC European Tour]] events on the [[2026 PDC Pro Tour]], and was the first European Tour event to be held in Poland.<ref>{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=11 September 2025 |title=European Tour set for further expansion in 2026 with PDC calendar released |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/september/11/european-tour-set-further-expansion-2026-pdc-calendar-released/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=2 June 2026}}</ref> It featured a field of 48 players and £230,000 in prize money, with £35,000 going to the winner.

[[Luke Littler]] and [[Gian van Veen]] contested the final, where Van Veen hit a [[nine-dart finish]] in the fourth [[Leg (darts)|leg]]. Littler won the tournament, his fifth European Tour title, by defeating Van Veen 8–4.

==Prize money==
As part of a mass boost in prize money for [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC) events in 2026, the prize fund for all [[2026 PDC Pro Tour#European Tour#European Tour|2026 European Tour]] events rose to £230,000, of which the winner will receive £35,000.<ref>{{cite web |last=Phillips |first=Josh |date=31 March 2025 |title=Biggest prize money increase in PDC history confirmed |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/march/31/biggest-prize-money-increase-pdc-history-confirmed |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=24 January 2026}}</ref><ref name="MCOverview">{{cite web |title=Poland Darts Open 2026  |url=https://mastercaller.com/tournaments/poland-darts-open/2026 |website=Mastercaller |access-date=14 November 2025}}</ref>

==Qualification and format==
The top 16 players on the two-year [[PDC Order of Merit]] will be [[Seeding (sports)|seeded]] and will enter the tournament in the second round, while the next 16 highest-ranked players from the one-year [[PDC Pro Tour Order of Merit]] automatically qualified for the first round. The seedings were confirmed on 5 February.<ref name="Entries">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=5 February 2026 |title=2026 ET1-2 Seeds & Tour Card Holder qualifier entries confirmed |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/february/05/2026-et1-2-seeds---tour-card-holder-qualifier-entries-confirmed |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=5 February 2026}}</ref><ref name="Race">{{cite web |title=Race to ET1 and ET2 2026 |url=https://www.dartsrankings.com/et2 |website=dartsrankings.com |access-date=14 November 2025}}</ref> The remaining 16 places will go to players from four qualifying events – 10 from the Tour Card Holder Qualifier (held on 11 February),<ref>{{cite web |date=11 February 2026 |title=PDC ET01 Tour Card Holder Qualifier |url=https://tv.dartconnect.com/event/pdceutcq26et01/matches |website=DartConnect}}</ref> four from the Host Nation Qualifier (held on 19 February),<ref name="HNQ">{{cite web |date=19 February 2026 |title=PDC ET01 Host Nation Qualifier |url=https://tv.dartconnect.com/event/pdc26et01hnq/matches |website=DartConnect}}</ref> one from the Nordic & Baltic Associate Member Qualifier (held on 6 February),<ref>{{cite web |date=6 February 2026 |title=PDCNB 2026 Tour - Latvia - Weekend 01 |url=https://tv.dartconnect.com/event/pdcnbpro26e01/matches |website=DartConnect}}</ref> and one from the East European Associate Member Qualifier (held on 24 January).<ref>{{cite web |date=24 January 2026 |title=PDC ET01 E. Europe Qualifier |url=https://tv.dartconnect.com/event/pdceeu26et01q/matches |website=DartConnect}}</ref>

After the draw was made, [[Gerwyn Price]] and [[Michael van Gerwen]] withdrew from the tournament due to medical reasons. [[Michael Smith (darts player)|Michael Smith]] and [[Callan Rydz]] replaced them from the reserve list, going straight in at the second-round stage.<ref>{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=20 February 2026 |title=Price & Van Gerwen withdraw from SUPERBET Poland Darts Open |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/february/20/price---van-gerwen-withdraw-from-superbet-poland-darts-open/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=22 February 2026}}{{dead link|date=February 2026|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref>

===Second round===
[[File:Michael Smith 6-3 Steve Lennon - Michael Smith - 2019249215612 2019-09-06 PDC European Darts Matchplay - 1068 - B70I6085.jpg|thumb|The [[2023 PDC World Darts Championship|2023]] world champion [[Michael Smith (darts player)|Michael Smith]] ''(pictured in 2019)'' entered the tournament following [[Gerwyn Price]]'s withdrawal. He defeated [[Joe Cullen (darts player)|Joe Cullen]] 6{{nbnd}}5 in the second round.]]
The second round (best of 11 legs) was played on 21 February.<ref name="PDCR2">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=21 February 2026 |title=Sensational Littler produces fireworks at Poland Darts Open |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/february/21/sensational-littler-fireworks-poland-darts-open |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=22 February 2026}}</ref> Luke Littler recorded a three-dart average of 113.84 and hit 100 per cent of his doubles as he defeated Mike De Decker 6–1 to open his campaign. "It's not gone my way in the first three weeks of the [[2026 Premier League Darts|Premier League]], but I certainly want to be the first winner here in Poland this weekend," said Littler afterwards.<ref name="PDCR2"/> After initially taking a 4–1 lead, world number two [[Luke Humphries]] was brought to a [[Glossary of darts#Decider|deciding leg]] by Krzysztof Ratajski, where Humphries eventually secured a 6–5 win. Speaking about the partisan Polish crowd after the match, Humphries said: "It's just sometimes, when you get crowds that, unfortunately, don't give you that kind of fair game that you want it is hard for me to perform."<ref>{{cite web |date=22 February 2026 |title=Poland Darts Open: Luke Littler hits six doubles from six in victory as Luke Humphries criticises crowd behaviour |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13510353/poland-darts-open-luke-littler-hits-six-doubles-from-six-in-victory-as-luke-humphries-criticises-crowd-behaviour |website=Sky Sports |access-date=23 February 2026}}</ref> [[2026 PDC World Darts Championship|World Championship]] runner-up [[Gian van Veen]], the reigning [[European Championship (darts)|European champion]], also survived a last-leg decider, earning a 6–5 victory over Andrew Gilding.<ref>{{cite web |last=Battison |first=Paul |date=21 February 2026 |title=Littler thrashes De Decker at Polish Darts Open |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/c743w83d38jo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=22 February 2026}}</ref> [[Jonny Clayton]], who entered the tournament having won a Premier League night in [[Glasgow]], defeated [[Adam Gawlas]] 6–4, while [[Stephen Bunting]] overcame 17 missed darts at double to beat Niels Zonneveld by the same scoreline. The [[2023 PDC World Darts Championship|2023]] world champion [[Michael Smith (darts player)|Michael Smith]] replaced [[Gerwyn Price]] in the second round and claimed a 6–5 victory over Joe Cullen; on the other hand, Luke Woodhouse won five consecutive legs to win 6–4 against reserve player [[Callan Rydz]], who replaced [[Michael van Gerwen]].<ref name="RTER2">{{cite web |date=21 February 2026 |title=Luke Littler coasts into last 16 at Poland Darts Open |url=https://www.rte.ie/sport/darts/2026/0221/1559739-littler-coasts-into-last-16-at-poland-darts-open/ |website=RTÉ Sport |access-date=22 February 2026}}</ref>

[[Josh Rock]] began his tournament by averaging 102.98 on his way to beating [[William O'Connor (darts player)|William O'Connor]] 6–1, while [[Ross Smith (darts player)|Ross Smith]] claimed his own 6–1 win against Daryl Gurney. [[Wessel Nijman]] averaged 103.46 in his 6–2 victory over two-time world champion [[Gary Anderson (darts player)|Gary Anderson]]. "I don't know how I keep beating him," remarked Nijman, who achieved his fourth consecutive win against Anderson.<ref name="PDCR2"/> [[James Wade]] and Connor Scutt suffered [[Whitewash (sport)|whitewash]] defeats to Cameron Menzies and [[Chris Dobey]], respectively. Cristo Reyes progressed to the final day of a European Tour event for the first time since 2018, beating [[Ryan Searle (darts player)|Ryan Searle]] 6–2. [[Nathan Aspinall]], the top performer on the [[2025 PDC Pro Tour#European Tour|2025 European Tour]], defeated Darryl Pilgrim 6–3. [[Danny Noppert]] missed three [[Glossary of darts#Match dart|match darts]] in his 6–5 loss to Jermaine Wattimena, while Boris Krčmar continued his run by winning 6–4 against [[Martin Schindler]].<ref name="PDCR2"/><ref name="RTER2"/>

===Final day===
[[File:Superbet Poland Darts Open 2026 - trofeum.jpg|thumb|left|The Poland Darts Open trophy]]
The third round, quarter-finals, semi-finals and final were played on 22 February. The third round and quarter-finals were contested over the best of 11 legs, the semi-finals over the best of 13 legs, and the final over the best of 15 legs.<ref name="PDCDay3">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=22 February 2026 |title=Sublime Littler defies nine-dart Van Veen to win Poland Darts Open |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/february/22/superb-littler-defies-van-veen-poland-darts-open |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=23 February 2026}}</ref> The final day saw Luke Littler and Gian van Veen reach the final. Littler overturned a 2–0 deficit to beat Ross Smith 6–2—his seventh straight win against the Englishman—before securing comfortable victories against Josh Rock and Chris Dobey, averaging 105.91 in the latter contest. Van Veen defeated Nathan Aspinall in a deciding leg after initially going 4–2 down, and followed that win by beating Wessel Nijman 6–2 with a three-dart average of 107.50. In the semi-finals, Van Veen defeated Luke Humphries 7–5 to set up a tie with Littler, marking his seventh triumph over Humphries in eight meetings. A rematch of the 2026 World Championship final that Littler won 7–1, Van Veen was looking to avenge that loss and claim his maiden European Tour title, while Littler aimed to add to his four European Tour titles.<ref name="PDCDay3"/><ref name="SkyDay3">{{cite web |date=23 February 2026 |title=Luke Littler defies nine-dart Gian van Veen to win first-ever Poland Darts Open and warm up for Premier League in Belfast in style |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13510834/luke-littler-defies-nine-dart-gian-van-veen-to-win-first-ever-poland-darts-open-and-warm-up-for-premier-league-in-belfast-in-style |website=Sky Sports |access-date=23 February 2026}}</ref>

[[File:2025-04-03 Premier League Darts Berlin 2025 by Sandro Halank–124.jpg|thumb|[[Luke Littler]] ''(pictured in 2025)'' won the inaugural Poland Darts Open by defeating [[Gian van Veen]] 8–4 in the final.]]
The first four legs of the final were [[Glossary of darts#Hold|holds of throw]].<ref name="IndFinal">{{cite web |date=22 February 2026 |title=Luke Littler clinches Poland Darts Open with victory over Gian Van Veen |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/sport/luke-littler-dutch-poland-chris-dobey-b2925420.html |website=The Independent |access-date=23 February 2026 |agency=Press Association}}</ref> In the fifth leg, Van Veen followed back-to-back maximums with a 141 checkout to complete a [[nine-dart finish]] and go 3–2 ahead, his first nine-darter on stage. The 141 finish was his second [[Glossary of darts#Ton|ton]]-plus checkout of the match, having converted 112 earlier in the contest.<ref name="SkyDay3"/> Littler then took control, winning the next four legs to lead 6–3.<ref name="BBCFinal">{{cite web |last=Battison |first=Paul |date=23 February 2026 |title=Littler wins in Poland despite Van Veen nine-darter |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cd708p32j8eo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=23 February 2026}}</ref> Van Veen ended Littler's run with a 74 finish in the tenth leg, but Littler claimed the next two in a total of 28 darts to win the match 8–4, pinning double 10 to secure victory.<ref name="SkyDay3"/><ref name="IndFinal"/> He finished the match with seven maximums and a three-dart average of 108.06.<ref name="BBCFinal"/>

Littler won the inaugural Poland Darts Open, his fifth European Tour title in 714 days. In his post-match interview, he revealed that Van Veen's nine-dart finish spurred him on to victory. "In the past people have hit nine-darters against me and it's a big achievement. It's not easy to do. In the final, so early, I was like just go for him now while he's a bit overwhelmed," he commented, adding that he hoped he could take his performances onto the next Premier League night in [[Belfast]].<ref name="SkyDay3"/> Speaking in defeat, Van Veen admitted that he wished he missed the nine-darter, explaining: "Of course I'm very happy to hit the nine-darter, but I think everyone noticed in the next two or three legs, I wasn't good. I was so excited about the nine-darter—my first one on the stage, and that probably cost me the final today." He added: "I'm getting a bit tired of losing finals now, but it's part of the game and I'm happy to make another final. It's been a great weekend."<ref name="PDCDay3"/>
