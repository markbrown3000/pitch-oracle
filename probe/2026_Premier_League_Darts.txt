'''2026 Premier League Darts''' (known for sponsorship reasons as the '''2026 BetMGM Premier League''')<ref name="PDCFixtures"/> was a professional [[darts]] tournament organised by the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC). The 22nd edition of [[Premier League Darts]], the event began on 5 February 2026 at the [[Newcastle Arena]] in [[Newcastle upon Tyne]] and ended with the play-offs at [[the O2 Arena]] in London on 28 May 2026. The total prize fund was £1,250,000, with the overall winner receiving £350,000 and each weekly winner receiving a £10,000 bonus.

The eight-player tournament began with a 16-week league stage. Following the league stage, the top four players competed in the play-offs. The 2026 edition marked the introduction of Belgium to the schedule, with a night in [[Antwerp]] being included for the first time. [[Gian van Veen]] and [[Josh Rock]] made their Premier League debuts. Rock hit a [[nine-dart finish]] during his 6–2 loss to Van Veen on night four.

[[Luke Humphries]] entered the tournament as the defending champion, having defeated [[Luke Littler]] 11–8 in the [[2025 Premier League Darts|2025 final]]. Littler equalled his own record of six nightly wins during the league stage, which he set the previous year. The pair contested the final for a third consecutive year, where Littler defeated Humphries 11–10 to win his second Premier League title.

==Overview==
===Background===
2026 [[Premier League Darts]] was the 22nd edition of the tournament to be staged by the [[Professional Darts Corporation]] (PDC) since the [[2005 Premier League Darts|inaugural event]] in 2005.<ref>{{cite web |title=Premier League |url=https://mastercaller.com/tournaments/premier-league |website=Mastercaller |access-date=5 January 2026}}</ref> The inaugural event was played in venues across the United Kingdom and was won by [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]], who defeated [[Colin Lloyd]] 16–4 in the final.<ref>{{cite web |date=1 June 2005 |title=Taylor Powers to title |url=https://www.irishexaminer.com/sport/arid-30205082.html |website=Irish Examiner |access-date=12 September 2025}}</ref><ref name="Where">{{cite web |last=Prenderville |first=Paul |date=24 February 2018 |title=Where next for Premier League Darts? |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/11262126/where-next-for-premier-league-darts |website=Sky Sports |access-date=12 September 2025}}</ref> Taylor won six of the first eight editions of the tournament, only being stopped by [[James Wade]] in [[2009 Premier League Darts|2009]] and [[Gary Anderson (darts player)|Gary Anderson]] in [[2011 Premier League Darts|2011]].<ref>{{cite web |date=5 February 2025 |title=Premier League Darts 2025: Schedule, venues, players, format as Luke Littler returns as defending champion |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13284669/premier-league-darts-2025-schedule-venues-players-format-as-luke-littler-returns-as-defending-champion |website=Sky Sports |access-date=12 September 2025}}</ref> The amount of participants in the event has varied over time. Originally consisting of seven players, a wildcard spot was introduced for the [[2007 Premier League Darts|2007 edition]] to bring the total to eight.<ref>{{cite web |date=8 January 2007 |title=Premier League Darts to continue to 2010 |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/premier-league-darts-to-continue-to-2010 |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=12 September 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Wilson |first=Steve |date=19 January 2007 |title=Lewis joins the Premier League |url=https://www.bournemouthecho.co.uk/news/1135634.lewis-joins-the-premier-league/ |website=Bournemouth Echo |access-date=12 September 2025}}</ref> The number increased to ten ahead of the [[2013 Premier League|2013 edition]],<ref>{{cite web |date=2 January 2013 |title=McCoy's Premier League expanded |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2013/01/02/mccoys-premier-league-expanded |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=12 September 2025}}{{dead link|date=February 2026|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref> before reverting back to eight players in [[2022 Premier League Darts|2022]].<ref name="22F">{{cite web |date=3 February 2022 |title=Premier League Darts: Tournament to undergo the most radical change in its history |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/12515863/premier-league-darts-tournament-to-undergo-the-most-radical-change-in-its-history |website=Sky Sports |access-date=12 September 2025}}</ref> The tournament has expanded outside of the United Kingdom, with nights held in Germany, the Netherlands and Ireland, as well as Belgium from 2026 onward.<ref name="PDCBel">{{Cite web |date=11 September 2025|title=BetMGM Premier League heading to Belgium as 2026 schedule confirmed|url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/september/11/betmgm-premier-league-heading-belgium-2026-schedule-confirmed|access-date=11 September 2025 |website=Professional Darts Corporation}}</ref><ref name="Where"/>

The 2026 Premier League took place from 5 February to 28 May 2026.<ref name="Ant">{{cite web |date=11 September 2025 |title=Premier League Darts 2026 dates and schedule confirmed: Antwerp replaces Exeter |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13428630/premier-league-darts-2026-dates-and-schedule-confirmed-antwerp-replaces-exeter |website=Sky Sports |access-date=17 September 2025}}</ref> Sportsbook [[BetMGM]] continued its sponsorship of the event after becoming title sponsor in 2024.<ref>{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=4 January 2024 |title=BetMGM stars as new title sponsor of Premier League Darts |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2024/january/04/betmgm-stars-new-title-sponsor-premier-league-darts |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=17 September 2025}}{{dead link|date=February 2026|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref> [[Luke Humphries]] entered the tournament as defending champion after defeating [[Luke Littler]] 11–8 in the [[2025 Premier League Darts|2025 final]] to win his first Premier League title.<ref>{{cite web |last=Bower |first=Aaron |date=29 May 2025 |title=Luke Humphries battles back to dethrone Luke Littler in Premier League final |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2025/may/29/luke-humphries-dethrone-luke-littler-premier-league-final |website=The Guardian |access-date=17 September 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=29 May 2025 |title=Premier League Darts final: Luke Humphries beats Luke Littler to claim first title |url=https://www.espn.com/darts/story/_/id/45379805/premier-league-darts-final-luke-humphries-luke-littler-title |website=ESPN |access-date=17 September 2025}}</ref>

===Format===
The 2026 Premier League used the same format that was introduced for the 2022 edition.<ref name="2026Format">{{cite web |date=2 January 2026 |title=Premier League Darts: Line-up revealed on Sky Sports News as PDC confirm no format changes to 2026 edition as Luke Humphries, Luke Littler chase title |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12040/13488062/premier-league-darts-pdc-confirms-format-to-remain-the-same-in-2026-as-luke-humphries-luke-littler-chase-title-again |website=Sky Sports |access-date=2 January 2026}}</ref> It remained an eight-person knockout bracket every Thursday night. The players were guaranteed to meet each other once in the quarter-finals throughout the first seven weeks, and once in the quarter-finals in weeks 9–15, with weeks 8 and 16 being drawn based on the league standings at that point. Players received two points per semi-final finish, three points per runner-up finish, and five points per final win. Following the league phase, the top four players in the table contested the two play-off semi-finals with 1st playing 4th and 2nd playing 3rd, where the winners progressed to the Premier League final.<ref name="26LineupSky" />

All matches were played in [[Leg (darts)|leg]] format, with the number of legs required to win increasing as the tournament progressed:
*'''League stage''': Best of 11 legs
*'''Semi-finals''': Best of 19 legs
*'''Final''': Best of 21 legs

===Prize money===
As part of the PDC's biggest prize money increase in its history, the prize fund for the 2026 Premier League was £1,250,000, up from £1,000,000 the previous year. The champion received £350,000 and each night's winner during the league phase earned a £10,000 bonus.<ref>{{cite web |last=Phillips |first=Josh |date=31 March 2025 |title=Biggest prize money increase in PDC history confirmed |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2025/march/31/biggest-prize-money-increase-pdc-history-confirmed |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=17 September 2025}}</ref>

It was also available for subscribers outside of Germany, Austria and Switzerland on the PDC's streaming service, PDCTV.<ref>{{cite web |last=Simpson |first=Will |date=5 February 2026 |title=Where to watch the 2026 BetMGM Premier League |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/february/05/where-to-watch-the-2026-betmgm-premier-league |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=14 February 2026}}</ref>

==Summary==
===Night 1 – Night 4===
[[File:2026-03-26 Premier League Darts – Night 8 – Berlin 2026 by Sandro Halank–045.jpg|thumb|Seven-time champion [[Michael van Gerwen]] ''(pictured on night eight)'' won the opening night, having failed to win a night during the [[2025 Premier League Darts|2025 edition]].]]
On night one in [[Newcastle upon Tyne|Newcastle]], reigning world champion [[Luke Littler]] faced Premier League debutant [[Gian van Veen]] in a rematch of the [[2026 PDC World Darts Championship|2026 World Championship]] final. Van Veen won the match 6–4 before beating [[2021 Premier League Darts|2021]] champion [[Jonny Clayton]] 6–4 in the semi-finals. ===Night 5 – Night 8===
[[File:2026-03-26 Premier League Darts – Night 8 – Berlin 2026 by Sandro Halank–019.jpg|thumb|[[Josh Rock]] ''(pictured on night eight)'', who hit a [[nine-dart finish]] on night four, earned his first points of the tournament on night eight.]]
On night five in [[Cardiff]], Luke Littler became the fifth different nightly winner in five weeks, rising from seventh to third in the table. After defeating Josh Rock, he produced a [[three-dart average]] of 111.05 and landed a [[Glossary of darts#Maximum check|170 checkout]] to beat Gerwyn Price 6–3 in the semi-finals. He hit another 170 checkout and missed [[Glossary of darts#Double|double]] 15 for a nine-dart finish on his way to winning the final 6–4 against Jonny Clayton.<ref>{{cite web |last=Stafford |first=Ali |date=6 March 2026 |title=Premier League Darts: Luke Littler beats Jonny Clayton in Cardiff final to secure impressive first night win of season |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13515871/premier-league-darts-luke-littler-beats-jonny-clayton-in-cardiff-final-to-secure-impressive-first-night-win-of-season |website=Sky Sports |access-date=6 March 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=6 March 2026 |title=Luke Littler returns to form with Cardiff Premier League victory |url=https://www.rte.ie/sport/darts/2026/0305/1561913-littler-returns-to-form-with-cardiff-premier-league-win/ |website=RTÉ Sport |access-date=6 March 2026 |agency=Press Association}}</ref> On night six in [[Nottingham]], Clayton became the first two-time winner during the season's league stage. Night six marked Humphries' first final of the season, which he reached after beating Littler 6–5 in the semi-final—his first win over Littler since their [[2025 Premier League Darts|2025 Premier League]] final. Suffering with [[gout]] in his ankle during the night's action, Clayton remarked that he "wasn't expecting much", revealing that he had to keep practising as his condition got worse when he sat down.<ref>{{cite web |last=Higham |first=Paul |date=12 March 2026 |title=Clayton overcomes gout to crush Humphries in final |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cze0z3rp91do |website=BBC Sport |access-date=13 March 2026}}</ref>

Littler surpassed Clayton at the top of the table by beating Van Gerwen 6–4 in the night's final, where Littler hit two 170 checkouts.<ref>{{cite web |last=Matthews |first=Callum |date=26 March 2026 |title=Littler takes out 170 twice to beat Van Gerwen |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/c239n020e9zo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=27 March 2026}}</ref>

===Night 13 – Night 16===
[[File:2026-03-26 Premier League Darts – Night 8 – Berlin 2026 by Sandro Halank–055.jpg|thumb|The defending champion [[Luke Humphries]] ''(pictured on night eight)'' secured his place in the play-offs by winning night fifteen, joining [[Luke Littler]], [[Jonny Clayton]], and [[Gerwyn Price]].]]
On night thirteen in [[Aberdeen]], Luke Littler claimed his fifth nightly win by recovering from 5–3 down to defeat Luke Humphries 6–5 in the final. "I'm very happy with myself and now I can go on to break my own record," he said afterwards.<ref name="Night14Sky">{{cite web |date=7 May 2026 |title=Premier League Darts: Luke Littler beats Luke Humphries in Leeds for record-equalling sixth nightly win |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13541253/premier-league-darts-luke-littler-beats-luke-humphries-in-leeds-for-record-equalling-sixth-nightly-win |website=Sky Sports |access-date=7 May 2026}}</ref> Humphries entered the play-off spots in fourth place, behind Price and ahead of Van Gerwen, while Rock's elimination from play-off contention was confirmed with a 6–5 loss to Humphries in the quarter-finals.<ref name="Night14Sky"/><ref>{{cite web |last=Matthews |first=Callum |date=7 May 2026 |title=Littler beats Humphries for record-equalling win |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/c62dz1j0e25o |website=BBC Sport |access-date=7 May 2026}}</ref>

Littler whitewashed Clayton 6–0 in the quarter-finals of night fifteen in [[Birmingham]], guaranteeing that he would finish the league stage at the top of the table for the third consecutive year. ===Finals Night===
The play-offs were played on Finals Night on 28 May, where Luke Littler faced Gerwyn Price and Luke Humphries faced Jonny Clayton in the semi-finals.<ref name="PDCFinal">{{cite web |last=Gorton |first=Josh |date=28 May 2026 |title=Littler denies Humphries in epic final to reclaim Premier League title |url=https://www.pdc.tv/news/2026/may/28/Littler-denies-Humphries-epic-final-reclaim-premier-league-title/ |website=Professional Darts Corporation |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref> Littler, Humphries, and Clayton maintained their perfect records of qualifying for Finals Night; Littler and Humphries debuted in 2024 and contested the 2024 and 2025 finals, while Clayton had qualified for the play-offs in each of his appearances since his debut in 2021.<ref name="PriceClaytonBBC">{{cite web |last=Mitchelmore |first=Ian |date=27 May 2026 |title=Price and Clayton's quest for all-Welsh showdown |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cj0pgy70d4zo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref> Clayton was also the only player to stay in the top four of the table for the duration of the league stage.<ref name="Proud">{{cite web |last=Mitchelmore |first=Ian |date=29 May 2026 |title=Clayton and Price should be proud, says Littler |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cvgz8v2xj92o |website=BBC Sport |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref> Meanwhile, Price was the only member of the lineup who had not won the Premier League before.<ref name="PriceClaytonBBC"/>

====Final====
[[File:2026-03-26 Premier League Darts – Night 8 – Berlin 2026 by Sandro Halank–080.jpg|thumb|left|World number one [[Luke Littler]] ''(pictured on night eight)'' won his second Premier League title, defeating [[Luke Humphries]] 11–10 in the pair's third consecutive final.]]
The final was contested between world number one Littler and world number two Humphries. It was the third consecutive year that the English pair played each other in the Premier League final, after Littler won the title in 2024 and Humphries won the rematch in 2025.<ref>{{cite web |date=28 May 2026 |title=Luke Littler focused on getting past Gerwyn Price before thoughts of Premier League trilogy with Luke Humphries |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13548137/luke-littler-focused-on-getting-past-gerwyn-price-before-thoughts-of-premier-league-trilogy-with-luke-humphries |website=Sky Sports |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref> It was the duo's 36th meeting in all competitions.<ref name="BBCFinal"/> Humphries was looking to become the third player to retain the Premier League title, after [[Phil Taylor (darts player)|Phil Taylor]] and Michael van Gerwen.<ref name="PDCFinal"/>

Humphries opened the final with successive 13-dart legs, but Littler claimed the next three to take a 3–2 lead. After winning the proceeding leg, Humphries converted three consecutive ton-plus finishes—134, 112, and 121—as he went 6–3 in front, entering the interval with a 6–4 lead. Littler responded to his deficit by levelling the match at 7–7, winning four of the last five legs. The compatriots remained equal at 9–9, with a 10-dart break of throw from Littler giving him the lead and the chance to win the match. However, he missed a match dart and Humphries returned with a 68 checkout to send the match to a deciding leg. Attempting to hold throw, Humphries was able to leave a one-dart finish, but Littler hit double 20 with his last dart in hand to complete an 11–10 victory.<ref name="PDCFinal"/><ref name="SkyFinal"/> Littler finished the final with a three-dart average of 111.67 and 12 [[Glossary of darts#Maximum|maximums]], while Humphries averaged 105.60.<ref name="BBCFinal"/> The 11–10 result meant that for the first time in Premier League history, all 59 possible legs were played on Finals Night.<ref name="Proud"/> The match, touted as one of the best finals in the competition's history,<ref name="SkyFinal"/><ref name="BBCFinal"/><ref>{{cite web |last=Chambers |first=Tom |date=29 May 2026 |title=Luke Littler triumphs in Premier League trilogy clash, but tears show human side to darts' winning machine |url=https://www.espn.co.uk/darts/story/_/id/48902152/luke-littler-triumphs-pdc-premier-league-darts-trilogy-luke-humphries-gerwyn-price-jonny-clayton-o2-arena |website=ESPN |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref> was also the third Premier League final to go to a deciding leg, after [[2017 Premier League Darts|2017]] and [[2022 Premier League Darts|2022]], and the first decider where the loser did not miss darts for the title.<ref name="Nicho">{{cite web |last=Nicholson |first=Paul |author-link=Paul Nicholson (darts player) |date=29 May 2026 |title=The greatest Premier League Finals Night of all-time: Paul Nicholson reflects on Luke Littler's triumph over Luke Humphries at the O2 |url=https://www.sportinglife.com/darts/news/the-greatest-premier-league-finals-night-of-all-time-paul-nicholson-reflects-on-luke-littlers-triumph-over-luke-humphries-at-the-o2/232386 |website=Sporting Life |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref>

Littler won his second Premier League title, his fourth major title of 2026 after triumphs at the World Championship, the [[2026 PDC World Masters|World Masters]], and the [[2026 UK Open|UK Open]].<ref name="BBCFinal"/> The win meant that Littler was the reigning champion of eight different major tournaments.<ref name="SkyFinal"/> He also became the fourth player to win multiple Premier League titles, after Taylor, Van Gerwen and [[Gary Anderson (darts player)|Gary Anderson]].<ref name="PDCFinal"/> Factoring in bonuses for nightly wins, Littler received £410,000 in total for winning the tournament, while Humphries received £180,000 for his runner-up finish.<ref>{{cite web |last=Chambers |first=Tom |date=29 May 2026 |title=What is the Premier League Darts 2026 prize money? All-time stats |url=https://www.espn.co.uk/darts/story/_/id/47620095/what-pdc-premier-league-darts-2026-prize-money-all-stats-luke-littler-nine-darter |website=ESPN |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref> Littler broke down in tears during his post-match interview as he talked about the reactions he had received from the crowd during his Premier League campaign. He revealed that he wanted to quit the Premier League midway through the season, stating: "After Brighton and the incident in Manchester, I was sat at home saying to Faith [his partner] 'I don't want to do it any more, just the crowd every week'. I said to her 'I'm down bad'."<ref name="Emotional">{{cite web |date=29 May 2026 |title='I didn't want to do it anymore': Emotional Luke Littler reveals he nearly quit Premier League |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/12288/13548910/luke-littler-breaks-down-in-tears-after-epic-premier-league-triumph-i-picked-myself-up-after-tough-times |website=Sky Sports |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref> He addressed his comments in a later press conference, saying he was "not asking for sympathy" and that the biggest outcome of the night was the trophy.<ref>{{cite web |last=Coates |first=Charlotte |date=29 May 2026 |title=Emotional Littler underlines greatness after almost quitting Premier League |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/darts/articles/cg7pmry8gkgo |website=BBC Sport |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref> "I'm not sure of the stats, but it was probably one of the best finals the Premier League has seen," said Humphries in defeat, admitting that he was "gutted" that Littler did not miss his final dart at double 20.<ref name="PDCFinal"/> In light of Littler's post-match reaction, he commented on the struggles of competing in the Premier League, stating: "It is emotional, you have seen it from Luke [Littler] there. You are playing in big finals and it shows how much it means to us as players ... You are on the road for 16 or 17 weeks and I don't get the chance to take my family with me. You want that trophy in your hands."<ref name="Emotional"/> Commentator and pundit [[Wayne Mardle]] expressed that Littler's comments were a "step forward" for him. He added: "We know how strong Littler is mentally but when you are feeling like you're public enemy number one, you can feel like you don't want to turn up. But he did turn up and this is the by-product of turning up."<ref>{{cite web |last=Mardle |first=Wayne |author-link=Wayne Mardle |date=29 May 2026 |title=Luke Littler: Wayne Mardle hails Premier League champion for opening up about struggles, says he has 'no weaknesses' |url=https://www.skysports.com/darts/news/31667/13548918/luke-littler-wayne-mardle-hails-premier-league-champion-for-opening-up-about-struggles-says-he-has-no-weaknesses |website=Sky Sports |access-date=29 May 2026}}</ref>
