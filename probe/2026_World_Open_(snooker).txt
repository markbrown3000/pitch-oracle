The '''2026 World Open''' (officially the '''2026 Likang Roasted Eel – Junma Cup World Open''') was a professional [[snooker]] tournament that took place from 16 to 22 March 2026 at the Yushan Sport Centre in [[Yushan County|Yushan]], China. Qualifiers took place from 10 to 12 February at the [[Barnsley Metrodome]] in [[Barnsley]], England. The 11th edition of the tournament since it was rebranded as the [[World Open (snooker)|World Open]] in 2010, it was the 16th [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season]], following the [[2026 Welsh Open (snooker)|2026 Welsh Open]] and preceding the [[2026 Tour Championship]]. The tournament was broadcast by [[TNT Sports]] and [[Discovery+]] in the United Kingdom and Ireland, by [[Eurosport]] in mainland Europe, by local channels in Asia and Africa, and by [[WST Play]] in all other territories. The winner received £175,000 from a total prize fund of £825,000.

[[John Higgins]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Joe O'Connor (snooker player)|Joe O'Connor]] 10–6 in the [[2025 World Open (snooker)|2025 final]]. He played the record-extending 2,000th match of his professional career in the last 64 but lost 4–5 to [[Stan Moody]]. After beating the world number one [[Judd Trump]] 6–4 in the semi-finals, [[Thepchaiya Un-Nooh]] defeated [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] 10–7 in the final to win his first World Open title and the second ranking title of his career. Un-Nooh became the second Thai player, after [[James Wattana]], to win a full-format ranking tournament.

The tournament produced 119 [[Century break|century breaks]], 32 at the qualifiers in Barnsley and 87 at the main stage in Yushan. O'Sullivan made the highest break in professional snooker history, a {{Cuegloss|16-red clearance}} of 153, during his quarter-final victory over [[Ryan Day (snooker player)|Ryan Day]]. It was the second [[Maximum break#Breaks exceeding 147|break exceeding 147]] in professional competition, following the 148 made by [[Jamie Burnett]] in the qualifying rounds of the [[2004 UK Championship]]. Un-Nooh compiled a [[maximum break]], the seventh of his professional career, in the penultimate frame of the final. It was the record-extending 23rd maximum of the season. 

== Overview ==
The [[World Open (snooker)|World Open]] originated as the Professional Players Tournament, of which [[Ray Reardon]] won the inaugural [[1982 Professional Players Tournament|1982 edition]], defeating [[Jimmy White]] 10–5 in the final.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Professional Players Tournament 1982|url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2071 |access-date=11 February 2026 |website=snooker.org |language=en |archive-date=14 December 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241214203240/https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?event=2071 |url-status=live }}</ref> Staged mostly as the Grand Prix from 1984 to 2009 (except 2001 to 2003, when it was held as the LG Cup), the tournament was played in England until 2004, after which it moved to Scotland. It was rebranded as the World Open in 2010.<ref>{{cite web |last=Turner |first=Chris |title=Brief History of the World Open |url=http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/worldopen.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160418005319/http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/worldopen.html |archive-date=18 April 2016 |access-date=14 March 2024 |website=Chris Turner's Snooker Archive}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Turner |first=Chris |title=A brief history of the Grand Prix and LG Cup (Formerly the Professional Players Tournament) |url=http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/GP.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150402102832/http://www.cajt.pwp.blueyonder.co.uk/GP.html |archive-date=2 April 2015 |website=Chris Turner's Snooker Archive}}</ref> The tournament was not held in 2011, and the following year it moved to [[Haikou]], China. The 2015 edition was cancelled after the contract with the promoter was not renewed,<ref>{{Cite web |date=7 November 2014 |title=World Open removed from calendar |url=http://www.worldsnooker.com/page/NewsArticles/0,,13165~4281931,00.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141110200349/http://www.worldsnooker.com/page/NewsArticles/0,,13165~4281931,00.html |archive-date=10 November 2014 |access-date=9 February 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]]}}</ref> and in 2016 the tournament moved to [[Yushan County|Yushan]]. It was not held from 2020 to 2023 due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]] but returned to the calendar for the [[2024 World Open (snooker)|2024 edition]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=18 August 2023 |title=Snooker's resurgence in China continues with Yushan to stage World Open |url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2023/august/18/snookers-resurgence-in-china-continues-with-yushan-to-stage-world-open/ |access-date=11 February 2026 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en |archive-date=26 April 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250426045129/https://www.wst.tv/news/2023/august/18/snookers-resurgence-in-china-continues-with-yushan-to-stage-world-open/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

The 2026 edition of the tournament—its 11th staging since it became the World Open in 2010—took place from 16 to 22 March at the Yushan Sport Centre in Yushan, China.<ref>{{cite web |title=Tournaments 2025–26 |url=https://www.wst.tv/tournaments |access-date=20 July 2025 |website=[[World Snooker Tour]] |language=en |archive-date=5 December 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251205154034/https://www.wst.tv/tournaments/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Qualifiers took place from 10 to 12 February at the [[Barnsley Metrodome]] in [[Barnsley]], England.<ref name="s.o_Q"/> It was the 16th [[List of snooker ranking tournaments|ranking event]] of the [[2025–26 snooker season]], following the [[2026 Welsh Open (snooker)|2026 Welsh Open]] and preceding the [[2026 Tour Championship]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Calendar 2025/2026 |url=https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?template=2 |access-date=20 July 2025 |website=snooker.org |language=en |archive-date=31 October 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231031171438/https://www.snooker.org/res/index.asp?template=2 |url-status=live }}</ref> [[John Higgins]] was the defending champion, having defeated [[Joe O'Connor (snooker player)|Joe O'Connor]] 10–6 in the [[2025 World Open (snooker)|2025 final]].<ref>{{cite web |date=1 March 2025 |title=Higgins wins World Open for first title in four years|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c62kzn7ddpyo|access-date=17 April 2026|website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260417150305/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c62kzn7ddpyo|archive-date=17 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Format ===
All matches were played as the best of 9 {{cuegloss|frames}} up to and including the quarter-finals. The semi-finals were the best of 11 frames and the final was a best-of-19-frame match played over two {{cuegloss|sessions}}.<ref name="s.o_M"/>

[[Neil Robertson]] withdrew before the qualifiers in Barnsley.<ref name="s.o_Q" /> [[Chris Totten]] withdrew before the held-over qualifiers in Yushan. [[Mark Selby]], [[Barry Hawkins]], [[Stephen Maguire]], [[David Gilbert (snooker player)|David Gilbert]], [[Chris Wakelin]], [[Jak Jones]], Umut Dikme, and [[Ishpreet Singh Chadha]] all won their qualifying matches but withdrew prior to the main stage.<ref name="WST_withdrawals" />

The last three years have been awful in terms of confidence. I'm trying to work on that now and see if I can get back to delivering the [[cue stick|cue]] freely." Higgins had the first chance in the decider but scored just 15 {{cuegloss|points}}, and Moody secured the frame and match with a break of 65. ==== Quarter-finals ====
[[File:Ronnie O'Sullivan and Marcel Eckardt 2026 (3).jpg|thumb|[[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] ''(pictured in 2026)'' made the highest {{cuegloss|break}} in professional snooker history, a {{Cuegloss|16-red clearance}} of 153.]]
In the first frame of his quarter-final match against Day, O'Sullivan made the highest break in professional snooker history, a {{Cuegloss|16-red clearance}} of 153. Awarded a {{Cuegloss|free ball}} after Day failed to escape from a {{Cuegloss|snooker}}, O'Sullivan potted the {{Cuegloss|green ball|green}} as his nominated free ball, followed by the black. He went on to pot all 15 reds, 13 further blacks, two {{Cuegloss|pink ball|pinks}}, and all the {{Cuegloss|colours}}. It was only the second time that a [[Maximum break#Breaks exceeding 147|break exceeding 147]] had been made in professional competition, following the 148 by [[Jamie Burnett]] in the qualifying rounds of the [[2004 UK Championship]]. O'Sullivan made additional breaks of 62, 110, 103, and 95 as he completed a whitewash victory. Day scored only 38 points in the match. "It was a great buzz," O'Sullivan said afterwards. ==== Semi-finals ====

==== Final ====
[[File:Thepchaiya Un-Nooh.jpg|thumb|[[Thepchaiya Un-Nooh]] ''(pictured in 2025)'' defeated [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]] 10–7 in the final to win his second ranking title. Un-Nooh made a [[maximum break]], the seventh of his career, in the penultimate {{cuegloss|frame}}.]]

The final took place on 22 March as the best of 19 frames, played over two {{cuegloss|sessions}}, between the 11th seed O'Sullivan and the 41st seed Un-Nooh. O'Sullivan played in his 66th ranking final, while Un-Nooh contested his third. Un-Nooh had previously been runner-up at the [[2019 World Open (snooker)|2019 edition]] of the tournament, when he lost 5–10 to Trump.<ref>{{Cite web|date=21 March 2026|title=Un-Nooh into final after dramatic finale|url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/21/un-nooh-into-final-after-dramatic-finale/|access-date=21 March 2026|website=[[World Snooker Tour]]|language=en|archive-date=23 March 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260323064328/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/21/un-nooh-into-final-after-dramatic-finale/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|date=21 March 2026|title=Rocket set for 66th ranking final|url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/21/rocket-set-for-66th-ranking-final-1/|access-date=21 March 2026|website=[[World Snooker Tour]]|language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260417154303/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/21/rocket-set-for-66th-ranking-final-1/|archive-date=17 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

In the first session, O'Sullivan made breaks including 73 and 124 as he won the first four frames, but Un-Nooh responded with breaks including 83 and 82 as he took the next five, ending the session 5–4 ahead.<ref>{{Cite web|date=22 March 2026|title=Un-Nooh fights back to lead O'Sullivan|url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/22/un-nooh-fights-back-to-lead-o-sullivan-1/|access-date=22 March 2026|website=[[World Snooker Tour]]|language=en|archive-date=22 March 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260322135857/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/22/un-nooh-fights-back-to-lead-o-sullivan-1/|url-status=live}}</ref> When play resumed for the second session, Un-Nooh made a clearance of 50 to win frame 10 on the last black, but O'Sullivan produced three consecutive century breaks of 114, 102, and 136 as he moved into a 7–6 lead at the mid-session interval. Un-Nooh made breaks of 77 and 132 as he regained the lead at 8–7. In frame 16, Un-Nooh compiled a [[maximum break]], his seventh in professional competition and his third of the season. It was the 240th official maximum break in snooker history and the record-extending 23rd maximum of the season. Un-Nooh completed a 10–7 victory with a third consecutive {{Cuegloss|total clearance}} of 131, meaning that the last seven frames of the final had produced six century breaks. Un-Nooh won his first [[World Open (snooker)|World Open]] title and the second ranking title of his career, following his previous ranking victory at the [[2019 Snooker Shoot Out]]. He became the second Thai player, after [[James Wattana]] at the [[1995 Thailand Open]], to win a full-format ranking tournament. The £175,000 winner's prize, the largest of his career, secured him a place in the Tour Championship and advanced him from 39th to 22nd in the [[snooker world rankings]].<ref name="WST_Un-Nooh_max">{{Cite web|date=22 March 2026|title=Un-Nooh makes maximum in classic final victory|url=https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/22/un-nooh-makes-maximum-in-classic-final-victory/|access-date=22 March 2026|website=[[World Snooker Tour]]|language=en|archive-date=23 March 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260323064328/https://www.wst.tv/news/2026/march/22/un-nooh-makes-maximum-in-classic-final-victory/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Max 240">{{cite news |title=Un-Nooh hits 147 to claim World Open title against Rocket |url=https://www.rte.ie/sport/snooker/2026/0322/1564645-un-nooh-hits-147-to-claim-world-open-title-against-rocket/ |work=[[RTÉ]] |date=22 March 2026 |access-date=22 March 2026 |archive-date=22 March 2026 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260322142545/https://www.rte.ie/sport/snooker/2026/0322/1564645-un-nooh-hits-147-to-claim-world-open-title-against-rocket/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Nelson |first=Craig |date=22 March 2026 |title=Un-Nooh makes 147 in World Open win over O'Sullivan |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c1mk9170xk5o |access-date=17 April 2026 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260417152130/https://www.bbc.com/sport/snooker/articles/c1mk9170xk5o|archive-date=17 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="TS_Day_06" />

"This has always been my dream, to lift the title against Ronnie O'Sullivan in the final," Un-Nooh said afterwards. "For the rest of my life, this is something I'm not going to forget." == Qualifying ==
Qualifiers were held from 10 to 12 February 2026 at the [[Barnsley Metrodome]] in [[Barnsley]], England. Qualifying matches involving the defending champion [[John Higgins]], the reigning World Champion [[Zhao Xintong]], the world number one [[Judd Trump]], the seven-time World Champion [[Ronnie O'Sullivan]], the next two highest-ranked Chinese players ([[Xiao Guodong]] and [[Zhang Anda]]), and four Chinese [[Wild card (sports)|wildcards]] (Han Fuyuan, Xu Jiarui, [[Wang Xinbo (snooker player)|Wang Xinbo]], and [[Liu Yang (snooker player)|Liu Yang]]) were held over to be played at the main venue in [[Yushan County|Yushan]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Day |first=Michael |date=9 February 2026 |title=2026 World Open qualifiers explained: Who is playing and what is the format? |url=https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/world-open/2026-world-open-snooker-qualifiers-explained-who-is-playing-in-barnsley-and-what-is-the-format-5507892 |access-date=10 February 2026 |website=Totally Snookered |language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260417154328/https://www.totallysnookered.com/sport/snooker/tournaments/world-open/2026-world-open-snooker-qualifiers-explained-who-is-playing-in-barnsley-and-what-is-the--5507892|archive-date=17 April 2026|url-status=live}}</ref> Numbers in parentheses after the players' names denote the players' [[Seeding (sports)|seeding]], and players in bold denote match winners.
